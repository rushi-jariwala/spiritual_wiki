The moment that sealed my understanding of the spirit world was an uncomfortable one. During one of my early out-of-body experiments, I found myself amused to discover I could fly through a ceiling fan to the floor above. I did — and decided to return the same way. On the way back, I was hanging halfway between the two floors, my ankles at the level of the spinning blades, suspended there for what felt like a few seconds while I admired the strange upended view. Then came a distinct cracking sound. Then the pain.

The next morning, my ankle hurt in precisely the same spot. I limped for around fifteen days. My belief is that the motion of the fan blades had generated a rotating energy field, and it was that field — not the solid blades themselves — that injured the ankle of my spirit body.

**The Physics of the Other Side**

This single incident settled, for me, one of the most counterintuitive facts about the spirit world: solid matter offers no resistance, but energy does. Walls, ceilings, floors — they were almost as if made of the thinnest paper. The fan blades, perfectly solid, passed through me without friction. It was the invisible field those blades generated by their rotation that stopped me cold.

Light works differently there too. Even at night, the outside world carries a post-dusk quality — interiors a few shades darker still, as if reality has been turned down a fraction. And the strangest inversion of all: spirits seen from within the spirit body appear as solid as any physical person. What looks ethereal to physical eyes becomes entirely tangible when it is your spirit looking at theirs. The spirit world is not a shadow version of this one. It is equally real. Only the vantage point shifts.

What this establishes is foundational: you have two bodies. Not metaphorically. The *sukshma sharir* — the spirit body — is a real, physical entity, one-tenth the density of smoke, slightly luminous from within, fine enough to pass through matter, and weightless enough to fly. It has been yours through every lifetime. It will outlast the present physical form by five to ten times. And under the right conditions, it travels while you sleep.

**When the Guru Departs**

I have described my own early experiments with the amusement of someone who knows he is still in training. Gurudev was something else entirely. He travelled astrally at least three times a week, for several hours at a stretch — meetings with spiritual masters across realms and across alliances formed in previous lifetimes, returning by 3:30 am.

The earliest documented witness account comes from Sitaram Taki ji, one of the first disciples, who was with Gurudev in Haridwar sometime in the 1970s. One night Gurudev asked him to press his feet and told him to hold on and accompany him on an astral journey when he left his body. He wrapped himself in a sheet and began his paath. What followed put the fear of God in Sitaram ji. He watched

> *"Gurudev wrapped in a sheet emerge from his body while an identical physical form also wrapped in a sheet remained seated on the reclining chair."*

The astral form was indistinguishable from the physical one. Both sheet-wrapped. Both Gurudev. Sitaram ji was so undone by what he saw that he wet his pants — a detail I have always appreciated for its honesty. He remained frozen until the astral form returned to the room and merged with the seated body.

**The Fuel Is Seva**

The question this raises is whether astral travel belongs only to someone of Gurudev's stature. It does not. *Guru Sutra* records a quiet incident that answers this directly.

A disciple named Nakul had been associated with the sthan for several years, doing considerable seva while making little visible impression. One morning before dawn, his wife Vinti woke to see his spirit body rise from the bed, cross the room, and pass through the window. She was not dreaming — she watched it leave and then looked at his sleeping body beside her, undisturbed. Nakul himself did not know until she told him.

*"Out-of-body experiences are one of the few ways in which people can consciously assess their spiritual progress."* Nakul's years of service at the sthan had built, quietly and invisibly, the spiritual fuel for exactly this. He may not yet have been able to do it at will — but something in him already knew the way.

*(→ [[stories/nakul-astral-travel-vinti|Vinti Witnesses Nakul's Astral Travel]])*

Developing *gati* — the capacity for superfast travel, the spirit body moving like a rocket through realms — requires the guru's grace. There is an adage that says it plainly: *"Guru bin gati nahi."* Without the guru, speed in the spirit realm does not come easily. But the ground beneath gati — the basic ability to move in the spirit body at all — that accumulates through service, through the unwitnessed work that leaves no impression on anyone except the one doing it.

Two of you travel. Only one needs a door.
