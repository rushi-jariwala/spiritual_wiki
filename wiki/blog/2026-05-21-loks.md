One of the most unexpectedly intimate moments I have experienced on this path came not from a vision of light or a transmission of energy, but from a glimpse of my grandmother.

I was in a vision state and found myself in what I now understand to be a darker lok — one of the lower levels of existence where beings dwell between incarnations. She was there, sitting among others. She saw me. She recognised me. And she deliberately looked away.

*(→ [[stories/hingori-lok-visits|Hingori's Visits to the Loks]])*

The grandmother of my childhood had been the most loving one could hope for. In that lok, I was just a number. The warmth, the recognition, the relationship — all dissolved. The others around her all wore togas of a dull green colour. She wore the same. Whatever she carried into that level of existence, it was not the story of us.

That single moment taught me more about the structure of existence than almost any scripture I have read since. The loks are not metaphor. They are as real as this room.

**The Building**

To understand what that scene revealed, you have to grasp the architecture first. Imagine the Milky Way as a fourteen-storey building. Each floor is a lok — a level of existence corresponding to a particular range of consciousness. Earth is the seventh floor: the exact middle. Above us, seven loks of increasing brightness and refinement. Below us, seven darker tals. *Bhu lok* — our world — is the only floor through which beings from every other level must pass. It is, uniquely, the karma lok: the only place in the entire building where movement in either direction remains possible.

That is both the gift and the burden of a human birth.

*Aatma Sutra* is precise about what the lower tals hold. They are not places of gothic suffering — *"merely darker, in light, concept and consciousness."* But the residents are there for a reason: the guna mix of their thoughts and actions across successive lifetimes has qualified them for exactly that floor, nothing higher. The grandmother I saw was not being punished. She was simply in a level that matched what she had carried.

**The Game Has Rules**

The question is always which direction you are moving, and the mechanics are not complicated. There is an old Indian game that captures them exactly. Snakes and Ladders. Every birth on Earth is a roll of the dice; every action is a choice between a ladder and a snake. The ladders require effort: selfless service, refinement of the guna mix, the systematic lightening of samskar debts. The snakes operate by themselves, without effort, without announcement.

Gurudev said it in four words: *"Tapo raj, rajo narak."* After sustained tapasya, one is typically born into a life of power and abundance. But if that abundance is then misused — if the palace becomes the destination rather than the instrument — the trajectory reverses and one slides back toward lower realms.

Climbing is not done by climbing. You cannot muscle your way up to higher loks. The correct description in *Aatma Sutra* is "spiritual ballooning" — you lighten the load of karmic debts and gross samskars until the being simply floats upward. Weight keeps you where you are. Releasing it is what moves you.

> *"Climbing the ladder needs effort and balance, sliding down the snake is gravitational."*
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.VIII, p.88

**The Yogi Who Chose His Parents**

I saw evidence of this structure operating from the inside many years ago, at a camp in Bathri near Dalhousie in Himachal Pradesh. I was staying at a guesthouse that turned out to be haunted — if that is the right word — by the spirit of a yogi who had been waiting there for decades. The watchman had seen him in dreams repeatedly: a yogi-like figure, waiting for a saint to arrive.

When Gurudev arrived, the yogi moved quickly. He appeared in the dream of a couple staying with us — the Tanejas — and made his request directly to Mrs Taneja: grant me birth. He assured her that most of their current problems would be resolved within her pregnancy. She refused at first; she felt too old. Gurudev overruled her gently: she had no choice.

A few months into the pregnancy, their financial and family difficulties dissolved. The child arrived healthy. Gurudev told me later that he had, weeks earlier, tried to introduce the same yogi spirit to another couple — but that couple had quarrelled at the location, and the yogi had rejected them outright.

*(→ [[stories/yogi-spirit-bathri|The Yogi Spirit of Bathri]])*

This is the lok system working from within: a powerful spirit, carrying the accumulated sanchit karma of a devoted yogi's lifetime, consciously navigating his re-entry into the karma lok. He evaluated, negotiated, and selected. He even resolved his future parents' difficulties from within the womb — demonstrating that genuine karmic credit persists between lives and can be actively deployed. The spirit knew exactly what floor it needed to reach, and it chose its ladder carefully.

**The Floor That Is Not the Ceiling**

The system also warns against settling at any floor — including the higher ones. Thirty-five years ago, Gurudev opened my third eye. I began to have visions of the future and to hear an inner voice. When I reported these experiences to him, he placed his hand on my head and slowly ran it down my spine, closing what he had opened.

*It felt cruel at the time.* Why would he take this back?

Later I understood. The third eye corresponds to the Tapa Lok — the thirteenth floor. Had I been allowed to remain absorbed in its capabilities, telling people their futures, living in that particular amazement, I would have stagnated there. The floor would have become a ceiling. Gurudev was not protecting my present experience. He was protecting the eventual trajectory.

*(→ [[stories/hingori-third-eye-opened-closed|Gurudev Opens and Closes Hingori's Third Eye]])*

He did this for most of his disciples. Whatever floor they touched, he ensured they did not mistake it for their permanent address.

**The Most Significant Line**

All of this — the building, the game, the yogi's deliberate selection, Gurudev's protective interruption — converges on a single instruction that Gurudev offered as one of his one-line teachings: *Apni mauth ko saath leker phirna.* Keep death always with you. Not as morbidity, but as clarification. Knowing that this incarnation is a temporary posting in the karma lok is the most efficient sharpener I have found. You are standing on the seventh floor. You have access to every direction. The infrastructure for change exists here, and nowhere else in the building.

> *"Do not try to spend a pleasurable life, but instead a more invested one."*
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.VIII, p.94

The grandmother in the green toga is not a warning. She is an orientation marker. She tells you exactly where the floor is and where the stairs begin.
