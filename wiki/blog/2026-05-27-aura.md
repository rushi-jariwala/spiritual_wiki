It was around four in the morning. My spirit had left my body — one of those journeys that were becoming, by then, less surprising and more instructive. I floated into the room where my wife lay sleeping on the floor, and my wacky sense of humour, apparently undiminished by the absence of a physical body, compelled me to try to startle her. Every time I attempted to land on her, I bounced right back. It was as if I had leaped onto a spring. She didn't stir. She didn't move an inch. What held me off was a blue conical light extending at least a foot from her body in every direction — a shield that my spirit body simply could not penetrate.

I floated back to my own body considerably more educated than when I had left it.

**What We Cannot See**

That blue field has a name, and every one of us carries one. The aura is the bioelectric energy field that surrounds all life — the visible radiation of *shakti*, the spiritual energy accumulated by the spirit body over years, and over lifetimes. Most of us cannot see it directly. We navigate our entire lives unaware that we are walking through a world of overlapping fields, each one broadcasting the inner state of the person it belongs to.

> "The aura acts like a protective shield to the body. Most external diseases we contract, first infect the aura and then the body."
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.IV, p.40

In the early 1980s, a Soviet scientist named Semyon Kirilian developed a method of photographing these fields. An Indo-Soviet study conducted in Rishikesh photographed sixty thousand people. The results were precise: corporate executives and businessmen registered the shallowest auras — barely half an inch. Farmers and gardeners did better. Yogis and philanthropists better still. Saints and serious spiritual practitioners reached twelve inches and beyond. The numbers mapped, without any ideological agenda, exactly what the tradition had always said: the inner life is a measurable reality.

**The Currency That Crosses the Border**

Understanding aura as a shield is useful. Understanding it as currency changes the way one lives.

The spirit body — which outlasts the physical by five to ten times its duration, wandering between incarnations sometimes for centuries — has no bank account, no portfolio, no savings. What it carries forward is *shakti*. This is the only wealth that survives death, the only asset that transfers intact across the border.

> "Your aura potency, in a manner of speaking, is your cosmic currency and you need a lot of it in order to be rich. It is one of the only assets that you can carry forward when you die, and to your future incarnations. It is also the armour that protects you from the invasion of other energies, including illnesses and spiritual attacks."
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.X, p.126

Wealth across lifetimes, then, is measured not in what you accumulated but in what your aura held at the moment of departure. The irony is perfect: the assets that most of us spend our lives accumulating — property, reputation, capital — dissolve at death. The one balance sheet that follows us home is the one very few of us think to tend.

**A Lesson in Ego's Price**

To understand this more concretely, I recall what happened with Nandlal, a devoted *sevadaar* at the *sthan* for several years. He was struggling to rent out one of his shops — something kept going wrong at the last moment. During meditation, I requested Gurudev to help. What arrived in that meditation was extraordinary: *Gurudev threw a ball of energy — dynamic, translucent white, almost a foot in diameter, expanding and contracting like a small imploding cloud.* I relayed it to Nandlal. The negative energies in the shop cleared. Within days, Nandlal discovered he could heal strangers — touching a man on a train whose pain vanished.

*(→ [[stories/nandlal-shakti-ball|Nandlal's Shakti Ball — Healing and the Price of Ego]])*

Then he made one mistake. Acting on his own — without offering the act to a higher power — he egoistically splashed energised water into the eyes of a patient who turned out to be far more spiritually advanced than he was. Energy flows to the stronger field. It drained out of Nandlal's hand. For six months his right hand was weaker than the other, the imbalance persisting as a precise and unambiguous invoice for one egoistic moment.

Doer-ship in healing is the fastest way to become the patient.

**What Builds It, What Drains It**

Every vritti — every thought, perception, imagining, memory — depletes the aura. Anger burns through it quickly; sustained hatred bleeds it slowly. Sexual interaction, excessive talking, handshakes with someone whose field is stronger — all register as withdrawals. This is why the tradition advises *namaste* over handshakes, silence over gossip, and the mantra over rumination.

What builds it is simpler than people expect: seva, greening places, mantra practice, and — surprisingly — honest self-observation. Touching the feet of a saint or drinking water sipped by one is not superstition; it is a transfer of energy from a richer field to a poorer one, operating by the same logic as any gradient.

The principle extends into something even more practical. When you think of someone — admire them, criticise them, dwell on them — a kind of wireless connection forms and qualities begin to flow. Criticise someone consistently and you gradually absorb the very qualities you are criticising. Admire someone and you absorb those qualities instead. The corollary from yogic practice: to acquire a specific quality, meditate on an animal or a figure that embodies it. What you meditate upon, that you become. Choose prudently.

**The Only Inheritance**

In the end, the question is simple: what are you actually earning? The hours we spend managing, worrying, comparing, and accumulating deploy our energy against a balance sheet that zeroes out at death. The one balance sheet that compounds indefinitely — across this life, across the interval between lives, across whatever comes next — is the aura.

Tend it as carefully as you tend everything else.
