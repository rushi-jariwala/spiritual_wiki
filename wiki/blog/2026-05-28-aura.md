One night, during a period when I was beginning to learn the terrain of the spirit body, I found myself out of my physical form at around 4 am. My wife lay sleeping on the floor nearby. In what I can only call a wacky impulse — I admit to the wackiness freely — I decided to jump on her to give her a fright. *Every time I tried, I bounced right back.* She was enveloped by a blue conical light, extending at least a foot off her body. She did not move an inch, despite my best efforts. That was the moment I understood that the aura is not a metaphor. It is a shield.

*(→ [[stories/hingori-wife-aura-protection|Hingori's Wife's Aura — The Blue Conical Light]])*

**What you are carrying without knowing it**

Every living being radiates a field of bioelectric energy — the external expression of the *shakti* accumulated by the spirit body. This is the aura. Most people have no idea it exists; less than ten percent of the world's nine billion people are even aware of it, and perhaps one percent know enough to work with it. The field is real and measurable: a Soviet scientist named Semyon Kirilian photographed the auras of 60,000 people in an Indo-Soviet experiment at Rishikesh in the early 1980s. Corporate executives and traders showed the shallowest fields — barely half an inch. Farmers and gardeners showed more. Yogis and serious practitioners showed fields extending up to twelve inches.

The depth of your aura is, in the most literal sense, the measure of your spiritual wealth.

**The cosmic currency**

This is where most people lose the thread entirely. We accumulate property, credentials, money, reputation — none of it crosses the threshold at death. The aura does. *Aatma Sutra* is precise: *"Your aura potency, in a manner of speaking, is your cosmic currency and you need a lot of it in order to be rich. It is one of the only assets that you can carry forward when you die, and to your future incarnations. It is also the armour that protects you from the invasion of other energies, including illnesses and spiritual attacks."* *(Ch.X, p.126)*

Between incarnations, the spirit body may exist for hundreds of years in the astral realms — unprotected, without physical form, without the ordinary anchors of life. During that entire stretch it runs on *shakti*. The physical lifetime is not an end in itself; it is an earning window. The question is how you use it.

*(→ [[concepts/sukshma-sharir]])*

**What depletes it — and one principle to remember**

Aura leaks constantly. Anger, excessive talking, emotional attachment, vigorous exercise, even a handshake with someone whose field is stronger than yours — each drains the account. But there is a subtler mechanism I find more instructive than all of these. *"When you think of someone or something, a remote bluetooth sort of connection is established. When you criticise someone, even unwittingly, you magnetise a fraction of the qualities of the person you criticised."* *(Aatma Sutra, Ch.X, p.125)* Every sustained thought is an open channel. Criticism does not punish the target — it gradually instils those very qualities in you. Praise works by the same logic. The reverse corollary is worth sitting with: what you meditate upon, that you become. Choose prudently.

This reframes every conversation you have with yourself about others.

**Two lessons from one disciple**

I recall a *sevadaar* named Nandlal who had been struggling to finalise the lease on one of his shops — something always went wrong just before the papers were signed. Sitting in meditation, I requested Gurudev to transmit power to help him. In that meditation I saw Gurudev throw a ball of energy: *"dynamic, translucent white, almost a foot in diameter and felt alive. It kept expanding and contracting like a small imploding cloud."* I passed it to Nandlal. His problem cleared. Days later, he was on a train and touched a stranger who was in pain — and the pain vanished. He had become a healer without knowing it.

Then came the second lesson. On a separate occasion, Nandlal egoistically splashed energised water into a patient's eyes. The patient was spiritually more advanced than he was, and because Nandlal had acted in his own name rather than as a channel for the sthan's power, the energy transferred in the wrong direction. For six months his right hand suffered from an imbalance of aura. The principle is precise: healing without doer-ship protects the healer. Ego turns you from a conduit into a casualty.

**Energy earned, not stolen**

In an earlier period of spiritual adventurism, I once attempted to absorb power from a temple at Renuka that belonged to one of Gurudev's disciples. While praying at the altar, I felt the power turn on me. A heaviness settled on my head that I could not clear. I walked to the lake. Standing with my feet on the ground helped a little — the earth drew out some of it — but the attack persisted. In desperation I prayed to Devi Renuka herself, the mother of sage Parshuram, at whose shrine I had just helped myself uninvited. *"What happened next was unbelievable. It was like watching a television screen on my forehead."* She appeared — white-robed, ornamented, crowned, and luminous. She touched my head, and the heaviness lifted instantly. When I returned to Gurudev's camp and sat quietly at the back like a chastened child, he walked over and confirmed what I already suspected: Parshuramji had punished me for the error.

I have kept a bottle of Renuka Lake water in my place of worship ever since. *I learned the golden rule that energy was better earned than stolen.*

*(→ [[stories/hingori-renuka-temple-attack|Hingori at Renuka — Attacked by the Son, Saved by the Mother]])*

The aura is armour, currency, and ledger in one. Build it through service, practice, and the careful stewardship of your attention. Protect it as you would your most irreplaceable possession. And never mistake proximity to power for the right to take it.
