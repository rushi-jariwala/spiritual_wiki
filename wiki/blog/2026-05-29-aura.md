It was around four in the morning. I was out of my body — a state I had learned to enter over years of practice and Gurudev's patient guidance — when I looked down and saw my wife sleeping on the floor. My wacky sense of humour compelled me to bounce on her, just to scare her awake. What happened next stopped me entirely. Every time I tried to jump on her, I bounced right back. *I was unable to touch her, as she was enveloped by a blue conical light, extending at least a foot off her body.* She did not move an inch. That is when I understood, beyond any doubt, that the human aura is not a poet's invention. It is a shield — a real, measurable, protective field that stands between you and whatever is trying to reach you.

**What the scientists found**

Some years after that night, I came across the work of Semyon Kirilian, a Soviet scientist who developed a method of photographing the aura. In an Indo-Soviet experiment conducted at Rishikesh in the early 1980s, 60,000 people were photographed. The results formed a simple hierarchy. Corporate executives and traders registered the thinnest auras — half an inch. Farmers and gardeners fared better. Yogis and philanthropists better still. Saints reached up to twelve inches of measurable depth. The numbers confirmed what the tradition had always held:

> "Wealth across lifetimes is measured in the currency of *shakti*. You are only as rich as your aura and its potential."
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.IV, p.49

This wealth does not stop at the boundary of a single lifetime. The spirit body — the *sukshma sharir* — exists between incarnations for periods far longer than any one physical life. It requires *shakti* to move between realms, to protect itself from more powerful spirits, and to ascend through the planes of existence. The aura you build here is the only currency you carry forward when the body is set down.

**The leaking battery**

Knowing the shield exists is one thing; knowing how you wear it down, moment by moment, is another. Sustained anger costs energy — actually depletes the aura's reserve. Hatred, because it persists over time, is a continuous drain. Excessive talk depletes it. Handshakes with someone whose aura is stronger than yours cost you too: energy, like water, flows to the stronger field. Attachments — the binding love called *moh* — draw the field outward and thin it.

*Aatma Sutra* uses the image of a cell phone. The mind is the phone; every thought, perception, and imagined grievance is an app running in the background. Most of us walk around unaware of the dozens of apps we have left open. The battery — the aura — drains steadily while we wonder why we are always exposed, always tired, always a little short of protection when we need it most.

**A ball of light, and the price of ego**

At my Mumbai sthan, a regular *sevadaar* named Nandlal had been struggling for some time. Something disrupted every agreement on his commercial property, just before it finalised. He suspected a spiritual obstruction and asked for help. During meditation, I requested Gurudev to assist. What I witnessed in that meditative state I have not forgotten. Gurudev threw a ball of energy — *translucent white, almost a foot in diameter, expanding and contracting like a small imploding cloud* — and it passed through me and entered Nandlal. Within days the obstruction cleared. Not long after, he encountered a stranger on a train who was in pain; Nandlal touched him and the pain vanished. He had become capable of healing, without fully knowing it.

Then ego arrived. On one occasion, Nandlal splashed energised water into a patient's eyes — a method I had taught him — but he did it on his own terms, without channelling the power of the sthan. The patient happened to be spiritually advanced, with years of her own mantra practice. Energy flows to the stronger field. Nandlal's hand drained. For six months, his right hand was weaker — a physical, measurable imbalance of aura, the price of claiming ownership over what was never his to own.

**The invisible economy**

The aura's economy runs constantly, whether we attend to it or not. There is a mechanism I have described as a Bluetooth connection: when you think intensely of someone — even to criticise them — a channel opens. Sustained criticism of a person's qualities will, over time, transfer some of those very qualities into your own field. Admiration works by the same logic in the other direction. Meditating on a horse can build speed in you; meditating on an elephant, strength; on a lion, courage. This is not folklore — it is the practical corollary of understanding how attention moves energy.

Enhancement works by the same principle. Mantra, chanted with arms and legs crossed so the vibration stays contained rather than bleeding outward, charges the field. Greening places, watering plants — the plant kingdom genuinely exchanges aura with us; gardeners in the Kirilian study were measurably richer than their employers. Seva, because it draws *dua* — the heartfelt gratitude of those genuinely helped — brings blessings that are added directly to the aura's balance.

And then there is what I learned, the hard way, after a reckless attempt to absorb energy from a temple I had not earned the right to enter:

> "I learned the golden rule that energy was better earned than stolen."
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.X, p.120

Your aura is the one asset that survives the body. As *Aatma Sutra* states: *"It is also the armour that protects you from the invasion of other energies, including illnesses and spiritual attacks."* The blue light that kept me from touching my own wife was not magic. It was the accumulation of a lifetime's choices — about where her energy went, what she gave it to, and what came back.

Make yours deliberately.
