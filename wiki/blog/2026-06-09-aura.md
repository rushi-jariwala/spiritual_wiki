On one of my early out-of-the-body journeys, I found myself drifting around the bedroom at around four in the morning while my wife slept on the floor below. I confess that my sense of humour got the better of me — I decided to bounce on her and give her a fright. What happened next was a lesson no scripture had prepared me for.

*Every time I tried to jump on her, I would bounce right back. She was enveloped by a blue conical light extending at least a foot off her body, and neither my most determined effort nor my most ridiculous persistence could get through it.* She did not move an inch.

*(→ [[stories/hingori-wife-aura-protection|Hingori's Wife's Aura — The Blue Conical Light]])*

That light was her aura. The word sounds abstract until you try to walk through one. The aura is the bioelectric energy field that surrounds every living being — the external expression of *shakti*, the spiritual energy accumulated by the spirit body within us. Most saints and deities in iconography are depicted with halos; this is not the invention of devotion. The wider the aura, the more spiritually enriched the person. And its first function — demonstrated that morning against my best efforts — is protection. Diseases first infect the aura before reaching the physical body, and negative energies cannot usually penetrate a powerful one.

> "The aura acts like a protective shield to the body. Most external diseases we contract, first infect the aura and then the body."
> — Hingori, *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.IV, p.40

**What Builds It, What Drains It**

To understand what determines the depth of this field, consider a set of measurements taken in the early 1980s. Soviet scientist Semyon Kirilian developed a technique of photographing the aura, and in an Indo-Soviet experiment at Rishikesh, 60,000 people were photographed. The results were direct: corporate executives and traders had the smallest auras — barely half an inch. Farmers and gardeners did better. Yogis and philanthropists better still. Saints and spiritual practitioners carried auras reaching twelve inches in depth. The connection between how a person lives and the field surrounding them was not an idea — it was measurable.

This has a practical implication that most people have never considered. *Aatma Sutra* offers a precise image for it: think of the *citt* — the total mind — as a cell phone, and every thought, perception, emotion, imagination, and memory as an app running in the background. Every app that runs depletes the battery. The battery is your aura. The executive with the smallest aura is not simply unlucky; he has been running too many apps on the wrong charge. Every outburst of anger, every sustained resentment, every thought-ripple passing through the mind is a withdrawal from the same account.

There is a subtler mechanism alongside this — what I call the Bluetooth principle. When you think of someone, a remote connection is established between your field and theirs. Criticise someone regularly and you begin, quietly and involuntarily, to absorb the very qualities you criticise, because sustained attention opens a channel and energy flows through it. Praise works the same way. Long devotion to a divine form works the same way. *What you meditate upon, that you become. Choose prudently.*

**Nandlal and the Shakti Ball**

To see how *shakti* actually moves between people — and what can go wrong — consider Nandlal.

He was a *sevadaar* at the *sthan* for several years. For months, every attempt to rent out one of his shops had failed at the final stage. He came believing the problem was spiritual. I sat in meditation and asked Gurudev to help. What I saw in that vision stayed with me: Gurudev threw a ball of energy in my direction — *dynamic, translucent white, almost a foot in diameter, expanding and contracting like a small imploding cloud, and alive in the way a sleeping animal is alive.* I threw it at Nandlal. It was absorbed within him. His problem cleared within days. Shortly after, on a train, he touched a stranger who was in pain. The pain vanished. He had become capable of healing without knowing it.

*(→ [[stories/nandlal-shakti-ball|Nandlal's Shakti Ball — Healing and the Price of Ego]])*

What came next was equally instructive. Nandlal, emboldened, used energised water on a patient's eyes — not as an instrument of the *sthan*, but from his own sense of power. The patient turned out to be more spiritually advanced than he was, with years of powerful mantra practice. *Shakti* always flows to the stronger field. For six months Nandlal's right hand was weakened — the energy had drained directly out of it. The lesson was immediate: healing done from the ego leaves the healer exposed. The *shakti* must move through you, not from you. See [[concepts/non-doership]].

**Energy Better Earned Than Stolen**

I learned another version of this at Renuka Lake in Himachal Pradesh, during what I can only call my days of spiritual immaturity.

I was drawn to the energy radiating from a temple belonging to one of Gurudev's disciples, and — in a moment of adventure rather than wisdom — I tried to quietly draw some of that power for myself while praying at the altar. The temple did not react gently. A heaviness settled over my head. Something began destabilising my mind and body in a way I had come to recognise. I walked toward the lake because the earth draws off negative energy, but the heaviness persisted.

I prayed to Devi Renuka — mother of the sage Parshuram, associated with that lake — and asked for protection. What appeared on the screen behind my eyes was a beautiful woman dressed in white, adorned with ornaments, a crown on her head. She touched my head. Within a moment, the attack was gone.

When I returned to Gurudev's camp, I sat at the back like a mouse. He walked over and confirmed it without my saying a word: Parshuramji had punished me for attempting to take what was not mine to take. *Attacked by the son, saved by the mother.* I learned the golden rule that day: *energy was better earned than stolen.*

**The Wealth That Follows You**

All of this — the blue conical light around my sleeping wife, Nandlal's drained hand, the heaviness at Renuka — points toward a single underlying truth.

The spirit body exists between incarnations for periods that often far exceed the sixty or eighty years spent in physical form — potentially centuries in other realms. For this it needs fuel: energy for movement, protection from more powerful spirits, the capacity to ascend. That fuel is *shakti*, and its measure is the aura. Every piece of property, every reputation, every cherished memory of this particular life stays behind when the body is laid down. What travels with the spirit is what was built during the life lived.

> "Your aura potency, in a manner of speaking, is your cosmic currency and you need a lot of it in order to be rich. It is one of the only assets that you can carry forward when you die, and to your future incarnations. It is also the armour that protects you from the invasion of other energies, including illnesses and spiritual attacks."
> — Hingori, *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.X, p.126

The golden rule follows cleanly from this: *Wealth across lifetimes is measured in the currency of shakti. You are only as rich as your aura and its potential.*

Start earning it today.
