One morning, during one of my out-of-body journeys in the early hours, I found myself floating above my wife as she slept on the floor. It was around four in the morning, and what I can only describe as a wacky impulse seized me — to bounce on her and give her a fright. I tried. I was sent back. Every attempt to touch her was deflected, as though she were wrapped in something solid. She was enclosed, from floor upward, in a blue conical light that extended at least a foot from her body. *She did not move an inch, despite my best efforts to wake her.* I floated helplessly around my own wife, unable to reach her. That moment, more than any teaching, showed me what the aura is: a shield so complete that even a disembodied spirit cannot penetrate it.

**What the Aura Is**

To understand why that light was there, it helps to step back and understand what we are. We are not simply a physical body. We are three bodies — physical, spirit (*sukshma sharir*), and causal (*kaarna sharir*) — occupying the same space. The spirit body, made of ether, barely one-tenth as dense as smoke and slightly luminous from within, generates an energy field as it gathers and expends *shakti*. That field is the aura. It surrounds every living being — plant, animal, human.

*Aatma Sutra* frames it plainly:

> "Wealth across lifetimes is measured in the currency of *shakti*. You are only as rich as your aura and its potential."
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.IV, p.49

This is not metaphor. A Soviet scientist, Semyon Kirilian, developed a photographic method that made the aura visible. In an Indo-Soviet experiment conducted at Rishikesh in the early 1980s, sixty thousand people were photographed. Corporate executives and traders had auras of roughly half an inch. Farmers and gardeners fared better. Yogis and philanthropists deeper still. Saints and serious practitioners reached up to twelve inches. The width of the aura is the width of an accumulated spiritual life.

**How the Aura Behaves**

Understanding what aura is, though, is only the first step. What depletes it matters just as much.

Think of the mind as a mobile phone. The total mind (*citt*) is the device; every thought — perception, memory, desire, imagination, comparison — is an application running in the background. Every application burns the battery. The battery is the aura. The more thought-waves (*vrittis*) we feed, the faster we drain the field we rely on for protection and, eventually, for travel in the spirit realms after death.

The same logic governs a subtler circuit. When you think of another person, a Bluetooth connection is established. Criticise them regularly — even privately, even without malice — and you begin slowly to absorb the very quality you are criticising. Praise works identically in the other direction. And in deeper practice: meditating on a horse builds speed, on an elephant builds strength, on a lion builds courage. *"What you meditate upon, that you become. Choose prudently."* (*Aatma Sutra*, Ch.X, p.125.)

**The Shakti Ball**

Understanding the aura as a field that receives and transmits changes how one thinks about healing — a lesson I was given through a disciple at the sthan named Nandlal. He had been a regular sevadaar for several years and was unable to finalise the rental of his shop. Something would go wrong at the last moment, time after time. He came to us sensing the problem was spiritual. Being new to healing at the time, I sat in meditation and requested Gurudev's help. What I witnessed in that state stays with me clearly:

*Gurudev threw a ball of energy — dynamic, translucent white, almost a foot in diameter, and felt alive. It kept expanding and contracting like a small imploding cloud.*

I received it and passed it to Nandlal. The shop cleared. Days later, he happened to meet a stranger on a train who was in pain. He touched him. The pain vanished. Nandlal had become, without realising it, powerful enough to heal.

*(→ [[stories/nandlal-shakti-ball|Nandlal's Shakti Ball — Healing and the Price of Ego]])*

But the story did not end there. Some time later, Nandlal attempted to heal a woman by splashing energised water into her eyes — and he did it with pride, as if the power were entirely his own. The woman happened to be spiritually more advanced than he was, carrying years of powerful mantra practice. Energy flows to the stronger field. Because Nandlal acted from ego — without surrendering the deed to the sthan's authority — his hand absorbed the full reversal. For six months his right hand remained weaker and depleted. The same energy that healed a stranger punished the healer's vanity.

**Energy Earned, Not Stolen**

That lesson in doer-ship took time to absorb. But I confess I needed a cruder version first.

During a period of what I can only call spiritual adventurism, I visited a temple in Renuka belonging to one of Gurudev's disciples. Rather than praying or serving, I attempted to absorb power from the temple directly — a kind of deliberate energy theft. What came back was not power. Something destabilised my mind and body; a heaviness settled around my head that I could not shake. I walked to the lake; the ground helped, but the weight persisted. Unable to think of anything else, I prayed to Devi Renuka — the mother of Parshuram, the deity of that very place I had tried to plunder.

*A beautiful woman in white appeared on my inner screen, adorned with ornaments and a crown. She touched my head. Within a moment the heaviness was gone.*

When I returned to Gurudev's camp, I sat at the very back. He walked over and confirmed it plainly: I had been punished by Parshuramji because I had erred. Attacked by the son, saved by the mother.

*(→ [[stories/hingori-renuka-temple-attack|Hingori at Renuka — Attacked by the Son, Saved by the Mother]])*

> "I learned the golden rule that energy was better earned than stolen."
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.X, p.120

The aura is the record of a life. It registers every act of service, every bout of anger, every prayer, every excess. It can be damaged by black magic or envy. It is depleted by rage, desire, careless contact with lower energies, by talking too much and wanting too much. And it grows — through seva, through mantra, through greening places, through patient daily effort. None of these are separate spiritual practices. They are deposits into the only account that survives the body's end.

> "Your aura potency, in a manner of speaking, is your cosmic currency and you need a lot of it in order to be rich. It is one of the only assets that you can carry forward when you die, and to your future incarnations. It is also the armour that protects you from the invasion of other energies, including illnesses and spiritual attacks."
> — *Aatma Sutra*, Ch.X, p.126

The physical body ends. What you accumulated in banks and portfolios ends with it. The spirit body continues — with its aura, earned or depleted — into realms and lifetimes we have not yet encountered.

Begin earning today.
