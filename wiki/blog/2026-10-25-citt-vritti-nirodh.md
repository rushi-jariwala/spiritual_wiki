*A one-minute read.*

![[blog/assets/2026-10-25-citt-vritti-nirodh-card.png]]

Look at your phone at the end of a busy day. You did not drop it, you did not break it, and yet the battery is nearly gone, quietly eaten by the apps running in the background. Your mind works in exactly the same way, and most of us never think to check its charge.

**Every thought costs something**

Around thirty thousand thoughts pass through a human mind each day, and each one draws a little from the same account. *Aatma Sutra* puts it precisely:

> "Imagine the cell phone as the *citt*, the usage and apps as *vrittis* and the battery charge as the energy or aura. With more and more usage of the apps, the cell phone battery charge is lost."

The *citt* is the mind in its totality, and every *vritti* (a thought, a worry, a memory, a daydream) is an app left open. *(→ [[concepts/citt-vritti-nirodh]])*

**The ocean does not become the wave**

Why, then, do some thoughts drain us far more than others? The *citt* is a vast ocean, and a *vritti* is only a wave in it. When you are sad, the ocean has not become sad; *sadness is only a ripple that passes through the citt, a passing thought.* The real cost comes when we react emotionally and claim the wave as ours. Then a fleeting thought becomes a credit or debit in our karmic record.

**The morning after the jeep ride**

I learnt this on a trip with Gurudev to Himachal Pradesh. I confessed that I suffered from a lot of negative thoughts, and he smiled wickedly. On the bumpy jeep ride that followed, the ugliest thoughts poured through me, one worse than the other. *It felt like a thousand years had passed by the time we reached our destination.* Yet the next morning my mind was quiet; I could smell the greenery and the soil. *There were fewer thoughts and more observations.* *(→ [[stories/himachal-vitark-exhaustion|The Himachal Jeep Ride]])*

**Try it today**

That quiet is *nirodh*. It is not switching off the phone. It is simply not chasing every app that opens. When the next string of thoughts arrives, remind yourself: *these are just passing through my citt. They are not permanent, not mine.* Or, as Pema Chödrön said:

> "You are the sky. Everything else is just the weather."

Watch the weather today. Do not become it.
