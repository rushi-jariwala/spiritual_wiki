*Accepting Yourself, Part 1 of 6. A series walking through Chapter XI of* Aatma Sutra*, one section at a time.*

![[blog/assets/2026-10-25-self-acceptance-1-card.png]]

Walk with me through an ordinary life, and notice how many people are standing along the road holding up a mirror. When you are young, the mirror says you are naive. In the middle years, it says you are too preoccupied to matter. When you grow older, it says you are no longer relevant. I have looked for the age at which a person is finally deemed to have the right balance of maturity, experience and relevance, and I have not found it. At no stage does the mirror approve.

**The mirror at work**

Our working lives simply hand the mirror to someone new. Every rung of the career ladder carries its own diminishment. The junior learns to curb his intelligence in order to survive. The senior carries the weight of accountability while fighting fierce competition for his place. Each of them goes home a little smaller than he arrived, and each believes the measurement.

**The mirror on the wall**

Then there is the mirror in the most literal sense. Most people are dissatisfied with their looks. My grandfather had a line for this:

> "*Seerat ko dekho, surat ko nahi.*" Look at the inner qualities, not the outer beauty.

It is easily given and rarely absorbed. We nod at it and go back to the glass.

**The mirrors we are born into**

Even before we have done anything at all, a mirror has been held up to the circumstances of our birth. Caste, community, region, education and religion, in India especially, each invite disapproval and sometimes outright insult. A person can carry that verdict for a lifetime without once asking who issued it.

**The mirrors closest to home**

The mirrors that cut deepest are held by those nearest to us. There are parents who reprimand where they could have counselled, teachers who damage self-esteem where they could have built it, and friends who are quick to criticise. None of it arrives all at once. It accumulates, one remark at a time, until one day we find ourselves at the bottom of the pile.

> "Thus, as we grow older, we have received so much negative feedback that we stop believing in ourselves."

*(→ [[concepts/self-acceptance]])*

**So who handed them the mirror?**

This is the question I want you to sit with. Every one of these evaluations comes from someone who has no right to make it. Each is a projection through that person's own limitations, a reflection of where they stand rather than of who you are. The answer is not to argue with every mirror, nor to smash them. It is simply not to buy into them. I will not pretend this is easy:

> "By the end of this chapter, it is my hope that, you will learn to assimilate the concept of self-acceptance, which is critical to reaching your spiritual goals. However, the obstacles are substantial. Circumvention is key."

Self-acceptance means holding yourself on an as-is-where-is basis, with all your flaws and faults and all your thoughts, good and bad. It is the first step of three. Acceptance grows into self-love, and self-love, in time, into self-worship. *(→ [[concepts/self-love]] · [[concepts/self-worship]])* None of it can begin while you are still living by someone else's reflection.

Before you believe a mirror, ask who is holding it.
