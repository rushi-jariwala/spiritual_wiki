# Boredom as a Spiritual Tool

## Definition

Boredom, in the Hingori framework, is not a problem to be solved but a spiritually intelligent state to be inhabited and used. It is the tamas guna deployed constructively — not as sluggishness or ignorance, but as an **emotion equaliser** and an **aura conservation method**.

> Boredom is an attitude of neither joy nor sorrow. It dulls the spikes of all feelings and thus acts as an equaliser of emotions. It prevents us from getting stuck in the over-analysis of any subject or obsession with any point of view. It also restrains us from being over-indulgent with likes/dislikes, attractions/distractions, activity/reactivity, etc.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.19, p.198

## Mechanism

The spirit deliberately instils boredom to pull attention inward and away from the external environment:

> Our spirit tries to instil boredom to dull our focus on the outside or external environment. By being too focused on the external environment, we deplete our aura by wasting it on pleasure seeking.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.19, p.198

The aura — the spirit body's energy field — is depleted by constant outward stimulation. Boredom is the inner governor that conserves this energy.

## Tamas Redeemed

Tamas is inertia — it gets reflected as lethargy, sluggishness, lack of inclination, and dullness of activity. Usually discussed as the guna to overcome, Ch.19 presents its redemptive face:

- Boredom through tamas **dulls** the emotional spikes of both pleasure and pain
- It **prevents over-analysis** and obsessive thinking
- It **restrains** the senses from excess: likes/dislikes, attractions/distractions
- It is the **foundation of an emptier mind** — and an emptier mind is the prerequisite for thoughtlessness and meditation

> [!tip] People often mistake extended boredom for depression. Hingori distinguishes them sharply: an extended state of excitement is not a spiritually apt state of mind either. Boredom is the neutral middle that depression is not.

## In Practice

Evolving disciples are advised to learn to be "tuned-off" at many stages of their journey and to go through extended periods of boredom. The purpose is to use tamas *for* the journey rather than against it. Thoughtlessness and meditation, which are the formal tools of mind-stilling, are made more accessible when the ground has been prepared by boredom.
