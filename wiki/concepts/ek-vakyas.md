# Ek Vakyas (Single-Sentence Teachings)

## Definition

*Ek vakya* means "one sentence." Gurudev's primary pedagogical method was not lecture or scripture study — it was the *ek vakya*: a single, compressed utterance that planted a seed and was left for the disciple to germinate over years or decades.

> The short sentences or ek vakyas he used to impart lessons became revelations decades later. He was confident that the next generation would crack the code.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.116

## How They Worked

Gurudev spoke sparsely on doctrine. He was a man of demonstration first — his daily life was the primary teaching. When he did speak, the formulations were brief and dense:

- *"Main sab ka hoon aur sab mere hain. Lekin main kisi ka nahi hu aur mera koi nahi."* — the whole teaching on [[concepts/vairagya|vairagya]] and role-play in two lines.
- *"Luxury is an expense in spiritualism."* — a complete philosophy of spiritual energy conservation in one sentence.
- *"I have made you a guru, not a doctor."* — a complete teaching on [[concepts/intent-as-healing|intent as healing]].
- *"If any member of my spiritual family anywhere in the world goes to bed hungry, I do not eat that night."* — the full scope of compassion without separation.
- *"Vichaar vishay se aata hai."* — (Thought arises from the object of engagement.) A seed-sentence on the origin of thoughts that opens into four distinct observations: thoughts emerge from sensory interaction; from the guna mix; from the magnetic nature of the mind; and from samskars surfacing. Hingori needed multiple paragraphs to unpack it. See [[concepts/hygiene]] and [[concepts/citt-vritti-nirodh]].
- *"As soon as I touch the briefcase, your headache will return. It is my duty to do seva, not my business."* — the entire teaching on commercial hygiene and the difference between seva and commerce in one response. See [[stories/sikh-headache-briefcase]].
- *"Son, nature has a cure for everything — all you have to know is where to look for it."* — delivered to youngest son Nitu ji after a Poison Ivy rash; the full philosophy of investigation before remedy. See [[stories/nitu-bichoo-booti-nature-heals]].
- *"Learn to heal yourself."* — given to daughter Alka ji with blessed cardamoms; unlocked by her only years after his passing as a direct experience of mantra-driven self-healing. See [[stories/gurudev-alka-self-healing]].
- *"We are fakirs."* — to daughter Renu ji, reminding her that the new drawing room was communal space, not private luxury. See [[stories/gurudev-we-are-fakirs-drawing-room]].
- *"Learn to respect food. This is barkat."* — spoken after eating a half-abandoned paratha himself; the teaching on gratitude for abundance. See [[stories/gurudev-half-paratha-barkat]].

None of these were explained at length when spoken. The point was the seed, not the commentary.

## Why This Method

Gurudev's approach reflects a specific epistemology: intellectual understanding of spiritual truths is not the goal. The ek vakya lodges in the disciple's experience and is understood — often suddenly — when life produces the situation that unlocks it. Hingori notes that teachings he heard 40 years earlier continue to yield new meanings. The ek vakya is not a unit of information but a key that may open many doors over a lifetime, in an order the disciple cannot predict.

> He never spoon-fed us but always guided us. He got us started on our spiritual journeys through self-study and personal experience.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.116

## Ek Vakyas Within the Family

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.6 confirms that the ek vakya method was not confined to the guru-disciple relationship. Gurudev deployed the same compressed seed-teachings with his own children, in domestic settings:

- *"Learn to heal yourself"* — to daughter Alka ji, who cracked the code years after his death (see [[stories/gurudev-alka-self-healing]])
- *"Son, nature has a cure for everything"* — to son Nitu ji after a Poison Ivy incident (see [[stories/nitu-bichoo-booti-nature-heals]])
- *"We are fakirs"* — to daughter Renu ji about the communal drawing room (see [[stories/gurudev-we-are-fakirs-drawing-room]])

> [!tip] The family context proves the ek vakya mechanism is universal: the guru plants seeds wherever there is readiness — in disciples, strangers, and his own children — without distinction. The germination timeline is the same: years or decades, after the moment is ripe.
