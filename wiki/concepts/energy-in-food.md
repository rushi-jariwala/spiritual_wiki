# Energy in Food

## Definition

Food is not merely nutritional substance — it carries the emotional energy and intention of the person who prepares it. Indian philosophy holds that a dish prepared by two different individuals will carry distinct energies, which affect those who consume it. The cook's state of mind, emotions, and intentions are transferred into the food during its preparation.

> For Daddy, the significance of food lay not in its taste or flavour, but in the intention and thoughtfulness behind its preparation.
> — Alka ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.89

## Core Teaching

### The Cook's Energy Enters the Food

Given the importance of intentional energy in cooking, temple cooks are meticulously selected. Their spiritual clarity is considered more significant than their culinary skill, because the food they prepare is charged with the energy they bring to it.

Hingori's own experience at the sthan in Gurgaon confirmed this:

> Meals prepared by Mataji there carried a distinctive essence — a difference those attuned to sensing such energies felt.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.89

A less elevated example: Sitaram, a domestic helper at Hingori's parents' house, frequently used colourful language. When Hingori ate meals Sitaram prepared, his own manner of speaking began to reflect that style — a direct transmission of energy through food.

### Light-Coloured Foods Are Particularly Receptive

Food can also be affected by the subtle energies in its environment. White and light-coloured dishes are particularly receptive to these energies. This characteristic makes light-coloured food a potential medium for harmful practices such as black magic.

Hingori's own experience:

> Before my life-changing experience with Gurudev, I fell victim to such negative influences. Someone used white pedas to channel harmful energy towards me. These innocent-looking sweetmeats were charged with negativity through specific rituals and reverse mantras, contributing to my development of severe arthritis.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

**Counter-measure:** Adding spices such as turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured foods changes their colour and pre-empts any potential negative energy in one's environment.

### Food as Karmic Debt

Gurudev cautioned against feeling compelled to consume food financed by others, because receiving food from another creates a karmic obligation. Similarly, he advised against selecting food solely on the basis of flavour — this too is an expression of sensory attachment, not genuine nourishment.

> To him, food was not merely sustenance; it carried spiritual significance, reflecting our emotions and intentions.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.89

### Gurudev's Own Mastery

Gurudev accepted any vegetarian food cooked for him, regardless of whether it was fresh or leftover, simple or elaborate:

> Gurudev readily accepted any vegetarian food cooked for him, no matter how simple. He never demanded particular dishes. His spiritual path had overshadowed his physical needs.
> — Puran ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.89

> To him, the type of dish or its freshness was not a priority. He often picked food from the previous day, even with fresh options.
> — Bittu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.89

His acceptance of food was not austerity for its own sake but mastery over the sense of taste — the seduction of flavour held no grip on him.

## In Practice

- Choose who prepares your food with discernment — the cook's inner state matters as much as the ingredient quality.
- Avoid light-coloured foods in environments of uncertain or hostile energy.
- Add turmeric or black pepper to white foods as a basic energetic protection.
- Do not feel obligated to eat food financed by others; the karmic debt accrued is real.
- Do not select food based primarily on flavour — preference for taste is a form of sensory bondage.
