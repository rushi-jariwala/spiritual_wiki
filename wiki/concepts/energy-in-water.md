# Energy in Water

## Definition

Water is described in Indian spiritual philosophy as the planet's lifeblood — and more than that, as a substance that reflects the emotions and intentions of those who interact with it. Despite retaining its chemical composition (H₂O), water's molecular structure can change in response to consciousness, giving it a dual potential: to heal or to harm.

> Water, often described as the planet's lifeblood, reflects the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. Despite retaining its chemical composition, water's molecular structure can change, manifesting its dual potential to heal and harm.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

## Core Teachings

### Water as a Healing Medium (Jal)

At Gurudev's sthans, *jal* (sacred water) is used for healing. This water is infused with powerful mantras, making it a medium of the guru's spiritual energy. See also [[concepts/guru-tattva]] — the case of Kathog teachers who became healers overnight after drinking jal charged by Gurudev.

The healing quality of jal is not ceremonial; it operates through the same principle that governs [[concepts/intent-as-healing]]: the guru's intention is the operative agent, the water merely the carrier.

### Gurudev Sharing Sipped Water

Gurudev used water in a distinctive way: he would sip from a glass and then pass the remainder to a disciple. This act was a conscious transfer of spiritual energy, sometimes for healing, sometimes to support a disciple's spiritual development.

> He often gave his disciples mantras by sharing his sipped water with them, saying, "I've transferred 10,000 recitations of this mantra to you." Such statements signified that he was imparting some of his extensive spiritual practices — such as mantra chanting — to bolster their spiritual development.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.93–94

This is one of two forms of [[concepts/mantra-transfer]] documented in the tradition — the other being the laung-based declaration of siddhi. The sipped-water form operates through direct energetic sharing, bypassing formal ritual.

### Guru Purnima Foot-Washing Tradition

On special occasions such as Guru Purnima, disciples washed Gurudev's feet with water. The disciples would then drink this water, infused with his spiritual energy, as a way to receive the mahaguru's blessings and deepen their bond with him. This practice is one instance of the broader tradition described in [[concepts/guru-vandana]].

### Water as a Vector for Harm

The same responsiveness that makes water a healing medium also makes it a potential instrument of harm. A disturbing instance:

> A young girl named Kamlesh from Gurgaon unknowingly drank sherbet mixed with a harmful taweez. The consequences could have been dire if the mahaguru had not intervened in a timely manner.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

See also [[stories/kamlesh-black-magic-cure]] for Gurudev's intervention in Kamlesh's more extended encounter with black magic.

## In Practice

- Water charged with mantras and pure intention operates as a healing agent — this is the mechanism behind jal distributed at sthans.
- Do not accept drinks from unknown or hostile sources; the medium of water is porous to intention.
- Receiving a guru's sipped water is a form of energetic transmission, not merely a social gesture.

> [!tip] The science here parallels the energy-in-food principle: both food and water carry the energetic imprint of whoever has prepared or charged them. Light, emotion, and intent leave a trace in the molecular structure of water — a finding echoed in the work of Masaru Emoto, though the Hingori tradition grounds it in mantra and consciousness rather than laboratory methodology.
