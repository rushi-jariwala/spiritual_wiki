# Food and Water as Spiritual Media

## Core Teaching

Food and water are not spiritually neutral. Both carry and transmit the energies, emotions, and intentions of those who prepare, handle, or infuse them. They can serve as vehicles of blessing, healing, and mantra — and equally as vectors of harm.

> Indian philosophy suggests that a dish prepared by two different individuals will carry distinct energies, affecting those who consume it. This belief hinges on the idea that a cook's emotions and intentions are transferred into their cooking.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

## Food as Energy Carrier

### The Cook's Emotions Enter the Dish

The same recipe produces two energetically different dishes depending on who prepares it. Temple cooks are therefore meticulously selected to ensure that food is charged with positive, sattvic energy. The selection is not ceremonial — it is functional.

Hingori's own experience confirms this: Sitaram, a domestic helper who frequently used colourful language, cooked for the family. Over time, Hingori noticed his own manner of speaking began to reflect Sitaram's style. The energy in the food was not symbolic — it was transmitted behaviourally.

Meals prepared by Mataji at the Gurgaon sthan carried a distinctive quality that those attuned to sensing such energies could feel.

### Light-Coloured Foods and Energetic Receptivity

Light-coloured foods — especially white dishes — are particularly receptive to environmental and intentional energies, whether positive or negative. This receptivity also makes them a potential medium for harmful practices such as black magic. Gurudev advised adding spices such as **turmeric or black pepper** to white or light-coloured foods to change their colour and pre-empt the impact of any potential negative energy present in the environment.

> [!warning] Before his life-changing encounter with Gurudev, Hingori fell victim to this. Someone used white pedas charged with negativity through specific rituals and reverse mantras, contributing to his development of severe arthritis. The same Kamlesh from Gurgaon who drank sherbet mixed with a harmful taweez was saved only by Gurudev's timely intervention. See [[concepts/spiritual-attacks]].

### Karmic Debt and Food

Gurudev cautioned against consuming food financed by others without awareness — eating such food can incur a karmic debt. He also held that food should not be chosen solely for its flavour; the intention behind it and the spirit in which it is offered matter more than the dish itself.

> "For Daddy, the significance of food lay not in its taste or flavour, but in the intention and thoughtfulness behind its preparation."
> — Alka ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

## Water as Spiritual Medium

### Molecular Responsiveness

Water retains its chemical composition while its molecular structure responds to the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. This dual nature — chemically constant yet energetically variable — gives water its unique power to heal and harm.

> Water, often described as the planet's lifeblood, reflects the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. Despite retaining its chemical composition, water's molecular structure can change, manifesting its dual potential to heal and harm.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

Jal at Gurudev's sthans is infused with mantras during the healing process. This mantra-charged water is not a symbolic gesture — the molecular structure shifts, and the water becomes a medium for the healer's spiritual energy. See [[concepts/guru-tattva]] for the Kathog healing account.

### Gurudev's Sipped Water — Sharing Siddhis

Gurudev regularly sipped from a glass of water and passed the remainder to a disciple. This was not incidental. The sipped water carried his spiritual energy, used both to cure illness and to accelerate a disciple's spiritual journey.

On mantra transmission: he would sometimes share his sipped water with a disciple and say, *"I've transferred 10,000 recitations of this mantra to you."* This is one form of [[concepts/mantra-transfer]]: not a verbal instruction or a laung-based siddhi declaration, but a fluid transfer of accumulated paath.

### Guru Purnima — Receiving Blessings through Jal

On Guru Purnima, a tradition involved disciples washing Gurudev's feet with water. The disciples would then drink this water — now infused with his spiritual energy — as a means of receiving the mahaguru's blessings and deepening their bond with him. See [[practices/significant-days]].

## In Practice

- Be aware of who prepares your food and in what state of mind — the cook's energy enters the dish.
- Add turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured food in environments where negative energies may be present.
- Avoid feeling compelled to eat food paid for by others merely out of social obligation — it can carry karmic weight.
- Jal from a sthan, infused with mantra, functions as a genuine spiritual medium — treat it accordingly.
- Sipped water offered by a spiritualist is an energy transfer, not a social gesture.
