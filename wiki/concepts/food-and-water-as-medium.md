# Food and Water as Spiritual Medium

## Definition

Food and water are not merely physical substances — they are energy conduits that absorb and transmit the consciousness of those who prepare, handle, and charge them. The same properties that make them essential to life also make them powerful vehicles for both healing and harm.

## Food Carries the Preparer's Energy

Indian philosophy holds that a dish prepared by two different individuals will carry distinct energies, affecting those who consume it. A cook's emotions and intentions are transferred into their cooking.

> "For Daddy, the significance of food lay not in its taste or flavour, but in the intention and thoughtfulness behind its preparation."
> — Alka ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

This principle is the reason temple cooks are meticulously selected — to ensure that food prepared for the deity and its visitors is charged with positive, sattvic energy. At Gurudev's sthan in Gurgaon, meals prepared by Mataji carried a distinctive essence that those attuned to subtle energies could feel.

Hingori's personal testimony extends this principle: Sitaram, a domestic helper at his parents' home who frequently used colourful language, prepared food that the author noticed began influencing his own manner of speaking. The energy of the preparer had transferred through the food.

> [!tip] This is the same mechanism as [[concepts/intent-as-healing]]: the operative agent is the consciousness and intention behind the action, not the material substance. Food is a particularly effective carrier because it is ingested — the contact is intimate and prolonged.

## Food as a Vehicle for Harm

Light-coloured foods — particularly white dishes — are especially receptive to subtle energies, and this receptivity can be exploited.

> Before my life-changing experience with Gurudev, I fell victim to such negative influences. Someone used white pedas to channel harmful energy towards me. These innocent-looking sweetmeats were charged with negativity through specific rituals and reverse mantras, contributing to my development of severe arthritis.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

Gurudev's protective instruction: add spices such as turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured foods. This changes their colour and pre-empts any negative energy that may be present in the environment. The principle is material (colour change breaks receptivity) and metaphysical (the energetic signature of the food is altered by the spice).

See [[stories/hingori-arthritis-cure]] and [[stories/kamlesh-black-magic-cure]] for full accounts of food used as a vehicle for black magic.

## Water as Spiritual Medium

Water, often described as the planet's lifeblood, reflects the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. Despite retaining its chemical composition, water's molecular structure can change — manifesting a dual potential to heal and harm.

> [!tip] This observation is consistent with Hingori's scientific parallels across the corpus: just as sound travels through water five times faster than through air, and water can detect directed sounds and alter its molecular structure, the same physical sensitivity applies to subtle energetic input. See [[concepts/mantra-transfer]] for the water-as-healer section.

At Gurudev's sthans, *jal* is used for healing — water infused with powerful mantras, making it a medium for his spiritual energy. A disturbing counterexample is Kamlesh from Gurgaon, who unknowingly drank sherbet mixed with a harmful taweez, with nearly dire consequences averted only by Gurudev's timely intervention. See [[stories/kamlesh-black-magic-cure]].

## Gurudev's Practice — Sipped Water as Transfer

Gurudev used sipped water as a vehicle for transmitting spiritual energy and mantras:

> He would sometimes sip from a glass of water and then pass the rest to someone else. This act was a way of sharing his spiritual energy and qualities with the other person, sometimes to cure and sometimes to help them on their spiritual journey. For example, he often gave his disciples mantras by sharing his sipped water with them, saying, "I've transferred 10,000 recitations of this mantra to you."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.93–94

On Guru Purnima, disciples would wash Gurudev's feet with water and then drink it — receiving the mahaguru's blessings and deepening their bond with him.

## Karmic Dimension

Gurudev also taught that food carries a karmic charge: one should not feel compelled to consume food financed by others so as to avoid incurring a karmic debt. Accepting food is not neutral — it creates an obligation toward the provider. See [[concepts/karmic-debt]].
