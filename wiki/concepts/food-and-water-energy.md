# Food and Water as Spiritual Media

## Definition

Beyond their nutritional function, food and water are energetically receptive substances that absorb the emotions and intentions of those who prepare or interact with them. This principle underpins both the spiritual protocols around cooking and the healing use of *jal* (blessed water) in Gurudev's practice.

> Indian philosophy suggests that a dish prepared by two different individuals will carry distinct energies, affecting those who consume it. This belief hinges on the idea that a cook's emotions and intentions are transferred into their cooking.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

## Food

### The Cook's Energy Transfers

A meal carries the emotional imprint of whoever prepared it. Temple cooks are therefore selected with care: they must maintain positive intention while cooking, so that the food offered to the deity and to devotees is charged with sattvic energy.

Hingori records a personal illustration: Sitaram, a domestic helper at his parents' house, frequently used coarse language. Over time, Hingori noticed that eating meals Sitaram prepared caused his own manner of speech to reflect Sitaram's style — direct evidence, in his experience, that the cook's energetic state transfers to the food and on to the consumer.

> For Daddy, the significance of food lay not in its taste or flavour, but in the intention and thoughtfulness behind its preparation.
> — Alka ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

### Light-Coloured Foods and Susceptibility

White or light-coloured foods are especially receptive to subtle energies — both positive and negative. This characteristic makes them potential vectors for black magic: negatively charged food (specifically *pedas* in Hingori's case) can transmit harm through specific rituals and reverse mantras. Hingori traces his own decade-long rheumatoid arthritis to white pedas charged with negative energy by a hostile party.

As a practical counter-measure, adding turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured foods changes their colour and reduces their susceptibility to environmental negative energies.

> [!warning] **Black magic via food** — Hingori explicitly connects his arthritis to white pedas charged with negativity through reverse mantras (*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92). This enriches the existing [[stories/hingori-arthritis-cure]] account, which only noted "a peda" as the vehicle.

## Water

### Water Reflects Intention

Water retains its chemical composition while its molecular structure responds to the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. The chapter describes this as a "dual potential to heal and harm": the same substance that can transmit black magic (taweez dissolved in sherbet, as in the case of a young girl named Kamlesh in Gurgaon) can also serve as the most powerful healing instrument in Gurudev's practice.

> Water, often described as the planet's lifeblood, reflects the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. Despite retaining its chemical composition, water's molecular structure can change, manifesting its dual potential to heal and harm.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

### Jal as Healing Medium

At Gurudev's sthans, *jal* infused with mantras is the primary healing instrument. Its uses:

**Sipped water — shakti transmission**: Gurudev would sip from a glass of water and pass the remainder to a disciple — a vehicle for sharing his spiritual energy, qualities, or completed mantra recitations.

> "I've transferred 10,000 recitations of this mantra to you."
> — Gurudev, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.94

The statement meant that thousands of hours of Gurudev's own mantra practice were being allocated to the recipient's spiritual account — compressing decades of effort into a single act of transmission. This is the operative mechanism also described in [[concepts/mantra-transfer]].

**Guru Purnima foot-washing**: on special days, disciples washed Gurudev's feet with water; they then drank this water infused with his spiritual energy as a way of receiving blessings and deepening their bond with him.

**Jal as elixir**: blessed water "retains its freshness and does not smell for years" (see also [[quotes/healing]] for the GOG attestation of this quality).

## Gurudev on Food as Sacred

Gurudev saw food as sacred in two directions:
1. It must not be wasted (the *barkat* teaching — see [[concepts/barkat]])
2. It must not be chosen purely by flavour or funded by others' money, as this incurs a karmic debt

> He often said that one should not feel compelled to consume food financed by others to avoid incurring a karmic debt or select food solely based on its flavour. To him, food was not merely sustenance; it carried spiritual significance, reflecting our emotions and intentions.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

Gurudev's progressive detachment from taste (see [[concepts/senses-management]]) was not aversion but a demonstration of sense mastery — he ate whatever was available without preference, including leftovers from the previous day when fresh food was also present.
