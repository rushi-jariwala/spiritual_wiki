# Food and Water as Energy Mediums

## Definition

Food and water are not merely nutritional substances — they carry and transmit the emotional, intentional, and spiritual energy of those who prepare or interact with them. Indian philosophy holds that a cook's emotions and intentions are transferred into their cooking, and that water's molecular structure can change based on the emotions and intentions of those around it.

> For Daddy, the significance of food lay not in its taste or flavour, but in the intention and thoughtfulness behind its preparation.
> — Alka ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

## Food as a Carrier of the Preparer's Energy

Indian philosophy suggests that the same dish prepared by two different individuals carries distinct energies that affect those who consume it. A cook's emotional and mental state at the time of preparation is transferred into the food itself. This is why temple cooks are meticulously selected to ensure the food prepared is charged with positive energy.

The narrator (Hingori) observed this principle directly. Sitaram, a domestic helper at his parents' home who frequently used coarse language, regularly prepared meals. Over time, Hingori noticed that his own manner of speaking began to reflect Sitaram's style — a direct transmission of the helper's energy through the food he consumed.

> [!tip] This is the principle behind the Indian spiritual tradition of *prasad* — food offered first to the deity and then distributed. The offering encodes devotion; the distribution transfers it.

## Gurudev's Teaching on Food and Karmic Debt

Gurudev taught that one should not feel compelled to consume food financed by others to avoid incurring a karmic debt, nor select food solely based on its flavour. To him, food carried spiritual significance — a reflection of the emotions and intentions behind its preparation.

This connects directly to the existing teaching in [[concepts/karmic-debt]]: accepting food, shelter, clothing, and gifts from others creates obligation. In ancient times people avoided eating others' food for exactly this reason. Gurudev's teaching makes the principle operational for contemporary life: be conscious of whose food you accept and why.

> Gurudev often said that one should not feel compelled to consume food financed by others to avoid incurring a karmic debt or select food solely based on its flavour.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

## Light-Coloured Foods and Negative Energy

Light-coloured foods — white dishes and sweetmeats — are particularly receptive to subtle energies in the environment. This characteristic makes them potential mediums for negative practices, including black magic.

The practical protection Gurudev taught: add spices such as turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured foods to change their colour, thereby pre-empting the impact of any potential negative energy in one's environment.

The narrator experienced this principle personally. Before his life-changing encounter with Gurudev, someone used white *pedas* (a traditional Indian sweetmeat made from milk) to channel harmful energy towards him. The sweetmeats were charged with negativity through specific rituals and reverse mantras, contributing to Hingori's development of severe arthritis. See [[stories/hingori-white-pedas-black-magic]].

> [!warning] The same principle that makes food a medium for healing also makes it a medium for harm. The receptivity of light-coloured foods is neutral — the intent of the preparer determines the polarity.

## Water as a Spiritual Medium

Water, described as the planet's lifeblood, reflects the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. Despite retaining its chemical composition, water's molecular structure can change, giving it the dual potential to heal and harm.

A disturbing example: a young girl named Kamlesh from Gurgaon unknowingly drank sherbet mixed with a harmful *taweez* (amulet charged with negative intent). The consequences could have been severe had Gurudev not intervened in time. See the inline account in [[stories/gurudev-sipped-water-mantra-transfer]].

At Gurudev's sthans, *jal* is used for healing. This water is infused with powerful mantras, making it a medium for his spiritual energy. This is consistent with the water-as-leitmotif running through Gurudev's entire life — born through the blessings of *jal* at Santoksar Sahib, healed through *jal* at Baba Balak Nath, and later wielding *jal* as his primary healing instrument. See [[concepts/intent-as-healing]] and [[concepts/guru-tattva]].

## Gurudev's Use of Water for Transmission

Gurudev used water in unique ways for transferring spiritual energy:

**Sipped water as mantra transfer.** He would sometimes sip from a glass and then pass the remainder to a disciple — an act of sharing his spiritual energy and qualities. He used this as a vehicle for mantra transmission, saying, "I've transferred 10,000 recitations of this mantra to you." This signified the imparting of extensive spiritual practices to bolster a disciple's development. See [[stories/gurudev-sipped-water-mantra-transfer]].

**Guru Purnima feet-washing.** On Guru Purnima, disciples washed Gurudev's feet with water, then drank that water infused with his spiritual energy — a way of receiving his blessings and deepening the bond. See [[stories/guru-purnima-feet-washing]].

This is also connected to [[concepts/mantra-transfer]], which documents the two formal modes of mantra transmission (laung and paath-hours). The sipped-water method is a third, more intimate form unique to Gurudev's personal practice.

## In Practice

- Be conscious of the emotional state of whoever prepares your food; when possible, choose food prepared with care and positive intent.
- Add turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured food if there is reason to be cautious about the energy in your environment.
- Do not feel obligated to consume food paid for by others merely out of social pressure — this can create karmic debt.
- The healing *jal* from a sthan carries the energy of mantras and the guru's intent; treat it accordingly.
