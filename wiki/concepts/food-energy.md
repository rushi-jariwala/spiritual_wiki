# Food and Consciousness

## Core Principle

Food is not merely sustenance — it is a carrier of the emotional and intentional energy of its preparer. A dish made by two different cooks carries distinct energies; the inner state of the cook enters the food and affects those who consume it.

> "A dish prepared by two different individuals will carry distinct energies, affecting those who consume it. This belief hinges on the idea that a cook's emotions and intentions are transferred into their cooking."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

This is why temple cooks are meticulously selected — those who prepare prasad must be chosen for their positive energy, so that the food offered to devotees carries a sattvic charge. Meals prepared by Mataji at the Gurgaon sthan carried a distinctive essence noticed by those attuned to such energies.

## Gurudev's Food Philosophy

Alka ji articulates the operating principle:

> "For Daddy, the significance of food lay not in its taste or flavour, but in the intention and thoughtfulness behind its preparation."
> — Alka ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.90

Gurudev also taught that one should not feel compelled to eat food financed by others to avoid incurring a karmic debt — the material gift of food carries an energetic obligation beyond the act of eating. He himself accepted any vegetarian food, fresh or leftover, indifferently. See [[concepts/senses-management]] for how this flowed from sensory mastery, and [[concepts/barkat]] for the connected teaching on not wasting food.

## Energy-Receptive Foods

Light-coloured or white foods — milk-based sweets, white dishes — are particularly receptive to environmental and intentional energies. This sensitivity cuts both ways:

**Positive**: food prepared with devotion and charged through mantra practice carries healing. Water at Gurudev's sthans infused with mantras is a direct application of this principle. See [[concepts/mantra-transfer]].

**Negative**: white foods can be charged with harmful energy through rituals and reverse mantras, making them a potential medium for black magic. The protective measure is to add spices — turmeric, black pepper — to white or light-coloured foods to change their colour and break any potential negative charge in the environment.

> [!warning] Hingori recounts a personal experience: someone used white *pedas* (a milk-based sweet) charged through reverse mantras to direct harmful energy towards him, contributing to severe arthritis before his encounter with Gurudev. See [[concepts/spiritual-attacks]].

Similarly, *jal* (water) can be weaponised: a young girl named Kamlesh from Gurgaon unknowingly drank sherbet laced with a harmful taweez. The mahaguru intervened in time. See [[stories/kamlesh-black-magic-cure]].

## A Personal Illustration

> Sitaram, a domestic helper at Hingori's parents' house, frequently used colourful language. When Hingori ate meals he prepared, his own manner of speaking began to reflect Sitaram's distinctive style — deepening his understanding of how the energy of the food's preparer impacts those who consume it.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.85–86

## In Practice

- Prefer food prepared by those with a calm, positive emotional state
- Do not feel compelled to eat food given by others when doing so creates an unwanted karmic obligation
- Add turmeric or dark spices to white or light-coloured foods as a precaution in environments of uncertain energy
- The choice of leftovers over freshly cooked food — as Gurudev practised — reflects non-attachment to taste, not disrespect
