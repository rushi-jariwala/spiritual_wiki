# Gaddi (The Guru's Seat of Power)

## Definition

The **gaddi** (literally "seat") is the physical location where a guru habitually performs his sadhana or conducts seva. Over time, the spiritual energy of the guru — and of all who sit there — accumulates, coalesces, and develops a **consciousness of its own**, independent of the guru's physical presence.

> Just like the energy in a temple or other places of worship amalgamates, collects and builds up over the years, developing a consciousness of its own, so does energy in the guru's house and the place of his sadhana.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.137

---

## How Energy Accumulates

During the guru's lifetime, the gaddi is powered by two streams:

1. **The guru's own sadhana** — sustained spiritual practice concentrates energy at the seat.
2. **The disciple's seva** — when a disciple sits on the gaddi during healing seva, the guru's power manifests through him, and the residual energy lodges in the seat itself.

> It was not the gaddi that was the custodian of his power, but because the disciple sat on it while doing seva, the guru's power manifested in him and the residual power accumulated in the seat, making it an energy source of its own. Just as it does in a temple or church.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.137–138

The gaddi thus becomes self-sustaining — a local reservoir that supplements the living guru and continues functioning after his passing.

---

## The Gaddi After the Guru's Death

When the guru dies, **most of the accumulated energy remains** and continues to work in synchronisation with the succeeding guru, carrying forward the spiritual legacy.

> After the guru's death, most of the energy remains behind and continues to work in synchronisation with the succeeding guru, thus carrying forward the legacy of the deceased guru. Therefore, it is paramount to select an appropriate successor.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.137

Exceptions apply only if the successor is incompetent or acts against the institution's interests. The gaddi is the mechanism through which the transference of energy takes place across generations of gurus.

Gurudev created gaddis not only at the Gurgaon sthan but in numerous cities in India and abroad, so that disciples could continue seva at distributed locations.

---

## Phenomena Reported at the Gaddi

**Hingori sleeping on Gurudev's gaddi:**
During a long stay at the sthan, Gurudev insisted Hingori sleep on his gaddi every night. The effects were remarkable: one morning he woke unable to move from his posture yet able to see the room with eyes that appeared shut; at another time he could float a few inches within the framework of his body. In a vision, he saw Mataji sitting on the floor breastfeeding an infant — the infant was himself. See [[stories/hingori-gaddi-sleep]].

**Puranji seeing deities:**
Sitting on the gaddi during seva at the Gurgaon sthan, Puranji sometimes saw deities present in the room.

**Scale of healing:**
During his lifetime, seated on the gaddi — himself or through disciples — Gurudev blessed and healed over a million people.

---

## Spiritual Contests at the Gaddi

The gaddi's accumulated power makes it a target for spiritual envy and attack. On one occasion, a man sitting before Hingori at the gaddi stared into his eyes while muttering a mantra — throwing energy. Hingori switched to the Mahagayatri Mantra; the man's eyes instantly shut, tears gushed out, and he fell at Hingori's feet begging mercy. See [[stories/hingori-mantra-contest]] and [[concepts/spiritual-attacks]].
