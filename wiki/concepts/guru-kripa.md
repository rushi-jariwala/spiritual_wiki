# Guru Kripa

## Definition

*Guru kripa* means the grace of the guru. It enables a disciple to advance to higher levels of consciousness. Social graces, indulgences, or gifts provided by the guru do not add up to guru kripa.

> Sometimes, a reprimanding sentence from the guru can mean more than a profusion of kind words. Thus, guru kripa may not always be sweet and sugarcoated but can be astringent, like a bitter pill that cures.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.12, p.115

## Core Teaching

Guru kripa is not a transaction between personalities — it operates at the level of multiple bodies. For a disciple to receive his guru's grace, he must be accepted by all three of the guru's minds: the physical, the mental, and the spiritual (the different fragments of the mind used by the three bodies — isthul, sukshma, and kaarna sharir).

When a guru showers his disciple with grace, he does so in his own currency and by his own standards, which are far higher than the expectations of the disciple.

A guru's grace may also be noticed when he takes a disciple for granted — because only when a guru feels a sense of ownership does he take the liberty of being informal, treating his disciple as a part of himself.

## Continuity Across Lifetimes

Guru kripa is not confined to the current life:

> A guru's grace on a person is not merely physical in nature but one that might be in continuity from previous lives and may continue to exist till the guru can guide the disciple towards the attainment of mukti (freedom from the cycle of life and death).
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.12, p.117

Being well-behaved, courteous, graceful, and generous may win favours but attaining guru kripa is a deeper concept that works at the level of multiple bodies.

## Distinction from Social Favour

Hingori's account of asking for guru kripa during his second visit to Gurudev — when his arthritis had relapsed — is the clearest illustration of this distinction. Rather than asking for a physical cure, he asked for guru kripa. The exchange became a "battle of wits" and a strange one: Gurudev stopped the queue, took him to a separate room, and insisted he ask for what he truly wanted. Hingori repeated the words like a parrot, not fully understanding them — and Gurudev eventually relented with the words: *"You will get a lot more than you can imagine."* See [[stories/hingori-guru-kripa-request]].

In retrospect, Hingori identifies those two words as the most consequential choice of his discipleship:

> It is entirely because of Gurudev's grace that I count myself as one of the wealthiest men on Earth (spiritually speaking), regardless of health, physical comfort or circumstances.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.12, p.116

## Short Anecdote — Karmakarji and Cocktail Thursday

A disciple named Karmakarji, from the Kolkata sthan, worked at a pharmaceutical company where cocktail parties were obligatory for executives. He observed a restriction of not consuming alcohol on Thursdays. One Thursday a party was scheduled. Having broken the restriction, he went to his guru Dutta Babu (a colleague of Gurudev's) to seek forgiveness. Dutta Babu told him not to be disappointed but instead to use the power of intent to ensure no such event would be held by his company on a Thursday again. Karmakarji willed it — and his company never hosted a cocktail party on a Thursday for as long as he worked there.

> A coincidence? Perhaps. But guru kripa? A distinct possibility.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.12, p.117
