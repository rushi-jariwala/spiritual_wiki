# Jal

## Definition

**Jal** — sacred water — is one of the most consistently recurring instruments in the Hingori Sutras tradition. It appears as healing medium, mantra carrier, energy conduit, and ceremonial blessing. Water's power derives from its unique capacity to receive, retain, and transmit the emotions, intentions, and spiritual energy of those who interact with it.

> Water, often described as the planet's lifeblood, reflects the emotions and intentions of those interacting with it. Despite retaining its chemical composition, water's molecular structure can change, manifesting its dual potential to heal and harm.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

## Water as a Leitmotif in Gurudev's Life

Jal was woven into the arc of Gurudev's destiny from the very beginning:

- **Birth**: the paternal grandfather sought divine intervention from a sage who gave the family *jal* to drink as a blessing for male heirs.
- **First healing**: bedridden after black-magic-poisoned food, Gurudev was cured at the Baba Balak Nath temple by drinking *jal* and vomiting ashes from a masaan.
- **Ministry**: *jal* became his primary healing instrument across all sthans. *"Water was one of the leitmotifs in Gurudev's life. He was born through the blessings of jal and, in later years, healed through it."* — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.9

## Consciousness of Water

Sound travels through water five times faster than through air. Water can detect sounds directed at it and alter its molecular structure without changing its chemical composition. This gives water a form of responsiveness — what Hingori calls its *"own consciousness."*

The practical implication: a glass of water placed beside an asana during mantra recitation absorbs any electromagnetic waves that escape from the auric layer, conserving spiritual energy. The water magnetises and holds what would otherwise dissipate. See [[concepts/mantra-transfer]].

## Jal as Dual Instrument — Heal and Harm

The same responsiveness that makes water a healing tool makes it a potential vehicle for negative energy. Light-coloured beverages are particularly receptive to subtle energies — which is why food and drinks offered by unknown sources, especially sweets and white dishes, carry risk in an environment where negative practices exist.

> A disturbing instance is the case of a young girl named Kamlesh from Gurgaon, who unknowingly drank sherbet mixed with a harmful taweez. The consequences could have been dire if the mahaguru had not intervened in a timely manner.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

See [[stories/kamlesh-black-magic-cure]] for the full account. Adding spices such as turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured foods is advised to change their energy profile and pre-empt the impact of potential negative influences.

## Jal at the Sthans — Infused Healing Water

At Gurudev's sthans, *jal* is used as the primary healing medium. This water is infused with powerful mantras — making it a carrier of the mahaguru's spiritual energy rather than merely a physical substance.

> "Be it water sipped by the guru or energised with mantras, the jal retains its freshness and does not smell for years. It is truly an elixir of life."
> — *The Guru of Gurus*, p.229

The Mahashivratri jal is the most potent annual preparation: water collected from five rivers, enhanced with cloves, black peppercorns, and green cardamoms, then spiritually energised by advanced disciples immersing their hands while reciting mantras. It retains potency even after three-fold dilution. See [[concepts/mantra-transfer]].

## Gurudev's Sipped Water — Mantra Transfer by Jal

Gurudev used sipped water as a direct channel for transmitting spiritual energy and mantra siddhis to disciples:

> He would sometimes sip from a glass of water and then pass the rest to someone else. This act was a way of sharing his spiritual energy and qualities with the other person, sometimes to cure and sometimes to help them on their spiritual journey. For example, he often gave his disciples mantras by sharing his sipped water with them, saying, "I've transferred 10,000 recitations of this mantra to you."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.94

Such statements meant that Gurudev was allocating a portion of his accumulated mantra practice — quantified in recitation-count — to the disciple's spiritual account. The mechanism parallels the laung-based siddhi declaration and the paath-hours allocation described in [[concepts/mantra-transfer]], but uses *jal* as the physical carrier rather than a verbal declaration.

## Guru Purnima — The Charanamrit Ritual

On Guru Purnima, disciples washed Gurudev's feet with water. They then drank this water — infused with his spiritual energy — as a way to receive the mahaguru's blessings directly and deepen their bond with him. This practice is *charanamrit* (water of the feet), a tradition present across many Indian spiritual lineages; in Gurudev's context it carried literal rather than merely ceremonial meaning, as the water was believed to hold his transmitted energy. See [[concepts/guru-vandana]].

## Kathog — Jal and the Activation of Guru Tattva

At the Kathog camp, Gurudev activated the healing capacity of assembled teachers by having them drink *jal*. Overnight, without formal instruction or practice, they became healers — their *guru tattva* awakened through the transmission in the water. See [[concepts/guru-tattva]] and [[stories/kathog-suresh-kohli-vision]].

## In Practice

- A glass of water beside the meditation asana absorbs and conserves aura during mantra practice.
- Sipped water shared by the guru carries mantra siddhis; receiving it is receiving allocated spiritual attainment.
- White or light-coloured food and drinks offered by unknown parties should be treated with caution in spiritually charged environments; adding turmeric or pepper is a protective measure.
- Showering after seva washes away subtle negative energies absorbed during healing work.
- Gurudev's samadhi at Neelkanth Dham: he predicted that people would circumambulate a glass of water placed there and drink it for relief. See [[entities/neelkanth-dham]].
