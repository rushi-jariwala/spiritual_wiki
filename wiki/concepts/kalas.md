# Kalas

## Definition

In Hindu and Yogic traditions, *kalas* refer to the divine qualities or capacities that manifest in a person or deity. These range from wisdom and strength to artistic expression, moral virtue, and humour. In the human realm, one can aspire to master 16 or more kalas. A mahaguru's range of abilities often exceeds this.

> "In the realm of human abilities, one can aspire to master 16 or more kalas. However, a mahaguru's range of abilities often exceeds these. A sharp sense of humour is essential among these kalas, and Gurudev was endowed with it in spades."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.01

## Core Teaching

The kalas are not merely talents or accomplishments — they are qualitative expressions of the divine operating through a being. A mahaguru's kalas are not cultivated through ordinary practice but arise from the accumulated spiritual capital of many lifetimes and the nature of the mission they are born into.

Humour is specifically highlighted as a kala that a mahaguru must possess. For Gurudev, this was not incidental:

> "As time unfurled, I observed Gurudev's adeptness at weaving humour and wit to leave a lasting impression. On the surface, his comments seemed light-hearted, yet the underlying messages always landed, resonating deeply with those who understood."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.01

## In Practice

Dwarkanath ji recalls that Gurudev's charisma was immediately apparent upon their first meeting: struck by his "humility and intellect," he saw that "every meeting transformed casual acquaintances into ardent admirers." This is the kala of charisma expressed naturally and without artifice.
