# Kalas

## Definition

**Kalas** are the divine qualities that manifest in a person or deity, as described in Hindu and Yogic traditions. The word is drawn from the Sanskrit *kalā*, meaning a portion or fragment — here understood as a fragment of divine capacity brought into expression. Traditionally, an evolved human being can aspire to master **16 or more kalas**. These range from wisdom, strength, and moral virtue to artistic expression, humour, and emotional mastery.

> In the realm of human abilities, one can aspire to master 16 or more kalas. However, a mahaguru's range of abilities often exceeds these. A sharp sense of humour is essential among these kalas, and Gurudev was endowed with it in spades.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.17

## Core Teaching

A mahaguru's set of kalas *exceeds* the standard 16. Where an accomplished human may embody wisdom, strength, artistry, and moral virtue, a mahaguru also manifests healing ability, elemental command, bilocation, prophecy, and total detachment — qualities that lie beyond the conventional 16.

Hingori singles out **humour** as emblematic: it is simultaneously a kala (a sign of spiritual maturity and inner ease) and a pedagogical tool. Gurudev's wit was never frivolous — his jokes carried teachings that "always landed, resonating deeply with those who understood."

## Nuances

- Kalas are not merely personality traits. In the yogic framework they are *spiritual capacities* — developed through sadhana, seva, and accumulated samskaras of a high order. They are expressions of the jivaatma's refinement, not just the personality's charm.
- The traditional Indian enumeration of **16 kalas** relates to the sixteen phases of the moon and to figures like Krishna (said to be *shodasha kala sampurna* — complete in all 16). A mahaguru — by definition — transcends this ceiling.
- Humour, in this context, is not entertainment. It is the capacity to perceive and communicate the absurdity of maya without losing equanimity. Only those free from the grip of ego can truly laugh.

## In Practice

Gurudev wielded humour and wit "to leave a lasting impression." His surface-level comments were light; the underlying messages always landed. Dwarkanath ji noted that "every meeting transformed casual acquaintances into ardent admirers" — the kala of charisma in action.
