# Mantra Vidya

## Definition

The science of mantras — in Gurudev's tradition, one of three pillars of self-realisation:

> Among the spiritual tools Gurudev advocated, mantra vidya is a powerful medium for inner transformation. The mahaguru fully embraced its significance, highlighting its essential role with two other vital pillars—seva and the refinement of gunas—on the journey of self-realisation. His mantras were siddh, distinct from those found in traditional scriptures or online resources. He selflessly bestowed these powerful mantras upon his disciples, enabling them to achieve siddhi in remarkably short durations.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.149

**Siddhi** (footnote, p.149): "Mastering a specific mantra through continuous recitation, often under the guidance of a guru. The practitioners aim to align their energy with the vibrational frequency of the mantra… Achieving siddhi is considered a sign of the mantra becoming fully activated within the practitioners."

The mechanics of practice (asana, fixed time, water, ajapa japa, round pillow) are on [[concepts/mantra-transfer]] (*The Guru of Gurus*) and [[concepts/tantra]] (*Guru Sutra* Ch.3). This page gathers the mantras themselves and what disciples have witnessed them do (*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.11).

## Core Teaching — The Four Mantras of Ch.11

### Chamunda — Protection

"A Shakti mantra dedicated to Goddess Chamunda, a wrathful form of Goddess Durga… She symbolises protection from and the destruction of negative energies" (p.150). Gurudev demonstrated it by leaving RC Malhotra ji alone among ethereal beings in a forest — [[stories/malhotra-chamunda-forest-spirits]].

Testimonies from the sthans (pp.150–151):

> "The Chamunda mantra can be used for defence and protection. When you are sent to someone's house to negotiate with a spirit that refuses to cooperate, you can use this mantra to either scare it away or as a protective tool."
> — Darius Moos ji, sevadaar at the Mumbai sthan, "over three decades dedicated to mantra vidya," p.150

> "As part of our seva of offering spiritual relief, we often find ourselves visiting houses infested with spirits. These negative energies are sensed as intense pressure around the head. However, specific mantras, such as the Chamunda mantra, act as armour against these unsettling energies. As we start chanting the mantra internally, the heaviness begins to fade. This is tangible evidence of the mantra's capability to counter negative forces."
> — Ridhim ji, p.150

> "At times, when facing hostile spirits, they can disguise themselves as revered entities like Shiv, attempting to provoke or challenge you. Sometimes, they may even use flattery as part of their cunning tactics. In these instances, the Chamunda mantra is especially powerful in countering their deceit."
> — Rajeev Hazarat ji, "an experienced practitioner and longtime sevadaar," p.151

Darius ji's dream (p.151):

> "I had a dream once where a very evil-looking spirit attacked me. When I started chanting the Chamunda mantra, I grew to around 20 feet in height, holding two weapons of blue light, and I chopped the spirit into pieces!"

"The remarkable ability to transform and reach a height of 20 feet showcases the extraordinary capability of an advanced suksham sharir, which Darius ji, given his longstanding commitment to seva and mantra vidya, evidently possesses" (p.151). See [[concepts/sukshma-sharir]].

**Healing** — "Upon integrating it into patient care, we have discovered its remarkable efficacy in treating skeletal, muscular, and neurological pain. Moreover, it has shown significant potential in alleviating stress and tension." (Darius ji, p.151)

**Coolness** — Ashish Khanna ji, during an intense meditation, "felt a distinct negative energy encroaching upon my space… I transitioned from the Mahagayatri mantra, which I was chanting, to the Chamunda mantra, seeking protection from this unwelcome force. The change in atmosphere was immediate. As the Chamunda mantra resonated within, a coolness and lightness surrounded me, contrasting sharply with the heat that the Mahagayatri mantra had radiated." (p.152)

### Mahagayatri — Fire, Water, Liberation

"A sacred verse from the Rig Veda… one of the most powerful mantras in existence… 24 syllables divided into three lines, each with eight syllables… a potent call to the solar deity Savitur, seeking illumination of the intellect" (p.153). Its origin with Brahmarishi Vishwamitra and its fragmentation by Guru Vashisht: [[stories/vashisht-fragments-mahagayatri]]. In *The Guru of Gurus*, Buddhe Baba gives Gurudev its eight missing words ([[concepts/mantra-transfer]]).

> "Mahagayatri is more than just a mantra; it embodies the spirit of the four Vedas. Charged with energy, it grants tangible and spiritual rewards to those who chant it. One can break free from the endless loops of birth and death by invoking its power."
> — Gurudev, recalled by Ravi Trehan ji, p.154

What it does (p.154): bridges "the tangible realm of our physical existence with the subtle expanses of spiritual dimensions"; channels "energies from the lower chakras to the higher ones"; purges [[concepts/samskars|samskaras]] — "a vital step toward achieving [[concepts/mukti|mukti]]… eventually paving the way for the ultimate spiritual goal: dissolution of identity or [[concepts/moksha|moksha]]."

**Fire and water** — "It ignites the fire element within practitioners and has a deep affinity with the water element" (p.154). Hingori's temper with Khemchand, and his vision of a swimming woman read by Gurudev as siddhi: [[stories/hingori-mahagayatri-fire-and-water]]. "Regular hydration is advisable to mitigate the intense heat generated by this mantra." Also:

> "Years into my practice of the Mahagayatri mantra, I began to experience clear water-themed visions. I saw myself walking on the water and noticed snakes under the water. Curious about this, I talked to Guruji (Gurudev's disciple), and he told me that these visions were due to the Mahagayatri mantra I was chanting."
> — Pooja Seth ji, p.155

> "The essence of Gayatri is the life in water… Perhaps this connection with water is why Gurudev consistently advised against eating fish or seafood."
> — Raji ji, p.155

See [[synthesis/mantra-and-water]].

**Witnessed uses** —
- Water surfacing on barren land: [[stories/ashish-khanna-gopiganj-water-mustard]]
- A spirit shrinking from five feet to one: [[stories/kapill-mahagayatri-spirit-shrinks]]

### Mahamrityunjay — Continuity

"A Shiv mantra commonly perceived as a shield against the inevitable clasp of death" (p.160).

> "[It] controls all that is destructible in the world. Its essence goes beyond safeguarding life; it prevents disintegration and ensures continuity."
> — Raji ji, p.160

> "When we infuse cloves and cardamoms with the Mahamrityunjay mantra, they become charged with a powerful healing energy."
> — Babloo ji, a disciple of Santosh ji from Nadaun, p.160

Said to have "shown remarkable healing results, particularly in cases where the medical prognosis was uncertain or inclined towards a long-term or seemingly impossible recovery" (p.160). See [[concepts/spiritual-healing]].

### Mahaguru Mantra — Where All Mantras Merge

Raji ji: the Mahagayatri (Shakti) and Mahamrityunjay (Shiv) are highly revered, but there is "a broader, more profound dimension within the mantric tradition that transcends the individual energies of Shakti and Shiv" (p.160). "This union represents the harmonic synergy of every naad or sound within the universe. Therefore, the Mahaguru mantra is the final, infinite mantra."

> "The Mahaguru mantra is incredibly powerful. It combines three divine energies: Guru Brahma, Guru Vishnu, and Guru Maheshwara. Despite their distinct forms, they emerge from and represent the same divine essence. This mantra goes beyond these forms to recognise a supreme power, the Par Brahm. The mantra's words, 'Guru shakshat par brahm, tasmayee shri guruve namah', mean that the guru embodies the ultimate truth, surpassing all illusion."
> — Ravi Trehan ji, p.161

See [[concepts/guru-tattva]], [[concepts/guru-vandana]].

## Nuances

### Intention amplifies the mantra

> At this stage, it is crucial to highlight another key aspect of the healing process—intention. The power of a mantra is greatly amplified when aligned with the healer's focused intention. This synergy explains how a single mantra can be effectively applied to achieve success across diverse spiritual endeavours.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.151

See [[concepts/intent-as-healing]].

### Mantras given in dreams

> "I have come across some people who have been given mantras in their dreams. I, too, received a mantra during a dream, but the deity's identity was not revealed to me. However, upon waking, I found I had lost memory of the words. When I shared this experience with Guruji (Gurudev's disciple), he suggested that I keep a record of any similar revelations in my dreams in the future."
> — Kapill Malhotra ji, p.152

> "Years ago, I was given a mantra in a dream. Its rhythm resembled the mantric chants with which we are familiar. Unfortunately, upon waking, the precise words and the deity it invoked slipped from my memory."
> — Rajeev ji, p.152

"These incidents offer a captivating perspective: dreams are not mere flights of fancy; they are channels for spiritual insight" (p.152).

### Switching mantras to the need

The sthans "ordinarily" use the Chamunda for clearing spirits (Kapill ji, p.157), yet the Mahagayatri appears in the same role across the chapter. Ashish ji switches from Mahagayatri to Chamunda mid-meditation when negativity approaches (p.152).

> [!warning] Diminished efficacy vs. witnessed feats
> *Guru Sutra* (Ch.3; cited in [[concepts/guru-disciple]]): "in our day and age, even very powerful mantras work only at the level of the aura and do not have the same effect as they once did." *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.11 reports water surfacing on land with no underground source, a spirit shrinking before two disciples at once, and safer mines.

## In Practice

> As a businessman, I believe three halves are better than one whole; I, therefore, advise people to do their mantras while performing daily tasks such as walking, cooking, driving, etc.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.161

> "When I began my journey with mantras, Gurudev encouraged me to recite them continuously, regardless of what I was doing. He assured me that he would acknowledge each recitation. From that moment, every mantra Gurudev gave me seamlessly blended into my daily routine and, eventually, became my breath."
> — Raji ji, p.161

> Mantra chanting transcends mere repetition. It serves as an anchor for the mind, steering it away from its tendency to drift, eventually becoming an integral part of one's essence—a phenomenon Raji ji eloquently refers to as 'becoming one's breath'. The consistent practice of chanting mantras has a transformative effect. The key to accessing these remarkable benefits is not in the pursuit of more, but in doing more.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.161

> "Mantra vidya requires a disciplined and dedicated approach. The more effort you invest in it, the greater the benefits you will experience."
> — Ravi Trehan ji, p.161

Cf. ajapa japa ([[concepts/mantra-transfer]]) and [[concepts/constant-awareness]].
