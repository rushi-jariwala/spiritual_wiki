# Patanjali's Eight-Fold Path (Ashtanga Yoga)

## Overview

The sage Patanjali used aphorisms (*sutras*) to lay out a blueprint for achieving realisation of one's true nature and connecting to the consciousness supreme. His prescription is based on an eight-fold plan — encompassing ethics, posture, breath, sense-withdrawal, concentration, meditation, and ultimately, samadhi.

> The great sage, Patanjali, used aphorisms or sutras to lay out a blueprint for achieving realisation of one's true nature and plugging in to the consciousness supreme. His methods included modification of attitudes and behaviour as well as the practice of asanas and meditation.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.19, p.193

Hingori presents a simplified version of the eight limbs to make them accessible. For the complete original, he recommends Christopher Isherwood's rendition and Dr. Karambelekar of Kaivalyadham, Lonavala.

## The Eight Limbs

### 1. Yamas — Ethical Restraints

The practice of *yamas* improves social conduct:
- **Nonviolence**
- **Not depriving anyone their due** and not stealing
- **Not eyeing someone's spouse**; overcoming sensuality and freeing oneself from thoughts of lust or carnal desires

### 2. Niyamas — Personal Discipline

Practicing a lifestyle of *niyamas* — maintaining bodily and mental hygiene and directing energy toward philosophy. Focusing on positive thoughts and accepting life on an "as-is-where-is" basis helps direct mental energies toward spiritual development.

### 3. Asanas and Mudras

Certain postures and healing *mudras* create auric colours that enhance the body's capacity to hold energy and work as an electromagnet.

### 4. Pranayam

Regulating *prana* (vital energy) so that energy within the body is evenly distributed, harmonising the functioning of body, mind, and spirit.

### 5. Pratyahara — Withdrawal of Senses

Indulgence in the senses keeps one rooted to the lower chakras (energy centres). The practice of *pratyahara* — withdrawal of attention from sensory objects — dulls the magnetic pull of the senses, freeing the kundalini (electromagnetic energy) to move toward the higher chakras.

### 6. Dharana — Concentration

Practicing concentration — by focusing on one's breath, a mental visual, or a symbol — hones the ability to stay steady and centred. Such focus directs energy to the subject of concentration. Dharana leads to Dhyana (meditation):

> Whatever you concentrate on, you naturally attract towards yourself.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.19, p.194

### 7. Dhyana — Meditation

The natural development of sustained concentration into meditation. Stage that immediately precedes samadhi.

### 8. Samadhi

The goal of the eight-fold path. By following and perfecting each of the prior seven stages, one can connect with one's true self. Until that connection is achieved, yoga serves a limited purpose.

## Connection to Hingori's Framework

The yamas and niyamas are referenced throughout the Hingori corpus as the behavioural prerequisites for reducing tamas and rajas in the guna mix. Pratyahara maps directly to [[concepts/senses-management]]. Dharana and dhyana align with [[concepts/citt-vritti-nirodh]] — the stilling of thought-ripples. Samadhi corresponds to the deepest stage of [[concepts/moksha]].

> [!tip] Adherence to yams and niyams can help reduce tamas and rajas in the guna mix. Pratyahara or withdrawal of consciousness can take one on a fast track to sattva. — *Guru Sutra* (paraphrase, earlier chapters)
