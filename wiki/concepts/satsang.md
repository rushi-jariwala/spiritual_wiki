# Satsang

## Definition

A *satsang* is a gathering of spiritualists for the purposes of discussing or practicing spiritualism. The word connotes *sat* (truth, the real) and *sang* (association) — a coming-together in the company of truth and of those who seek it.

## Purpose

At satsangs, like-minded people come together to discuss spirituality, share experiences, and search for solutions. This serves two interconnected functions:

1. **Mood-framing** — the satsang frames the participants' mood in alignment with a spiritual frame of reference, keeping them oriented toward their practice rather than toward the distractions of ordinary social life.

2. **Sustaining momentum** — the collective energy sustains spiritual momentum for a longer time than individual practice alone.

## Group Dynamics and Collective Energy

The more significant function of satsang is energetic rather than informational:

> The group dynamics at satsangs ensure that the energies of all involved work collectively towards a common intent or interest. This is best demonstrated when spiritually inclined people come together.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.19, p.192

The same principle operates in any group context: spending time with corporate intellectuals results in some of their intellectual intensity rubbing off; spending time with those who lack sincerity of purpose results in the acquisition of some of that insincerity. Association is not merely social — it is an energetic transfer.

## Choosing Association Carefully

Because the quality of association shapes the quality of one's own energy and intention, Hingori recommends deliberate selectivity:

> I would suggest that you carefully choose the people to mingle with and make sure that their aspirations and intentions match yours.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.19, p.192

This is not social exclusiveness but an application of the same aura-protection logic found elsewhere in the tradition: what you consistently expose your energy field to, you absorb.

> [!tip] The collective energy principle at satsangs is the same mechanism that makes the sthan powerful: shared intention amplifies individual practice. See [[concepts/aura]] and [[practices/seva]].

## Relationship to the Guru-Disciple Path

Satsang is explicitly recommended for those who have not yet found a siddh guru. Gurudev's most effective teaching tool was seva, but for the seeker who has neither a guru nor a direct seva assignment, the satsang provides the next best energetic support: community, momentum, and the subtle transmission that occurs when spiritually aligned people gather.
