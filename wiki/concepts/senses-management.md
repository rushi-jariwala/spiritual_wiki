# Senses Management

## Definition

Senses management is the practice of governing the five senses — sight, hearing, touch, taste, smell — not by suppression but by *observation*, so that the practitioner becomes an observer of the sensory play rather than the actor who indulges in it. This detachment allows the senses to be used as tools rather than rulers.

> Gurudev learned to manage his senses not by suppressing them but by observing his reactions as they played out. In this manner, he became an observer of the sensory play rather than the actor who indulged in them. By being an observer, the mahaguru immediately created a template for controlling and manipulating his senses as per his will.
> — *The Guru of Gurus*, p.172

This is distinct from asceticism or mortification. It is a shift in *position* — from actor to observer — that allows full engagement with the world while remaining unbound by it. See [[concepts/role-play]] for how this same posture shapes Gurudev's emotional functioning.

## The Deeper Problem — Maya of the Senses

Human sensory perception is fundamentally partial. We detect approximately 0.00000000000035% of the electromagnetic spectrum — leaving us blind to infrared, ultraviolet, and the overwhelming majority of light. Sounds at frequencies used in dog whistles are inaudible to us. The solid objects we touch are, at the atomic level, primarily empty space. Despite these limitations, our senses form the foundation of our perceptions, which mould our beliefs, thoughts, and actions. Our limited perceptions make us mistake our narrow slice of experience for the entire scope of reality — the concept referred to as [[concepts/maya]] in Eastern philosophy.

The brain has no direct interface with the outside world. It interprets electrochemical signals from the sensory organs. As a result, *what we see is an internal model of our personal world, not what is out there.* Our perceived reality is a more personalised view of reality and not the cosmic reality. To align with the cosmic perspective, you must overcome the limitations of a personalised worldview — which begins with managing the senses.

## Why the Senses Bind

> Multiple senses working together give rise to a series of emotions leading to a range of actions.
> — *The Guru of Gurus*, p.172

## Gurudev's Methods, Sense by Sense

### Sight

The mahaguru's formula for dealing with the opposite gender: imagine every attractive woman simultaneously in her *gudiya* (child) and *budiya* (aged) forms to accurately evaluate attraction — dissolving the immediate impulse or *vaasna* that surfaces. Hingori went further, visualising every woman he met as a skeleton — insurance against untoward desire that also revealed *"beauty was literally in the mind of the beholder."*

Gurudev exemplified this indifference through practice: he was never seen wearing cologne, held that one should not be overly drawn to beauty or repulsed by a lack of it, and lived without being swayed by life's dualities in either direction.

Gurudev taught that when evolved spiritualists die, their spirits arrive at a crossway — one path looks enticing, the other is simple and plain. He consistently advised choosing the plain path: the simpler route leads to higher spiritual dimensions, while the attractive path misleads and keeps the spirit in lower realms. Hingori's own visits to the loks confirmed this: *"The higher realms were bright yet sparsely inhabited, a stark contrast to the lower realm, which felt like a dark, seedy nightclub."* See [[stories/hingori-lok-visits]].

### Smell

Taking a leaf from Aghor vidya: *develop an indifference to stench if you want to cultivate an indifference to incense.* Hingori practised tolerance and nonchalance to unpleasant smells; over time he also gave up colognes and perfumes. The sense of smell as a teacher in both directions.

### Taste

Gurudev's own food journey: foodie in youth — relishing chole bhature and pinnis, whistling tunes and enjoying flavourful Indian meals (documented by Gaggu ji). From the 1970s–1980s onward, a clear shift: sattvic by the time of his ministry. Lassi (buttermilk) for lunch on most days; nimbu pani (lemonade) for breakfast. On Mahashivratri, he did not eat until the last person in the queue had been met — which could take two days. He would also go days with just tea and no food, and often close his eyes before eating to scan if all his spiritual children had been fed. See [[stories/gurudev-bittu-scan-before-eating-wg]].

> "He who learns to feed others before feeding himself transcends the desire for food."
> — Gurudev to his daughter, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.171

> "Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda."
> (When one experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison.)
> — Gurudev, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92; also *The Guru of Gurus*, p.35

Gurudev's principle: one should not feel compelled to consume food financed by others (to avoid incurring a karmic debt), nor select food solely based on flavour. Food was not merely sustenance — it carried spiritual significance reflecting the emotions and intentions of its preparation.

#### Food as Energetic Medium

Indian philosophy holds that a dish prepared by two different individuals carries distinct energies, affecting those who consume it. A cook's emotions and intentions are transferred into their cooking. Temple cooks are meticulously selected for this reason — to ensure the food they prepare is charged with positive energy.

This principle works in both directions. Hingori observed that a domestic helper who frequently used colourful language affected his own manner of speaking through the food he prepared. Light-coloured foods (white dishes) are particularly receptive to subtle energies — a quality that makes them useful in spiritual practices and potentially dangerous in hostile hands. Adding spices such as turmeric or black pepper to white or light-coloured foods changes their colour and reduces their susceptibility to negative energy absorption.

> [!warning] Food is a documented attack medium. Hingori's own severe arthritis was caused by white *pedas* charged with negative energy through specific rituals and reverse mantras, before his meeting with Gurudev. See [[stories/hingori-white-pedas-arthritis]]. A young girl named Kamlesh was harmed through sherbet mixed with a taweez. See [[stories/kamlesh-black-magic-cure]].

He also recommended fasting once a week as a detox that reduces bodily secretions and frees the mind from activity and stress.

An anecdote illustrates sensory management through taste: Gurudev once visited an associate's home with disciples. The associate and wife were absent; their eight-year-old daughter offered to make tea and brewed it on a kerosene stove. Upon leaving, the mahaguru gave his disciples a sharp mouthful for barely sipping the kerosene-smelling tea: *"Is it possible they lost a lot because they could not bear a little?"*

### Hearing

The sense of hearing connects us to others in ways no other sense can. Gurudev discouraged idle talk and gossip. His discipline: develop discernment — intuitively hear the unsaid, and be selective in responding to what is heard. *"Do not believe anything we hadn't seen or heard for ourselves."*

Gurudev's own evolution with sound mirrors his broader sensory arc: in youth he enjoyed music deeply — whistling popular tunes, playing the mouth organ, loving the classic songs from *Binaca Geet Mala* on the radio. As he progressed spiritually, his relationship with auditory stimuli transformed from passive enjoyment to an active spiritual tool. He could hear thoughts and perceive spoken words from great distances.

During a road trip Hingori took with his father from Chandigarh to Gurgaon, the two argued most of the way. Upon arriving in Gurgaon, Gurudev gently yet promptly admonished Hingori for his behaviour, fully aware of the entire exchange. *"His vigilance was such that nothing went unnoticed."* See [[stories/hingori-chandigarh-gurgaon-argument]].

A celebrated anecdote on discernment: when a disciple denied a mistake in front of Gurudev, the mahaguru had Bittu ji fetch a sealed set of blank cassettes from a bedside cabinet and play the third one. Those seated heard the verbatim first-person conversation of the disciple bragging about the same 'mistake' he had vehemently denied. See [[stories/blank-cassette-disciple]].

### Touch / Physical Sensation

Gurudev's mastery over physical sensations was documented by multiple witnesses. In the cold winters of the hills, others would layer themselves heavily; Gurudev was comfortable in merely a sweater. At his camp at Mungaoli, Madhya Pradesh, his influence over physical sensation extended to those around him: Hingori had been wearing only a T-shirt in the cold, but hurriedly put on a sweater upon Gurudev's return out of apprehension. He immediately started shivering uncontrollably. Gurudev's simple question — *"Why did you do that?"* — miraculously stopped the shivering. The lesson: *"Our senses, though strong, can be influenced in ways we might not realise... being genuine; the harsh pitfalls of pretence."* See [[stories/hingori-mungaoli-shivering]].

High temper in powerful spiritualists is attributable to the uprisen fire element owing to intense tapasya. Gurudev's method in early years at IARI: he sat in the corridor outside his office abusing passersby. When they retaliated, he smiled back — deliberately inciting abuse to test his resilience. He erased a strong emotion by inducing it until it lost its grip. *Lesser the emotion, more the stability of the mind.*

### Managing Sight — The Young Devotee's Tea

Gurudev once blessed the tea offered by an eight-year-old hostess and handed the cups to his disciples. Some barely sipped and left the rest because it smelled of kerosene. The mahaguru's rebuke was direct: could they not endure a small discomfort for the sake of what had been offered and blessed?

## Neuroplasticity

When the brain is denied input in one sensory modality, it can rewire itself to support and augment other senses. The areas of the brain dedicated to the lost sense are remapped to process other senses, sometimes including the birth of new neurons.

Gurudev instructed Hingori to open a school for the deaf in Dehradun, which Hingori eventually did. Working with the deaf revealed their exceptional manual dexterity and high concentration — seldom distracted by sound, their sense of touch is dramatically enhanced.

> The mahaguru suggested we let one vice remain till we manage to control or offset the others. Ultimately, we must give up even the remaining vice.
> — *The Guru of Gurus*, p.173

Hidden in this simple suggestion were *the secrets of neuroplasticity* — progressive sensory control, with the last vice serving as an anchor while the others are progressively relinquished. Hingori identifies his remaining challenge as the sense of taste.

## The Mind

The brain is a multisensory organ blending information from various senses. The mind — distinct from the brain — is the spirit and causal-body counterpart. It provides extrasensory information to the brain, granting access to psychic abilities: intuition, telepathy, remote sensing, clairvoyance, clairaudience, psychokinesis.

The physical body is not solid matter but vibrating strings of energy — seven octillion atoms made of energy vortices, each with a distinct energy impression. As a result, the universe is nothing more than vibrating strings of energy, consistent with quantum physics' findings.

To change the state of your consciousness, you must change the level of your mind. The higher the level of the mind, the lesser its movement and the greater your awareness. When the mind is stiller, it reflects the param-aatma to a greater degree.

## In a Nutshell

> The form exists in the seeing. The sound exists in the hearing. The feel exists in the touching. The taste exists in the tasting. The smell exists in the smelling. If a person can diminish the experience of seeing, hearing, touching, tasting, and smelling, then there isn't much to experience. At this stage, the experiencer begins to experience himself. In that experience of himself, he grasps his limitlessness and realises, as is he, so is the experience.
> — *The Guru of Gurus*, p.175

Sensory management is ultimately the method by which the jivaatma strips away the personalised worldview and begins to perceive its own nature.

> [!tip] **Structural pattern — intention imprints on matter**: WG Ch.7 gives the clearest cross-corpus articulation of a single underlying principle: a practitioner's intention enters and is carried by matter — whether food (cook's emotions), water (mantra-charged *jal*, sipped-water transmission), or objects used in rituals (negative practitioner → white pedas). The principle operates identically in healing (see [[concepts/intent-as-healing]]) and in spiritual attack (see [[concepts/spiritual-attacks]]). It is the same substrate: matter as a medium for consciousness. The chapter places this principle at the intersection of the sensory world and the subtle world — precisely the domain that senses management navigates.
