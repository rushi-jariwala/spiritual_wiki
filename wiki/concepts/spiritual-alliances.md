# Spiritual Alliances (Cosmic Collaboration)

## Definition

The working partnerships Gurudev formed with deities, rishis, saints and spirits of other traditions and realms — "cosmic collaborators" — in service of one shared goal. *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.8 closes:

> "These alliances are not grounded in typical emotions or societal norms but rather focus on a shared goal: to uplift and enlighten all beings collaboratively."
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.8, p.115

## Quotes

> "Siddh gurus innately understand the spirit of collaboration; it is embedded into their philosophy. Supporting and uplifting each other's spiritual families is a shared endeavour."
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.8, p.97

> "Imagine the prime minister coordinating various ministries—be it finance or defence—to advance the nation's prosperity… All these divine entities are in harmony with my intentions."
> — Gurudev (to Giri Lalwani ji), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.8, p.98

More in [[quotes/spiritual-alliances]].

## Core Teaching

**Beyond the logic-driven mind.** The chapter opens by comparing rishis descending from the higher lokas to comic-book heroes: Superman is "readily accepted" because of his fictional landscape, while real beings from beyond bhu-loka seem "unbelievable to our logic-driven mind." Many of us may have come from those realms, but "the veil of forgetfulness confines our beliefs to what our senses can comprehend" (p.97). See [[concepts/loks]], [[concepts/senses-management]].

**The cosmic order.** "Most devi-devtas do not originate from the most exalted lokas, so they respect and engage with beings higher in the cosmic order" (p.98). Gurudev, embodying "the essence of Shiv," therefore spoke to them as a coordinator speaks to ministers:

| Need | Entity Gurudev approached (to Giri Lalwani ji, p.98) |
|---|---|
| Children | Brahma |
| Economic hardship | Vishnu or Laxmi |
| Extension of life | Shankar, then Vishnu for well-being |
| Protection | Chamunda or Mahakali |

"When Gurudev speaks of his spiritual connections, it may sound like boasting to the uninitiated" (p.98). See [[entities/giri]].

**Alliance by diplomacy.** At Renuka, the domain of Parshuram ji, "no spiritual entity can venture there without Parshuram ji's consent." The alliance of 1980 "necessitated a perfect blend of tact, diplomacy, and spiritual synergy" — Parshuram ji shrank from 27 feet to 18 to his regular nine as he recognised Gurudev's depth. See [[stories/gurudev-renuka-parshuram-alliance]]. An alliance protects the disciples too: Hingori's impulsive act at Renuka drew only "a spiritual rebuke rather than something more severe" because of it ([[stories/hingori-renuka-temple-attack]]).

**Kinship through a shared guru.** Ravan, a disciple of Shiv, is Gurudev's gurubhai ([[stories/gurudev-ravan-gurubhai]]); Augarh, Gurudev's disciple, is his disciples' gurubhai and elder brother ([[entities/augarh]]). Gurudev read Guru Gobind Singh as embodying "the essence of Shiv" ([[stories/surinder-gurudev-namaaz-vision]]).

**Fingers of one hand.** "To Gurudev, the various spiritual paths were like the fingers of a single hand—each unique, yet all integral to the whole" (p.106). He prayed namaaz in a Chicago disciple's vision, facing north: "the essence of prayer transcends the direction in which one bows" ([[stories/surinder-gurudev-namaaz-vision]]).

**Alliance-building as practice.** Gurudev sent disciples to temples and shrines "for spiritual outreach and alliance-building." Gaggu ji: "We were in Kashmir when Gurudev directed us to visit the Hazratbal shrine and the Shankaracharya temple. He instructed us to go inside and pay our respects on both occasions, while he chose to remain outside. I never saw him entering these places of worship" (pp.107–108). At Badrinath he sent Hingori in to hug the deity as a brother ([[stories/hingori-badrinath-brother]]).

**Alliances outlive the body.** Hundreds of ancient sages, with Sai Baba of Shirdi at their centre, sat among the mourners at Gurudev's chautha — "when spiritual beings leave the earthly realm, those connected to them on the spiritual path come to pay their respects." Months later Sai Baba promised Pradeep ji undisturbed seva ([[stories/pradeep-sai-baba-chautha-and-seva]]).

## Nuances

- **Respect flows upward.** In Punchoo ji's dream, Laxmi, Parvati and Saraswati "paid their respects, hinting to Gurudev that he should have summoned them" ([[stories/punchoo-trinity-of-devis]]). Dhir Saheb saw himself "escorting Gurudev, Jesus Christ, Sai Baba of Shirdi and Guru Nanak Dev to the sthan in Mumbai. They had arrived there to meet Guruji (Gurudev's disciple)" (p.97).
- **Visions granted to disciples.** Puran ji: "Gurudev granted me visions of the Buddha, Jesus Christ, Lord Shankar, and even Sri Sharada Devi, the wife of Sri Ramakrishna Paramhansa." Shankar ji was "a radiant young man in his mid-twenties with fair skin, and standing about 5 feet 10 inches tall"; Jesus, "speaking in imperfect Hindi," said, "Your guru is truly magnificent." Puran ji: "Every saintly figure I have encountered has an ethereal glow. Their auras are incredibly powerful" (pp.98–99). See [[concepts/aura]].
- **Approach decides the response.** "In stark contrast" to Hingori's rebuke, Pradeep ji approached Parshuram ji respectfully: "In the early morning, maybe around 4 am, I saw a clear lake where I could see every little stone and fish. From its centre, a figure emerged, standing on khadaus, towering at around nine feet, with hands folded together in a gesture of prayer, gazing upwards. What caught my eye were his strong, bright red eyes. This was when I realised I was seeing Parshuram ji for the first time" (p.101).
- **Hingori's own list.** "Raji Sharma ji was blessed by Lord Shankar through Gurudev's intervention" ([[stories/rajee-shiv-darshan]]); Hingori's vision of Ram, Laxman and Sita ([[stories/hingori-vision-of-ram]]); Darius Moos "was graced with a glimpse of Jesus"; and "recurring appearances of Sai Baba of Shirdi in our journeys have prompted us to reconsider and even discard traditional beliefs about what is deemed impossible or implausible" (p.102).
- **Hanuman and Mangal.** FC Sharma ji: Gurudev mentored Malhotra ji nightly, "imparting powerful mantras, and leading him to divine encounters with deities like Hanuman ji. Gurudev aimed to elevate Malhotra ji's spiritual stature to the level of Hanuman ji himself" ([[entities/rc-malhotra]]). Hingori, who has "not directly experienced Lord Hanuman's bond with Gurudev," was given a Mangal mantra "by a mysterious force" in a vision, and on a mountain linked to Mars, he and his gurubhai **Nitin Gadekar** "were gifted two handkerchiefs, seemingly materialising from thin air" — "influenced by the mahaguru's strong alliance with this powerful entity" (p.103).
- **Guardians of the sthan.** At Bayri, during renovation, "a panther mysteriously appeared and began to guard the site each night, posing no threat to anyone. It vanished once the renovations were over" (p.101). Augarh "protected the sthan when Daddy was away" (Renu ji, p.109).
- **Recognition by test.** "Many spiritual luminaries often test each other's spirituality before recognising each other formally" (*WG* Ch.13, p.189). At Renuka Gurudev kept his identity hidden "until he had established a significant spiritual alliance with Parshuram ji," and Parshuram ji tested "his emotional ties to his family" through his ill daughter's visit ([[stories/renu-renuka-parshuram-test]]). The Jwalaji priests tested him with a siddh purush's khadaus ([[stories/jwalaji-khadaus-test]]). "Gurudev utilised his camps to establish several spiritual alliances, not only with the venerable Parshuram ji and his mother, Renuka ji, but also with other devi-devtas and many other spiritual entities yet to be unveiled" (p.189). See [[synthesis/gurudev-under-test]].
- **Tests.** Some collaborators also test. Augarh was "Gurudev's personal test-o-meter" — see [[synthesis/tests-in-disguise]].

## In Practice

- Disciples are encouraged to call on the allied energies: Hingori has "encouraged many of my disciples to deepen their connection" with Augarh (p.111); Geeta Nagpal ji called him "Augarh Bhaiya" ([[stories/geeta-nagpal-augarh-bhaiya]]).
- Approach another's domain with respect and consent — the Renuka contrast.
- Gurudev marked the days of his allies: sleeping on the floor on Dussehra eve for Ravan; deep paath on Kartik Purnima (Guru Nanak Dev's birth anniversary); on Janmashtami, delegating prasad distribution to a chosen few and withdrawing to meditate (p.106). See [[practices/significant-days]].
- "Spirits, having traversed many lifetimes, return as mentors and guides to their relatives and spiritual followers. They offer guidance and support, particularly to those who seek and respect them" (p.115).
