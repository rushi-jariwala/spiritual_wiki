# Spiritual Attacks

## Definition

**Spiritual envy** is the corrosive form of jealousy that arises when a spiritualist watches another attain what they cannot. It can transcend into the bloodstream, leading practitioners to use their accumulated power to attack those they envy — through mantras, yantras, black magic, or direct energy projection.

> Envy is a quality that can transcend into the bloodstream, leading a spiritualist to often resort to malpractice by using power to attack he envies.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.139

The chapter's central verse on this:

> *"Envy bludgeons reason*
> *with the sight of another on the rise.*
> *Resentment thrives, swords are drawn*
> *so he doesn't reach the prize."*
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.140

See [[stories/guru-sutra-ch13-poem2]].

## Methods of Attack

### Black Magic via Personal Objects
A personal object — clothing, belonging — can be used as a **conduit** for directing negative energy. Hingori cites a young boy whose swimming trunks were stolen from a clubhouse and used as a conduit for black magic, causing paralysis of his legs. Hingori himself was a victim of black magic performed by a temple priest, which led to his contracting rheumatoid arthritis.

### Death Yantra by Post
A yantra — a geometrically charged occult diagram — can be embedded in a letter. Gurudev received such a letter; knowing its contents before opening it, he asked Hingori to leave the room, then tore the letter and reassembled it to expose and neutralise the yantra. The attempt on his life failed. See [[stories/inland-letter-yantra]].

### Direct Energy Projection (Mantra-Throw)
A practitioner can sit before their target and project energy by reciting a mantra while maintaining eye contact. This method was encountered at the gaddi itself. See [[stories/hingori-mantra-contest]].

### Attacks in the Dream State
Spiritual attacks can occur during sleep — immobilisation, choking, assault by hostile energy forms. Practitioners must fight their way out using mantras and spiritual weapons. See [[stories/hingori-deity-attack-sleep]].

### Temple Energy Reactions
Smaller temples can react negatively to a powerful spiritualist walking through their doors, if the temple's energy considers them a threat to its influence. This is not personal malice but a territorial response of accumulated energy. See [[stories/sthan-hostile-temple]].

## How to Defend

Gurudev trained disciples in the art of **self-preservation**. The primary tool is the Mahagayatri Mantra, used with unwavering focus and steady eye contact against an attacking force.

The key principle: when attacked spiritually, **do not absorb — redirect**. Mental recitation of the Mahagayatri while looking directly into the attacker's eyes causes the projected energy to rebound. The attacker's own force returns to them.

> As I switched to the Mahagayatri Mantra while staring deeply into his eyes, the man's eyes instantly shut and tears gushed out. He then fell at my feet begging for mercy.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.141

This principle also applies to hostile temple energies. Rather than retaliating, a more effective approach is to send offerings — sweetmeats — to the temple deity, communicating goodwill and non-rivalry. See [[stories/sthan-hostile-temple]].

### The Keel
A **keel** is a circle of spiritual protection drawn mentally with directed energy around a person or object, preventing negative spirits or energies from entering. See [[stories/hingori-magician-keel]].

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.11 (p.158) defines it more widely: "Energy boundaries to contain or neutralise negative energies within a given space. Additionally, they function as protective barriers, efficiently shielding areas, individuals, and objects from negative influences within a specified radius." Keels are created with mantras such as the Mahagayatri, and are used to **trap** a spirit so it can be cleared — Hingori's keel around Nandlal's shop, which drew out the spirit of Nandlal's uncle ([[stories/nandlal-shakti-ball]]); Kapill ji's keels in a Delhi hotel and a Dehradun room ([[stories/kapill-keels-delhi-dehradun]]).

### The Chamunda Mantra
The sthans' usual mantra for spirits: "used for defence and protection" (Darius Moos ji); "armour" against the "intense pressure around the head" of negative energies (Ridhim ji); "especially powerful" against hostile spirits disguised as "revered entities like Shiv" or using flattery (Rajeev Hazarat ji) — *WG* Ch.11, pp.150–151. See [[concepts/mantra-vidya]], [[stories/malhotra-chamunda-forest-spirits]].

## Gurudev's Warning on Misuse

Gurudev always taught the positive use of energy and warned specifically against wishing somebody ill:

> "If we did, we would pay the price of a negative deed."
> — Hingori recalling Gurudev's teaching, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.142

This connects to the karma framework: a negative wish that fructifies becomes negative karma for the wisher, not only the target. See [[concepts/karma]], [[concepts/negative-karma]].
