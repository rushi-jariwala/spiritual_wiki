# Supernature

## Definition

**Supernature** is the transcendent nature of an advanced spiritual being — the sum of capacities that exceed all human and natural limits. In *The Guru of Gurus*, the term is used exclusively in relation to the mahaguru, and it is distinguished from *siddhis* (acquired powers that he renounced). Supernature is not an accumulation of powers; it is what remains — and indeed what *emerges* — after all powers are surrendered.

> He rose to the status of a mahaguru when he renounced his siddhis in the Ganges and became the manifestation of the powers he represented.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.248

The mahaguru's supernature is described as the full expression of the Shiv-parivaar: the OM, trishul, shivling-gileri, Ganpati, Nandi, and jyot manifested as symbols on his body — not decorative, but functional certifications of the powers they represent. See [[concepts/power-symbols]].

## The Central Paradox

The defining formulation of this concept is the inversion Hingori arrives at in the closing section of the chapter:

> The mahaguru's supernature was not limited by his destiny, but he chose to limit his destiny to express his supernature.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.248

This is the answer to the chapter's opening question: *"Why did this spiritual superman design for himself the destiny of a commoner?"* The answer is that the choice of ordinary circumstances was itself an expression of extraordinary nature — not a constraint upon it. Limitlessness choosing limitation is itself the proof of limitlessness.

The Brighu Samhita offers the only available external description:

> "He is like Shiv while in meditation and like Vishnu in his speech. In seva and protection, he is like Brahma, and in relieving people of their pain, he is like Jagdamba. His aura is like the sun, warm on the outside, soft inside, and filled with hidden knowledge. People will not be able to know the real entity behind this human form."
> — Brighu Samhita, cited in *The Guru of Gurus*, p.232

And Gurudev's own claim, confirmed by every account in the chapter: *"We would never know who he really is."*

## Dimensions of Supernature

### Multiple Identities / Form-Changing

The mahaguru was witnessed appearing in different forms by several disciples. He appeared in a five-headed form (with a body bathed in golden light) to Shambhu ji — one of the few times he allowed such a glimpse, and even then, at great cost to the viewer. Other disciples caught partial glimpses:

- **Kwatra ji** (Gurgaon): on Mahashivratri at 3 am, the room "instantly filled with dazzling light as if hundreds of suns had risen" — he collapsed at Gurudev's feet; see [[stories/kwatra-ji-blinding-light]]
- **Ravi Trehan ji**: asked to see Gurudev's spiritual form; in a dream days later saw him seated on tiger skin in a cave — Shiv's iconography, Gurudev's face; Gurudev silenced him the next day without being told see [[stories/ravi-trehan-shankar-dream]]
- **Raj Laxmi ji (Guddan ji)** of Kanpur: dream of Gurudev seamlessly shifting his form between himself and Lord Shankar, "back and forth, in a continuous transformation"
- **Rajpal ji** saw his head double in size; **Gupta ji** of Parwanoo saw a dwarfed form (~4 feet through a glass door)
- Pappu ji (son of Shambhu ji) saw the long-bearded form within minutes of the clean-shaven avatar

The connection between Gurudev and Shiv — described in *Witnessing Greatness* as rooted in the conviction that the mahaguru is an *ansh* of Shiv, confirmed by Rishi Bhrigu in the Bhrigu Samhita — is the theological basis for these form-sightings.

See [[stories/shambhuji-five-headed-form]] and [[stories/uddhav-ji-black-triangles]] for the most detailed accounts.

> At best, we saw him as a human being with an advanced spirit rather than an advanced spiritual being in human form.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.235

### Elemental Synergies

Gurudev's connectivity to the elements endowed him with influence over them:

- **Water**: He located the exact spot on his Khandsa farm that would yield potable water when an earlier borewell had failed; converted arid land at Renuka into greenery by digging a water channel. Waves at Juhu Beach and Mahabalipuram moved selectively to caress his feet while others standing nearby were not touched.
- **Rain**: He could will rain to fall or stop. On one Guru Purnima, rain fell to exactly the extent of the queue of visitors — and stopped precisely at the queue's end.
- **Fire**: The Mahagayatri mantra confers the ability to contain fire. Most Mahashivratri nights, the mahaguru dipped his hands in boiling lemon tea to bless it — without a flinch, wince, or blister. The same mantra "ignites the fire element within practitioners and has a deep affinity with the water element" (*WG* Ch.11, p.154) — see [[synthesis/mantra-and-water]].

Disciples empowered by him also demonstrated elemental command: Pappu ji of Jwalamukhi was protected from tantrik fireballs by his deceased father Shambhu ji. See [[stories/pappu-shambhuji-flames]].

### Body-Mind Capacities

- **Food**: Could live without food for days. On Mahashivratri, queues lasted two days; he did not eat until he had met them all.
- **Temperature**: Could withstand extreme temperatures; continued in a light cardigan when others shivered in layers.
- **Sleep**: Could put one hemisphere of his body to sleep while the other remained awake and operational. Pehalwan ji observed him riding a scooter with eyes closed — he was doing *paath* at the time.
- **Speech**: A man of few words, yet communicated far more than he spoke. His words had the power to come true. He never said 'NO'. As Ashok Bhalla ji recalls: *"The word NO does not exist in my dictionary."*

### Omniscience of Time

> "When people come to meet me, their past, present and future flashes on a screen in my mind. I can also tell if they have come with faith or just for fun."
> — Gurudev to Das Saheb, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.245

He lived in constant awareness of past, present, and future. He could exhaust karmas, change samskars, and subtract years from a person's future life to add to their present one. See [[concepts/constant-awareness]] and [[concepts/spiritual-healing]].

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.9 (p.121) gives Das Saheb's fuller version: "There is an ethereal screen before me, visible only to my eyes. Every detail about everyone in the queue—their past, present and future, problems, faith, devotion, and even their casual curiosity—all manifest on that screen. I know the help they seek within moments and can determine the appropriate guidance." Gurudev also told Hingori: "After seva, simply remember the first and last person you met, and all those in between will naturally settle in your thoughts." Das Saheb is identified as "a follower from Chandigarh."

### Bilocation and Invisibility

He was physically present and absent at the same time. Documented bilocation instances from across the sources:

- **KL Bakshi ji / Sunil Ghai**: Sunil called from England to report Gurudev had visited his London shop — at the exact moment Bakshi ji was seated with Gurudev in Gurgaon
- **Dr. Shankarnarayan ji**: directed to a hilltop temple by Gurudev; on leaving the temple, saw Gurudev at a distance — yet when he returned to the office, Gurudev had never left; Gurudev's response: *"What's the big deal?"* See [[entities/dr-shankarnarayan]]
- Conversing at his office and his farm at about the same time
- Attending to a phone call in a locked room in Nagpur while seated with disciples moments earlier

The mechanism is the **nirman kaya** (created body): by internalising and manipulating energy — envisioning oneself in a specific asana and using specific mantras — advanced yogis shaped an energy body mirroring the physical form. This practice, mastered by ancient yogis, allowed them to create energy duplicates for discharging karmic liabilities more efficiently. Gurudev's bilocation is understood as his adeptness in creating multiple nirman kayas. Notably, his disciples — and their disciples — also bilocated, often without conscious awareness. See [[stories/virenderji-uninvited-guests]], [[stories/ashish-khanna-goa-bilocation]], and [[stories/nakul-malhotra-bilocation]].

He could walk through crowds unrecognised at will. The crowd at his Gurgaon home gate did not see him — *"They will stop us only if they see us"* — and Rajpal Sekhri ji, accompanying him, was equally unseen. See [[stories/rajpal-sekhri-gurudev-invisible]] and [[stories/gurudev-invisible-srinagar-shop]].

Highly advanced yogis can transform the molecular structure of their bodies to stop light from reflecting off them — and thus vanish from sight. This capacity was also found, involuntarily, in disciples: Ashish ji was photographed partially invisible in Kazakhstan. See [[stories/ashish-ji-kazakhstan-partial-invisible]].

If he did not wish to be photographed, shutters froze or reels came out blank.

For the full astral-travel treatment, see [[concepts/astral-travel]].

### Spiritual Mastery

He had command over the world of spirits — helping them move from lower realms to higher ones, granting them human birth when appropriate. He travelled astrally four to five days a week for periods of two to seven hours. He took disciples on astral voyages to Paris, to a raft on an open sea, and across the planets of the solar system. See [[concepts/astral-travel]] for the full account.

He deployed his astral form in service — appearing in disciples' dreams to heal, reveal futures and pasts, transmit mantras, and exhaust karmas in the dream state rather than the physical one. He developed multiple *nirman kayas* (subtle energy bodies) to simultaneously attend to many people.

He allied with advanced spiritual entities to jointly serve life forms, including Shirdi Sai, Guru Nanak, Guru Gobind Singh, Guru Vashisht, Parshuram ji, Lord Krishna, Dattatreya, Ganpati, Hanuman, several Muslim saints, Shiv manifestations, and Devis such as Mahakali, Laxmi, Saraswati, and Renuka.

### Post-Mortem Appearances

Ordinarily once individuals pass away they do not manifest physically, but Gurudev defies this norm. After his 1991 passing, he has appeared physically to multiple witnesses:

- **Asha Sekhri ji's daughter**: attended her wedding physically, gifted his ring; years later walked protectively with her in Oslo between her and following men, disappearing when she reached her front door. See [[stories/asha-sekhri-daughter-ring-oslo]]
- **Rajneesh Sharma ji** (married to Gurudev's youngest daughter Alka ji): a neighbour saw Gurudev around the building premises for three to four days in a row after his death
- **Narender ji and Saroj ji**: briefly saw Gurudev in physical form the day before their Maharudra yagya (July 1992); saw a figure in all white approaching the yagshaala; their daughter saw him standing at their Karnal sthan years later — he vanished instantly. See [[stories/narender-saroj-maharudra-yagya-apparition]]
- **Sudhir** (Hingori's childhood friend): in full waking consciousness, saw Gurudev approximately seven to eight feet tall, shirt sleeves folded, in white, saying "Come, son" — and was thereby led to the sthan. See [[stories/schoolmate-gurudev-gurgaon]]

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.3 frames these post-mortem appearances within the concept of Maya: what ordinary people call miracles are simply another day in the mahaguru's world. Advanced spiritual practice expands consciousness to where the impossible becomes conceivable. The chapter's question to the reader: *"If one individual can manifest as Shiv, what might you, too, be capable of achieving?"*

### Statue Consciousness

The chapter also introduces a new dimension of supernature: the mahaguru's consciousness operating through consecrated statues. At the Asthal temple in Dehradun, statues of Gurudev (and Mataji) underwent pran pratishtha by over 65 disciples — and have since been the site of multiple healings, visions, and one reported case of a disciple's consciousness entering the statue and directing remote healing. See [[concepts/statue-consciousness]] and [[stories/asthal-temple-statue-miracles]].

## Kept Under Wrap

Hingori notes that the chapter deliberately underplays the supernature accounts so the reader does not get distracted from the core theme: the activation and enablement of spiritual transformation.

> He used every power he had in that service but depending on the angle of view, you either saw the ordinary man or the extraordinary mahaguru.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.248

> [!tip] The choice of ordinariness was not modesty — it was precision. The mahaguru calibrated exactly how much of his supernature to express in each moment, and that calibration was itself a supernatual act.

## Nuances

> [!question] Supernature versus siddhi — what is the distinction?
> Siddhis are *acquired* powers that the mahaguru renounced at Har ki Pauri in 1970. Supernature is what emerged *after* that renunciation — it is what he *is*, not what he *has*. The power symbols that appeared on his body are certifications of this nature, not trophies of accumulation. This distinction is made explicit in the chapter's closing lines.


## *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.12 — Elemental Symphony

Ch.12 gathers the eyewitness record of the elemental command summarised above. Its thesis, from the RC Malhotra ji initiation: "The elements of nature are under my control" (p.164); and from Baljeet ji: "Controlling the weather is a simple task for me" (p.166).

**Water.** Freshwater under a chosen spot on the saline Khandsa farm; a canal traced with a stick at Renuka that "receded to its source" once the camp was vacated ([[stories/gurudev-khandsa-renuka-water]]). The Ganges stilled "the moment he complied" ([[stories/malhotra-initiation-ganges]]). Two short testimonies:

> "At that time, Punjab was devastated by severe floods, and our hometown of Hariana was heavily affected. When Gurudev entered the temple grounds where my husband's body lay, something extraordinary occurred. The floodwaters, threateningly close to the temple's entrance, began to recede. It was as if the water showed respect, softly touching Gurudev's feet before withdrawing."
> — Snehalata ji (widow of Gurudev's younger brother, 1988), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, p.166

> Santlal ji shares a compelling story of an incident at Juhu Beach, offering another instance where nature seemed to acknowledge Gurudev's spiritual eminence. He saw an isolated wave emerge, seemingly out of nowhere. It moved intently, softly caressing Gurudev's slippers before quietly receding into the ocean. This deliberate gesture by the sea, singling out Gurudev's slippers from everything else on the beach, served as a subtle, yet powerful, testament to the mahaguru's spiritual powers.
>
> Giri ji and I also witnessed similar phenomena. The ocean's behaviour bore a striking resemblance to what Santlal ji had witnessed, leading us to affectionately coin the term 'same to same' to describe these shared experiences.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, p.166

**Rain.** Two short testimonies, and one longer one ([[stories/baljeet-sector-10-rain-pause]] — nine dry days in 1989):

> He describes an incident in Odi, situated in the rugged beauty of Narkanda in Himachal Pradesh: "The heavens had opened up, unleashing a fierce torrent of rain and hail. As Gurudev stepped outside his camped tent, he spoke to the raging heavens with a commanding tone, declaring, 'Enough is enough'. As if directly responding to Gurudev's powerful command, the stormy skies immediately cleared!"
> — Gupta ji of Parwanoo, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, p.164

> I had the privilege of witnessing something similar at a camp in Mungaoli. Gurudev and I were seated in a tent when a heavy downpour began. He stepped outside to look intently at the sky, then returned, yet the rain continued unabated. As I conversed with him, I noticed his focus seemingly divided. Suddenly, he rose, angrily pushed aside the tent flap, and with an authoritative voice directed towards the heavens, he commanded the rain to cease, and cease it did, almost instantly!
>
> As a witness to this extraordinary spectacle, I found myself momentarily at a loss for words, captivated by the sheer magnitude of the moment.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, pp.164–165

**Nature's rhythm.** Giri ji: "Gurudev was a true embodiment of his teachings, demonstrating through his actions the significance of planting trees and engaging with agriculture" — at Khandsa "He would plough the fields on his tractor, hand-pick the vegetables, and milk the cows. During harvest season, he would also lend a hand in separating the chaff" (p.167). "Gurudev always emphasised that our actions should align with the nature's rhythm" — and nature "can sometimes push back": [[stories/devraj-khare-lonavala-trees]] ("Just let nature be"). See also [[stories/gurudev-mohammedpur-greening]], [[concepts/karmayoga]].

**Fire.** Rashmi ji watched him put his hands into boiling lemon tea "not just once but thrice" ([[stories/gurudev-boiling-lemon-tea]]); Roshini ji's burn vanished at the thought "He mastered control over fire" ([[stories/roshini-tea-burn-conviction]]). And a jyot that lit itself:

> "During a visit to a devotee's home, accompanied by another sevadaar, Akash Sharma ji, we departed without lighting any jyot," he recalls. Unexpectedly, they later received a call from the homeowner inquiring if they had lit the jyot before leaving. "We had not done anything like that, yet the jyot had mysteriously ignited by itself, without any ghee," he reveals.
> — Gautam Bhushan ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, p.170

**Space.** Astral travel — "The vast expanse of space often set the stage for many of the mahaguru's spiritual journeys" (p.170). See [[concepts/astral-travel]] (Ch.12), [[synthesis/missed-astral-invitations]].

**Matter.** Kheer and gulab jamuns that "never seemed to diminish" at Shilai ([[concepts/barkat]]); a car driven 80–85 km on an empty tank ([[stories/pravesh-kapoor-empty-tank]]).

> Gurudev's connectivity to the highest realms of consciousness granted him extraordinary abilities to transform the nature of objects, alter the atomic structure of elements, and impact the smallest particles of the universe.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, p.177

> [!tip] Ch.12 closes by pointing beyond the miracle: the narratives "emphasise the limitless potential that spiritual awareness can unlock" (p.177) — the same move as Ch.3's "what might you, too, be capable of achieving?"
