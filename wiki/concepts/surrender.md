# Surrender

> *"Surrender to the guru is a prerequisite for a fruitful guru-disciple relationship."*
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.9, p.77

## Definition

Surrender (*sharanagati*) in the spiritual context does not mean defeat or abdication. In ordinary usage the word implies giving up, acknowledging defeat, or losing — hence it is "often viewed negatively." A siddh guru equips his disciple to think differently.

Surrender in the guru-disciple relationship means surrendering at all three levels of existence — physical, mental, and spiritual — to the guru's guidance. It implies **flexibility to re-examine one's attitude, philosophy, and lifestyle** and thereby accept change. It begins with unlearning: "There is much to eradicate, undo and cleanse, before rewriting and redoing."

> [!warning] Misuse of surrender is documented. "By selling the idea of surrender, many professed spiritualists have exploited and enslaved their disciples, promising them upliftment." Hingori acknowledges the abuse but insists the misuse does not denigrate the pristine concept.

## Core Teaching

### The Three-Fold Framework: Tann, Mann, Dhann

Surrender to the guru entails the renunciation of **tann** (body), **dhann** (wealth), and **mann** (the conscious mind of both body and spirit).

#### Surrender of Tann (Body)

The disciple surrenders physical time and effort to the guru's diktats — kriyas, yoga, pranayam, fasting, asanas, mantra recitation, seva, visiting spiritual places.

Hingori's personal testimony: after his first visit to Gurudev in 1977 for arthritis relief, Gurudev gave him a set of Thursday restrictions, which Hingori took lightly. The arthritis recurred. Five years later, Gurudev prescribed seva at the Gurgaon sthan. One day, Gurudev saw Hingori doing seva despite agonising pain. He stared at Hingori for a few seconds in a strange manner. The next morning, Hingori was pain-free — for 35 years running. See [[stories/hingori-arthritis-cure]].

The sthan sustained dozens of such examples: ladies who cooked, cleaned, and swept for decades; followers who visited hospitals at odd hours. "This surrender of tann has rewarded them with gifts of spiritual progress and power." **Lal Saheb**, a disciple in Canada, cooked prasad and helped heal visitors for more than two decades. "Surrender to the guru and his mission made a saint out of him."

Gurudev also insisted Hingori remain a householder and earn his keep. When Hingori grew disenchanted with his family business, Gurudev instructed him to attend work for a few hours a day — even if only to read a novel in office. See [[stories/hingori-family-business-seva]].

#### Surrender of Dhann (Wealth)

Greed is hard to overcome; family objections can compound it further. Hingori documents two pivotal episodes:

- Gurudev insisted on air travel Mumbai→Gurgaon for every bada guruvar, despite the expense. Following this instruction over Hingori's objections, Gurudev gifted him "some of the divine symbols he possessed — the value of that is immeasurable." See [[stories/hingori-dhann-airfare]].
- When Hingori declined to fly to a Hyderabad wedding because he could not afford it, his wallet disappeared. Found the INR 6,000 back in his blazer at home in Mumbai. See [[stories/hingori-wallet-lost-found]].

> Golden Principle: Money spent on oneself is a debt, while that spent on charity is seva.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.9, p.83

**Roshni**, a graphic designer, has contributed years of digital seva — time and money — for the sthan's outreach work. Hingori finds it difficult to refuse her participation precisely because her seva is genuine.

Surrendering wealth to the guru does not mean financing his luxuries. "Buying him a Rolls Royce is not surrender of wealth, unless you have chosen a guru who prefers life's luxuries to simplicities. But then, most siddh gurus are certainly not that." Gurudev lived in a 250-square-yard property in a middle-class sector of Gurgaon; his wife, a government school teacher, knitted his woollens. When affluent disciples offered large contributions, he refused and "sent them packing." See [[stories/guru-sutra-ch9-kabir-poem]] for Kabir's verse on this distinction.

#### Surrender of the Spiritual Self

The guru-disciple relationship can go beyond a single lifetime. At Hingori's first meeting, Gurudev asked, "So, you've come?" — a greeting that only made sense decades later.

During Hingori's formative years, Gurudev would use disciples' energies in his healing work. He would meditate on them and channel their energies to help patients. See [[stories/hingori-seva-spirit-availability]].

> "Son, all I want you to do is be around, so I may use you for doing what I am doing, when I need to."
> — Gurudev, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.9, p.85

The disciple's spirit understands that its best interests are served at the instance of the guru, not itself. Material distractions — fancy cars, prestigious jobs, social status — "play no role in the evolution of the spirit and are certainly not its idea of wealth." The disciple's spirit is more loyal to the spirit of the guru than to its own. "Thus surrender is the disciple's best option for self-advancement."

#### Surrender of Mann (Mind)

The mind of the physical body and the mind of the spirit body both need to be surrendered. They can be considered "fragments of the same mind."

The mind resists because it trusts its own logic. But the guru knows incomparably more on the subject of spiritualism: "our queries and dissents, are at best baby talk." See [[stories/hingori-mind-training-non-duality]] for Gurudev's four-year programme of systematically dismantling Hingori's mind and rebuilding it.

Key techniques Gurudev used for mind-cleansing:
- Four years of no laughter, no joy, no reaction to good news — training the mind toward equanimity
- Fasting, giving up alcohol and non-vegetarian food
- Avoiding namak (salt) and ann (cereals) paid for by others — battling temptation and avoiding obligations

> "There are things that your mind will never be able to understand or analyse. Always remember to do whatever you are asked without questioning it."
> — Gurudev, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.9, p.88

> Golden Principle: The guru is always right.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.9, p.89

> "A beautiful line in the Guru Gita states 'Mantra mulam guru vakyam' (the root of mantra is the word of the guru), which means, whatever the guru speaks is a priceless mantra."
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.9, p.89

## Nuances

### Surrender Produces Dignity, Not Subservience

Surrender to the guru does NOT mean subservience to anyone. After the period of mind-demolition, Gurudev placed his hand on Hingori's head and told him "not to be dominated by anyone again." He could be respectful and show humility but "never subservience." See [[stories/hingori-chira-self-esteem]].

This is the paradox of surrender: the ego must be made permeable to the guru's influence — "in complete surrender, the mind ceases to exist at an individual level and begins to exist only in relation to the guru" — but this ultimately produces a disciple who is more, not less, capable of standing upright.

### Surrender Enables Synchronicity

When the guru and disciple minds are synchronised, intuitive communication flows in both directions. Hingori cites three examples: Anjali (who had already written the exact sentences Hingori was about to dictate); Kapill and Ridhim (who independently arrived at treatments Hingori was simultaneously thinking). See [[entities/kapill]].

This is the practical goal of surrendering the mind — not dependence, but synchronisation with a more evolved consciousness.

### The Guru Is Not a Crutch

Surrender is misunderstood when the disciple uses the guru as a crutch rather than a launch pad. Hingori found it "disappointing to watch people ask Gurudev silly questions like, which car or television to buy." Begging gurus to attend social functions, inaugurate business ventures, or resolve domestic matters mistakes the guru's role.

> "A guru is the remover of ignorance and the one who bestows internal knowledge. Therefore, one should depend on such a person for spiritual support and advancement, rather than as a crutch."
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.9, pp.93–94

> [!tip] The key distinction is between dependence for spiritual direction (the guru's proper domain) and dependence for material decisions (a category error that reduces a spiritual instrument to an oracle for trivialities).

## In Practice

- Surrender tann: commit physical time and effort to the guru's prescribed kriyas and seva, even when you cannot see the correlation
- Surrender dhann: follow financial instructions without resistance; the return is "an earning for eternity"
- Surrender the spiritual self: make yourself available — be physically present and mentally empty — so the guru can use your energy in his work
- Surrender mann: allow the guru to use his eraser on your prior conditioning; do not cling to old attitudes while the process of rewriting is underway
- When criticised, celebrate it (distribute sweetmeats) — by criticising you, someone reduces your shortcomings; a false critic incurs debt to you
