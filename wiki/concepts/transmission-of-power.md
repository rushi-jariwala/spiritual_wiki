# Transmission of Power

## Definition

**Power**, in this context, is the ability to do things uncommonly — attained through spiritual learning and practice, usable for the benefit of self and others. It goes beyond what is commonly used for existence and sustenance. A guru spends a major part of his life acquiring it; a disciple receives a distilled form through the guru's grace.

> Power, as referred here, is the ability to do things uncommonly.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.127

There are several distinct types of power a disciple can acquire through the grace of a guru.

---

## The Power of Knowledge

A guru distils decades of study, contemplation, and experience into transmissible wisdom. The disciple receives not raw scripture but pre-digested understanding. In rare cases like Gurudev's, this knowledge was **intuitive** — sourced from the third eye and inner being rather than books:

> Since Gurudev was neither inclined towards reading books or scriptures nor attending spiritual courses, he initially learnt what he did from his advisor, Sitaramji of Dasua. However, on the Mahaguru awakening in him, his answers came from Buddhe Baba and several symbols of Shiv appeared on his palms. His intuition worked most of the time.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.128

Gurudev actively discouraged his disciples from reading religious or spiritual books. He believed they should work toward tapping the knowledge lying dormant within their own unconscious minds. A *siddh guru* can awaken the power of knowledge or intuition in disciples by will.

*Anecdote:* A stranger — apparently an evolved celestial being — told Hingori he was "fortunate to get pre-cooked bread and did not have to bake his own." The stranger had appeared first as a strawberry seller who claimed to have seen Hingori in his dream, and blessed him with immense wealth. See [[stories/hingori-strawberry-seller]].

---

## The Power of Spiritual Energy (Shakti)

Gurudev's approach to spiritualism was **power-based** — unprecedented in the modern era. He had attained innumerable powers manifesting as symbols on his hands, feet, and body, and could share them with disciples. Along with the symbols came the capacity to use their power.

Methods of energy transmission Gurudev employed:

### Via Willpower (Direct Transfer)
Most disciples received spiritual energy purely through Gurudev's willpower — no elaborate ceremonies. He would make disciples practice mantras for years before bestowing *shakti*. Some (like Santoshji) were recruited spontaneously. See [[stories/santoshji-kathok-jal]].

### Via Worn Clothing (Aura Mingling)
Gurudev gave disciples his worn clothes to wear, so his residual aura would mingle with theirs.

> During an annual Mahashivratri event, Gurudev had given me his worn clothes, and I wore them for four days without bathing, so I would not lose even 1% of his aura.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.114

### Via Used Plate (Eye-Transmitted Aura)
Disciples were asked to eat from Gurudev's plate after he finished his meal. The used plate carried aura transmitted by his eyes.

### Via Object Exchange
Gurudev once asked Hingori for the worn belt he was wearing — not to use it, but to mingle their auras. He wore it the entire day.

> To him, it was a way in which he would allow our auras to mingle and get connected. It was his philosophy that his disciples and he were one.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.133

### Via Visualisation of the Disciple
Gurudev would treat patients on behalf of a disciple by **visualising the disciple performing the healing**. This multiplied his presence across locations — from one, to many.

> He the 'server', had all of us — the 'laptops' — connected, and hence power became shareable. Naturally, he has access to all the power of every connected device.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.151

## Power by Attainment of Siddhis

During sadhana, some gurus seek *siddhis* — control over the power of certain mantras, yantras, and tantric practices. Siddhis range from walking on water (*Khawaja Ki Siddhi*), materialising objects, curing diseases, and becoming invisible, to minor powers like gaining control over a single plant's energy.

Gurudev considered most siddhis **tamasic** and never introduced his disciples to them. Instead, he directed disciples toward sattvic practices: seva, tapasya, and mantra vidya. For the taxonomy of mantra siddhis specifically, see [[concepts/tantra]].

---

## Power by Attainment of Mantra Siddhis

When a mantra has been recited in the prescribed manner with the requisite number of repetitions, its power becomes the practitioner's to use at will. Hingori distinguishes three types:

### Tamasic Mantra Siddhis
Practiced by negative tantric vidya practitioners — black magicians, those who perform miracles using spirit forces. Often performed in graveyards to leverage available energy. See [[concepts/spiritual-attacks]] and [[stories/aghori-disciple-dead-body]].

*Example:* A contractor had attained the siddhi of making appliances malfunction. He cast a spell on the water pump at Gurudev's farm. Chacha neutralised it with mantra-charged jal. See [[entities/chacha]], [[stories/chacha-tantriks]].

### Rajasic Mantra Siddhis
Aimed at benefiting the aspirant or others for personal material gains — wealth expansion, self-protection. The Diwali Laxmi mantra is a common example. When done correctly, it provides *barkat* (the luck to make money last longer).

### Sattvic Mantra Siddhis
Attaining the highest levels of power through mantras and intense meditation. Gurudev, Guru Nanak Dev, Kabir, and Shirdi Sai Baba all attained the symbol of **Om** on their hands. Gurudev passed it to at least 100 disciples, who passed it further. Attainments like the trishul, jyot, shivling, gileri, Ganpati, Nandi fall in this category.

*Example:* Sharmaji received the jyot in full view of all visitors at the first sthan in Mumbai — a flame entered from the window, traversed the room, and lodged itself on his left hand for the rest of his life. Witnessed by at least 40 people. (See [[concepts/power-symbols]].)

Notable sattvic mantras: Gayatri, Mahagayatri, Mahamritunjay, Sanjivini, Mahaguru.

> An insight Gurudev specifically ingrained in us was that power is not a tool to be used for the benefit of family and friends, or ever misused. However, the use of power for self-protection and sustenance is justifiable.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.138

## Misuse of Power

> "Power corrupts, absolute power corrupts absolutely."
> — Lord Acton (British politician), quoted in *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.143

Power corrupts primarily due to insecurities and lack of confidence. Wielding power over others creates a sense of significance — virtually a state of intoxication — that often leads to misuse.

Hingori confesses to several early experiments with misuse. See [[stories/hingori-rain-mantra]] and [[stories/malhotra-power-misuse]] for documented cases. Gurudev reprimanded disciples who misused powers — paying for damages caused, and threatening withdrawal of power. His foundational teaching: the power bestowed must be respected above all else.

> In the initial days, many of us have amateurly used the power Gurudev vested in us. However, over the years, the foundation of Gurudev's teachings has provided us with checks and balances to prevent such misuse.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.145

---

## Theft of Power

Power exists everywhere and accumulates at specific locations — busy crossroads, forests, gymnasiums, cowsheds, and places of worship. Some spiritualists resort to **stealing power** by magnetising it from locations and people.

> I have experienced that power attained this way does not give the receiver long-term benefits. It either depletes itself through wastage and misuse, or gets magnetised by someone with a stronger energy field.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, p.146

This connects to [[stories/hingori-renuka-temple-attack]], where the lesson was explicitly stated: "energy [is] better earned than stolen."
