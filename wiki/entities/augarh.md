# Augarh

> *"A phantom in the garb of a human / manifesting and multiplying at will. / Eyes bright as rubies, an intoxicating swagger, / he appears wherever he pleases still."*
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.51

## Who / What

**Augarh** is Gurudev's mysterious disciple — the head of the **Aghor sect** — whose spiritual domain spans **Haridwar and its adjoining areas**. He is described as a **realised manifestation of the singular form of Shiv (Shiv minus Shakti)** with the following capacities:

- Manifesting in various human forms at will
- Appearing in multiple locations simultaneously
- Communicating telepathically
- Disappearing instantaneously and without trace

While Augarh was a disciple of Gurudev, **his own followers are not Gurudev's disciples** — a distinct boundary noted explicitly by Hingori.

## Role in the Lineage

Gurudev referred to Augarh as disciples' **elder brother (gurubhai)**, to whom they could turn for help in spiritual matters:

> Gurudev referred to Augarh as his disciples' elder brother, to whom they could turn for help in spiritual matters.
> — paraphrased from Ravi Trehan ji's account, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.52

Many of Gurudev's disciples received his support when they requested it. **Shobha ji** (Surender Taneja ji's wife) used Augarh as a "personal genie" to grant mundane wishes. However, some disciples could not connect with the abstractness of his unmanifested form and thus could not benefit from his spiritual might.

## Physical Manifestation

When Augarh appeared in human form, Hingori describes him as:
- Dressed in white (kurta-pyjama and turban, or covered in a white sheet)
- Grey-haired
- With **large red eyes** and a penetrating gaze
- A **confident, drunken swagger** in his gait
- Capable of chanting mantras — including the exact mantra being recited mentally by another person

He met Gurudev in human form on visits to Haridwar. Encounters at other locations were often unannounced and of short duration — he would appear, fulfil the spiritual purpose, and vanish.

## Notable Encounters

- **Har ki Pauri (Haridwar)** — bent to touch Gurudev's feet at aarti; disappeared with Gurudev into the crowd; touched Mataji's feet as a mark of respect without her awareness. See [[stories/augarh-har-ki-pauri]].
- **Laxman Jhoola (Rishikesh)** — appeared as a white-sheet-clad man on a bench at a ghat; stared at Shobha ji with red eyes; simultaneously placed hands on Surender ji's son's feet as Gurudev placed hands on the boy's head; vanished before Surender ji emerged from the Ganges. See [[stories/augarh-laxman-jhoola]].
- **Mataji's hometown** — appeared to Hingori as a drunk and dishevelled man with a bicycle, chanting the exact mantra Hingori was reciting mentally; circled the house all night guarding where Mataji was staying. See [[stories/hingori-augarh-mataji-town]].
- **Asthal, Dehradun** — appeared in response to Hingori's mental invitation; drank no refreshments; stated he was "there to honour an invitation"; younger disciples followed him out and found he had vanished. See [[stories/augarh-laxman-jhoola]] and [[stories/hingori-augarh-tea]].
- **Telepathic tea guidance** — guided Hingori step by step via thought-waves to brew perfect tea for a late-night guest, while Hingori was alone in Gurgaon and Augarh was physically absent. See [[stories/hingori-augarh-tea]].
- **Mr Bagga's radio shop (Hamidpur)** — appeared in Hamidpur and pestered Mr and Mrs Bagga at their radio shop with no intention of buying, driving them to furious Punjabi cuss words. When Mr Bagga next arrived in Gurgaon, Gurudev greeted him with a sarcastic replay of every word. See [[stories/augarh-bagga-radio-shop]].
- **Darius's forearm** — appeared at Darius's car window while he drove, drew a symbol on his forearm, and addressed him tauntingly as "So, Mr Lawyer...!" despite Darius not being in court attire. Vanished laughing. See [[stories/augarh-darius-forearm]].

## *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.8 — "Cosmic Collaborators" (pp.108–115)

"Gurudev kept many secrets close to his chest, one of which was the being known as Augarh" (p.108).

**Origin in the Shiv Puran.** FC Sharma ji: "There is a mention of Augarh in the Shiv Puran. Once, when Lord Shankar lost his divine energies, he transformed into Augarh and lived in graveyards to regain his powers. To my understanding, Augarh lives and performs his rituals on cremation grounds, surviving on whatever he finds there. He is a manifestation of Shiv" (p.108). Believed to be the leader of the Aghori sect, he "once roamed the areas around Haridwar in human form before departing the earthly realm years ago" (p.109).

**Gurudev's disappearances at Haridwar.** FC Sharma ji: "Gurudev would frequently vanish for hours on these trips. Upon returning, when pressed about where he had been, he would explain, 'I went to meet Augarh'. We would protest, 'But we did not see or meet him'. Gurudev would answer, 'What would you see? Augarh is six and a half feet tall, has red eyes, and mostly remains in the cremation ground'" (p.109).

**Iconography.** Imagery "shows him with a beard, an intense expression, clutching a trishul in his **left** hand, contrasting to the Grihasth Shiv, who holds it in his right." Advanced Aghoris strive to embody his attributes, but "while Augarh is a disciple of Gurudev, not all Aghoris are followers of the mahaguru" (p.109). See [[concepts/aghor]].

**Encounters by family.** Few have met him directly — "among them Gurudev's wife, daughter, sister, and some of his disciples." Renu ji: "I knew Augarh protected the sthan when Daddy was away, but I had never seen him. Then, one morning, I did. He was incredibly tall—so tall that I could not see his face. His presence was overwhelming and terrifying. When Daddy returned from his trip, I told him what I had witnessed, and he confirmed that it was Augarh" (p.109).

**Hingori at Asthal (Ch.8 version).** "I have encountered Augarh several times, with the most recent meeting at Asthal. Responding to a mental invitation I had sent, Augarh entered the temple grounds in the garb of a villager with a noticeable swagger resembling a drunken gait. I was engaged in seva at the sthan at that moment. As he paused outside, our eyes met, rendering any formal introductions redundant" (pp.109–111).

**Gurubhai and brother.** FC Sharma ji: "Gurudev introduced Augarh to us as his disciple, positioning him as our gurubhai." Hingori: "Gurudev also assured me that Augarh was like a brother to me, someone I could call upon whenever needed—and I have. He even guided me in brewing the perfect kettle of tea!" ([[stories/hingori-augarh-tea]]). Hingori has "encouraged many of my disciples to deepen their connection with this powerful and gracious energy" (p.111) — see [[stories/geeta-nagpal-augarh-bhaiya]].

**Guardian, informant, tester.** "He could change his form, read thoughts, present spiritual challenges, and relay insights back to Gurudev." FC Sharma ji: "Augarh kept an eye on us and reported our actions to Gurudev, who would often say, 'Augarh tells me everything'" (p.112). Hingori: "I am convinced Augarh was Gurudev's personal test-o-meter… His divine naughtiness is off the charts!" (p.113). He "continues to test the third and fourth generation" — see [[stories/humsa-augarh-tests]], [[stories/augarh-bagga-radio-shop]] and [[synthesis/tests-in-disguise]].

> [!warning] Descriptions of Augarh vary
> *The Guru of Gurus* describes him as grey-haired, dressed in white, with a drunken swagger. *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.8 gives six and a half feet tall with red eyes (Gurudev via FC Sharma ji); "so tall that I could not see his face" (Renu ji); a beard and trishul in the left hand (imagery; Geeta Nagpal ji's vision); and in disguise a thin unkempt man, a wheatish curly-haired beggar "of modest height" (Humsa ji), or a clean beggar with a white sack. The shape-shifting capacity the sources attribute to him accounts for this; flagged for completeness.

> [!question] "Shiv minus Shakti" vs. "Shankar who lost his energies"
> *The Guru of Gurus* calls Augarh "the singular form of Shiv (Shiv minus Shakti)"; FC Sharma ji's Shiv Puran account has Shankar *become* Augarh to regain lost energies. The two readings may be the same idea from different angles; the sources do not connect them.
