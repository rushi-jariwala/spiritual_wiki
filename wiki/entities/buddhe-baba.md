# Buddhe Baba

> *Gurudev's mysterious spiritual mentor. Omnipresent, identity undisclosed. "The teacher had a teacher, someone old, wise and true. He called him Buddhe Baba and despite countless excavations, that was all we ever knew!"*

## Who / What

**Buddhe Baba** is the enigmatic spiritual guide whom Gurudev described as "an omnipresent part of his and our lives." Gurudev was always quick to attribute his work entirely to Buddhe Baba:

> He was not the doer and all credit for his seva belonged entirely to his spiritual mentor.
> — paraphrased from Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, pp.47–48

Gurudev's signature attribution was simply: **"He does. I don't."** — the most compressed expression of [[concepts/non-doership]] in the tradition, applied by the mahaguru to his own relationship with his guide.

When disciples asked intensely profound questions, Gurudev would sometimes say he would need to "confirm with Buddhe Baba" before answering — treating the mentor as a living, accessible oracle even after his own advent as mahaguru.

He never revealed Buddhe Baba's identity, leaving room for conjecture. **That conjecture was never settled.**

## Gurudev's Multiple Teachers — and Why Buddhe Baba Held the Reins

Gurudev was not spiritually self-taught, nor did he have only one guide. Hingori traces the progression:

> "From the nurturing guidance of sadhus and fakirs during his formative years to the spiritual teachings of Sitaram ji of Dasua and Sitaram ji of Benaras in his adolescence, and later, the wisdom imparted by Buddhe Baba in adulthood — each played an important role in the mahaguru's spiritual ascent. All were teachers, essential to his journey, yet in his destiny's grand design, only one held the reins to his aatma's odyssey — Buddhe Baba."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, p.26

The analogy invoked is that of school teachers at different levels — a student has different teachers for different stages, but only one mentor shapes the ultimate destiny. See [[wiki/analogies/analogies]] — *School Teachers at Different Levels*.

## First Meeting

Sometime before Gurudev gave up his siddhis in 1970, he was posted to Madhya Pradesh for a soil survey camp. Taking a long walk, he stumbled upon a deserted temple where a sage was in deep meditation. The exchange of a pinch of tobacco began a chain of events that changed everything. See [[stories/buddhe-baba-deserted-temple]].

The commission Buddhe Baba gave at that first meeting — delivered both in vision and in the appearance at the camp's next day — was:

> "You do not need to seek me physically. I will always be present during your paath to guide you. But remember, you must do nisvarth seva. Whoever you touch will be healed, and I will guide you every step of the way."
> — Buddhe Baba to Gurudev, narrated by Suresh Kohli ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, pp.22–23

## Siddhi Discharge and Transformation

Under Buddhe Baba's guidance, Gurudev first mastered many siddhis under Sitaram ji of Dasua. Buddhe Baba, seeing the mahaguru's ultimate destiny, urged him to transcend these preliminary achievements and relinquish the siddhis:

> He urged Gurudev to strive for the ultimate state of spiritual enlightenment, where his initial abilities would seem minor in comparison. Following this advice, Gurudev relinquished his siddhis into the Ganges at Har ki Pauri in Haridwar.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, p.21

See [[stories/gurudev-siddhi-discharge]].

## Gurudev's Perpetual Consciousness of His Presence

Buddhe Baba was an unseen force, yet Gurudev remained perpetually conscious of his presence. Several disciples testify to this:

**FC Sharma ji** (one of Gurudev's humblest disciples): Gurudev directed those in need to the sthan in Sector 7, Gurgaon, saying, "Place the card at the sthan, and ensure Buddhe Baba is invited first." Even wedding invitations were to be placed before Buddhe Baba before coming to Gurudev.

**Ravi Trehan ji** (who manages a sthan in Kirti Nagar, New Delhi): "Whenever individuals approached him with their troubles, he would gently advise, 'Visit the sthan, offer your respects to Buddhe Baba, and confide in him. He will address your concerns.'"

**Rajpal Sekhri ji** (disciple): At major events such as Guru Purnima and Mahashivratri, visitors typically presented one garland at the sthan and another to Gurudev. At one Mahashivratri, Rajpal ji observed Gurudev accepting garlands from certain devotees while gently declining from others. Hours later, he asked why. Gurudev paused mid-blessing and explained:

> "I have to consider my guru, and I certainly cannot wear more garlands than my guru does."
> — Gurudev to Rajpal Sekhri ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, p.24

**Santlal ji** (disciple from Sonepat): "Gurudev once said that during seva, Shiv would appear and sit beside him and guide him." Hingori reads a double significance: Gurudev likely used *Shiv* as a synonym for Buddhe Baba; and Buddhe Baba's presence was perceptible only to the mahaguru.

**Ashok Bhalla ji** (devotee): During a private conversation, Gurudev gazed at a picture of Lord Shankar with a white beard above his bed and said: *"My maalik controls life and death."*

**Alka ji** (Gurudev's youngest daughter): "Daddy would often point to a picture and say, 'This is Buddhe Baba'. That image showed Lord Shankar with white hair and a flowing white beard."

## Appearances

The mystery of Buddhe Baba endured throughout Gurudev's eighteen years as mahaguru and continues to this day. A common thread across all sightings: **an elderly, slim, tall figure dressed in white with white hair and a white beard.**

- **Gurudev's younger sister Rashmi ji** — weeping in his empty bedroom after misinterpreting his words as a farewell, she sees a luminous white light reveal an older man in white with long flowing white hair. He consoles her, explains Gurudev is going on a soil survey tour, and fades. See [[stories/rashmi-buddhe-baba-grief-vision]].

- **Mataji** — Gurudev was overheard in the middle of the night conversing in a low voice with someone he addressed as "Baba." He ended the conversation abruptly when Buddhe Baba informed him that Mataji was overhearing them.

- **Renu ji (Gurudev's eldest daughter)** — home alone and sick with fever at their home in Shivpuri, Gurgaon, she sees a white-clad man with long white hair emerge from the storeroom. Terrified, she covers herself. Mataji, upon returning, identifies him: "That was Buddhe Baba. He watches over this sthan and all of us." See [[stories/renu-buddhe-baba-childhood-encounter]].

- **Puran ji** (in dream state) — saw Buddhe Baba as a towering, robust figure in pure white, with a mane of white hair and a distinguished white beard, distributing grain to the needy.

- **Mrs Bhalla** (Ashok ji's wife) — near Gurudev's samadhi at Najafgarh in Delhi after his departure from the physical realm: "He had a towering presence, emerging almost as a translucent figure before my eyes. Dressed in a tehmat, revealing a slender yet strong physique, he moved swiftly and gracefully." Malhotra ji later confirmed the sighting.

- **RC Malhotra ji** (in an astral meeting with Gurudev) — encountered Buddhe Baba, who struck him with a yog dand for expressing unwelcome opinions. Malhotra ji woke to find real pain and a physical imprint on his back. See [[stories/malhotra-buddhe-baba-yog-dand]].

- **Hingori** (in a powerful vision) — Gurudev guides him to a riverbank where a divine being wearing a green toga with a square-shaped face stands on a moored raft. Gurudev reverently offers the only seat to this figure; the raft glides in silence as Hingori feels he is being introduced to someone of a higher spiritual order. See [[stories/hingori-raft-vision-buddhe-baba]].

## Identity — Three Theories, No Resolution

Only a select few — most notably Gurudev himself — knew Buddhe Baba's true identity. Three theories circulate among disciples:

**Theory 1 — A manifestation of Shiv**
Gurudev's own hints consistently pointed here. When gazing at a picture of Lord Shankar with a white beard, he said, "My maalik controls life and death." Alka ji confirms he identified the same image as "Buddhe Baba." FC Sharma ji believes he "might be a manifestation of Shiv or Mahashiv." Consistent with the high calibre of guidance provided and the attribute-set (omnipresence, withdrawal from identity, authority over life and death).

**Theory 2 — The Shiv roop of Sitaram ji of Benaras**
Raji Sharma ji (disciple) articulates a nuanced position: Sitaram ji of Benaras, the guru of Gurudev's early mentor Sitaram ji of Dasua, was an exceptionally evolved saint who departed *seh sharir* — his physical body dematerialised entirely, a feat achieved by only a handful of spiritualists including Jesus and Saint Kabir. Raji Sharma ji explains:

> "Our par dada guru is one with Shiv. Gurudev often posed the question: What truly is Shiv? Is Shiv simply the figure perched on a mountain peak with a trident in hand and matted hair? In reality, Shiv symbolises Brahman consciousness or cosmic consciousness — this is the true essence of Shiv. This cosmic consciousness is what our par dada guru embodies."
> — Raji Sharma ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, p.27

On this reading, theories 1 and 2 do not contradict each other: to be the Shiv roop of Sitaram ji of Benaras *is* to be a manifestation of Shiv.

**Theory 3 — Gurudev's own enlightened jivaatma**
Chachi ji (Gurudev's sister-in-law) proposes the most unusual reading: "Gurudev was Shiv himself, and he referred to his evolved form as 'Buddhe Baba'." On this account, Buddhe Baba is not an external guide at all but Gurudev's own higher consciousness personified.

> [!question] The three theories are not mutually exclusive in the metaphysics of this tradition — where individual consciousness, cosmic consciousness, and the Shiv principle fold into one another at the highest levels. Gurudev's deliberate silence is itself a teaching: some questions are meant to remain open.

## Anonymity as Spiritual Strategy

The chapter closes with Hingori's analysis of why Buddhe Baba remained deliberately unknown:

> "Buddhe Baba chose anonymity as a strategic means to sidestep the constraints and diversions associated with a public identity. He understood that being known could undermine his deeper spiritual objectives, specifically his role in developing Gurudev into a mahaguru and guiding his spiritual growth. Selfless service without attachment to one's own identity exemplifies true seva."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, pp.31–32

The closing saying attributed to Buddhe Baba's principle: *"Hum yahaan apna astitva mitaane aaye hain, banaane nahin"* — we are here to erase our identity, not to construct it. See [[concepts/non-doership]] and [[concepts/guru-vandana]] (fame vs. anonymity section).
