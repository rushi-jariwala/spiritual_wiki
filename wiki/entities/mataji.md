# Mataji (Sudesh Sharma)

> *Gurudev's wife. Called Mataji — "mother" — by his disciples. The Shakti to his Shiv.*

> "The queen he wed became his pillar of strength.
> The Shakti to his Shiv.
> She took on the mantle of mother to his clan,
> serving selflessly as long as she lived."
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.26

## Who

**Sudesh Sharma** married Gurudev in **1960**. She was twenty years old, from a well-respected family in **Bilga, near Ludhiana, Punjab**. Her father was a coal trader who died when she was a child. She was the youngest of seven siblings — her oldest brother a well-known freedom fighter and member of the Indian National Congress; another brother, **Rudra ji**, committed himself to teaching.

It was Rudra ji who had the first encounter with his future brother-in-law in 1960. Reflecting on their initial meeting, he recalls:

> "From the first moment, his striking appearance was undeniable. Our conversation aimed to understand his thoughts, character, and sentiments, allowing me to form impressions of his views on life. In an era when letters bridged distances instead of telephones, I wrote to my family after our discussion. In that letter, I conveyed my impression of him as a kind-hearted individual and an ideal match for my sister."
> — Rudra ji (Mataji's brother), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.75

Years after her marriage she became known reverently as **Mataji** (mother) among Gurudev's disciples. She worked as a **schoolteacher**, contributing her salary to the running of the household alongside the money Gurudev gave her.

## Marriage

Shortly after their wedding, Mataji found Gurudev lying motionless on the bed — not breathing. She frantically tried to wake him, shaking his hands, feet, and body, but there was no reaction. In a panic, she called her sister-in-law, who calmly explained: *"There's no need to be anxious; he is in paath."* Mataji recalled this first encounter with her husband's spiritual side with laughter, confessing she had no idea what *paath* meant at the time.

Shortly after his marriage, Gurudev left Mataji under the care of her parents to embark on an intense journey of meditation and spiritual practices, temporarily setting aside his worldly duties. A few years later, while meditating near the **Santoksar Lake in Amritsar**, he heard an **akashvani** (celestial voice) conveying that his spiritual quest could only reach completion if he returned to his wife and embraced the life of a householder. This enigmatic message — possibly orchestrated by his then-future guru, [[entities/buddhe-baba|Buddhe Baba]] — played a significant role in reshaping his path. Heeding it, he reunited with Mataji and settled into the life of a householder — a teaching he later formalised as [[concepts/grihasth-ashram]].

Gurudev affectionately addressed his wife as **"Master"** (because she worked as a schoolteacher). Before his advent as a mahaguru, their lives were filled with simple pleasures: movie nights, exploring new places together, and everyday joys. When he told her, *"Master, wait and see what I become at thirty-five!"* she assumed he meant a promotion and a salary increment. She did not know he was referring to the age at which he would catapult from spiritual seeker to mahaguru.

### Mataji's Own Spiritual Path

Mataji's spiritual journey was entirely self-directed — Gurudev never tried to steer her path. She felt a deep affinity for the **Gayatri mantra** and shared her experiences with Gurudev, who acknowledged the mantra's significance. As her commitment deepened, he eventually shared the more powerful **Mahagayatri mantra** with her — a gradual unveiling that reflected his thoughtful and measured approach to spiritual transmission.

## Family Life — Raising Children as a Mahaguru's Wife

Gurudev frequently stated that his five children embodied the reincarnated spirits of his ancestors and spiritual connections. He ensured they received a proper upbringing and education, fulfilling his familial responsibilities and discharging his karmic debts. He believed and often stated that **women are manifestations of Shakti** — and in his own family, there was no differentiation between sons and daughters.

**Nitu ji — the reincarnation of Gurudev's father:** Gurudev had confided in Mataji that his father would return as their son in time for the wedding of Gurudev's younger brother (Chacha ji). Astonishingly, Nitu ji was born just one week before the scheduled wedding festivities. Gurudev addressed Nitu ji as 'Pandit ji' — the same honorific his own father had borne. See [[stories/nitu-bichoo-booti]] for the Himachal Pradesh incident in which Gurudev taught him: *"Son, nature has a cure for everything — all you have to know is where to look for it."*

**Gurudev as "strict" Daddy — an act of love:** Renu ji, the eldest daughter, recalls that her father was perceived as strict during childhood — so much so that the children feared bothering Mummy lest she complain to him. Only in adulthood did they realise the strictness was a calculated act: Gurudev was away on tour for months at a time, and by projecting sternness, he kept the children from going astray. *"He was, in fact, a simple and uncomplicated man with no preconceived notions about anything."*

**Renu ji's school reluctance:** When Renu ji hesitated to start school, Gurudev — returning from a tour — gently asked why she was being pushed to go against her will. Rather than insisting on formal schooling, he let her spend time at Mataji's school informally, absorbing nursery rhymes and alphabets at her own pace. When she eagerly showed him what she had learnt, he simply asked: *"Are you ready for school now?"* Renu ji responded with eagerness.

**The "true love" confrontation:** As Gurudev's following grew, Renu ji experienced increasing distance from her father — stretches of ten to fifteen days or even a full month without exchanging words. One day she mustered the courage to tell him directly: *"You do not have time for us. Mummy is everything to us."* He locked his gaze on her for two minutes, then said:

> "I do not know how to convey affection like your mother. One day you will realise, and then you will understand what true love is."
> — Gurudev to Renu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.80

Renu ji admits: *"It was only in the aftermath of his passing that I began to grasp the depth of his concept of love. I now understand what guru kripa is, and how his influence encompasses all."*

**Vacation promises:** When Gurudev was in a good mood, he would say: *"Where shall we travel next?"* These words were meant to delight his children. Yet the reality of his limited availability was inescapable — the trips often did not materialise. Renu ji, as the eldest, felt this absence most acutely.

**Gurudev names all the children — and the farm animals:** When Renu ji challenged Gurudev to name all his children (doubting he could), he effortlessly recited their given names and classes. But he also knew by name every cow and buffalo in the shed at the Khandsa farm — and they responded to him. A jet-black buffalo in the corner rumbled in acknowledgement when he called her name. During phone calls from America, Chacha ji would update Gurudev on the farm animals at such length that the children had to demand the receiver back to speak with their parents. *"He genuinely loved animals. He cared for their well-being even when he was miles away."* (Ila ji)

**Gurudev cooks:** When Mataji humorously suggested one day that Gurudev cook for himself, he accepted the challenge and prepared a delicious vegetable dish — using a week's supply of tomatoes in the process. He then repeatedly asked each family member whether they had enjoyed it. Mummy had to remind him that it had to be tasty when he used that much. (Ila ji)

**Learning to heal yourself:** Gurudev occasionally blessed cardamoms and cloves for Alka ji when she was ill as a child, but he also told her: *"Learn to heal yourself."* Years after his passing, when Alka ji experienced severe pain in her arm, his words rang in her ears. She focused on the arm for a minute or two while chanting her mantras. The pain subsided — and when it did not return after fifteen days, she understood: *"If we have self-belief, we can heal ourselves."*

**Respect for food / Barkat:** He treated food with deep respect. The family's unwritten rule was that no food be left unfinished. On one occasion, he discovered a half-eaten paratha in the kitchen sink — left by an unknown disciple during a gathering. He picked it up and ate it himself before Indu didi could react. Then he addressed everyone: *"Learn to respect food. This is barkat — those who have it, waste it. Ask someone less fortunate what a square meal is worth. We must be grateful for what we have."* See [[stories/gurudev-barkat-wasted-food]].

**Feeding visitors:** He generously added heaps of white butter to parathas served to visitors at the sthan, ensuring every guest felt welcome. When organising camps during tours, he chose cooks not for their skill but for their need — giving financial help to those who needed it. *"He tried to help others however he could."* (Ila ji)

**The drawing room becomes a dormitory:** When Renu ji requested a drawing room to be added to the house, Gurudev initially resisted. Eventually he agreed, adding an entire floor — but the moment he surveyed the completed room, he said: *"It is perfect, and about 20 to 25 people can sleep here comfortably."* When Renu ji looked surprised, he simply reminded her: *"We are fakirs."* She quietly accepted his decision. See [[stories/drawing-room-fakirs]].

## The Shakti to His Shiv

She was the **Shakti (feminine principle)** to his **Shiv (masculine principle)** — shouldering responsibility for the household while he focused on seva. Running a home on meagre finances while raising young children was no cakewalk, yet she never complained. She became a supportive partner and **facilitator** in his journey.

Uma Prabhu ji offers a perceptive tribute:

> "More than Gurudev, who was very driven, Mataji must have had a difficult life. Her quiet simplicity and spirituality strongly anchored his spiritual drive. That is why the concept of Shiv and Shakti is appropriate in their situation. Mataji's resilience, grit, and simplicity resulted in the spirituality that oozed from her, which is why she did not need to give any blessing. Her presence and the aura she had was enough to bring comfort."
> — Uma Prabhu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.534

*"Mataji was much taller than she measured physically."*

She was often perplexed by the depth of his spiritual knowledge. She could not fathom its source — she had only seen him read Urdu newspapers or detective fiction like *Colonel Vinod*, and nothing more.

Gurudev never imposed his belief system on her. He pragmatically encouraged her to adhere to her own spiritual and religious practices, once telling her she did not need to chant any mantras because half the benefit of his nisvarth seva and *paath* would accrue to her anyway. He later gave her the powerful **Mahagayatri mantra** — and ribbed her that she should stop chanting lest she become more powerful than him. Their good-natured banter was delightful to watch.

Every morning, Mataji engaged in the laborious task of separating butter from curd using a **madhani** (traditional wooden churning tool), because Gurudev was incredibly particular about the purity of the ghee used at the sthan temple. After completing the morning chores, she went to school to teach; when she returned many hours later, a massive pile of work awaited her. Hingori's verdict: *"After completing the morning chores, she went to school to teach, and when she returned home many hours later, a massive pile of work awaited her. Many people work hard, no doubt, but she was the Queen of Queens!"*

> "No husband ever born has been spared by femininity!"
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.30

## Mother to Disciples

Mataji was a fierce and loyal mother figure to Gurudev's disciples. When Gurudev — a hard taskmaster — put his disciples through trials by fire, she would transform into a **fierce defence lawyer**, fighting their cases valiantly in what disciples called "**Judge G's courtroom**" while they hid from his gaze.

> Mataji treated us as if we were her children. She defended us, cared about our well-being, and cheered us on as we navigated the snakes and ladders of Guruism.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.30

## Spiritual Force in Her Own Right

Mataji was not only a supporter — she was a **spiritual force in her own right.**

**The Annapurna miracle:** When a disciple hosting his daughter's engagement ceremony found he had far more guests than food prepared, he requested Mataji's help. She accompanied him to the kitchen, peered into the food containers, covered them with lids, and instructed those present not to look inside while serving. Food prepared for about 150 people turned out to be more than enough for around 250. When Gurudev heard, he remarked: *"Your mother is Annapurna"* (the giver of food and nourishment). See [[stories/mataji-food-miracle-engagement]].

**Skull fracture healing:** When a disciple's son suffered multiple skull fractures in an automobile accident, Mataji arrived at the hospital and placed her hands on the boy's head. She walked away assuring the parents everything would be fine. A week later, the MRI scan revealed only one microfracture.

## Legacy of Seva

Mataji's unwavering dedication carried forward Gurudev's legacy of nisvarth seva after he passed into the beyond. Her commitment to his cause reflected her undying love for her husband and his selfless work. She was on her feet at work and seva for at least eighteen hours every day; her entire life was marked by self-sacrifice. Hingori's verdict: *"Outside of fiction, such a wife does not exist!"* (*The Guru of Gurus*, Who's Who, p.255)

She passed away in **May 2014.** On the hour of her death, the stars were auspiciously aligned. It was a queen's muhurat given to very few — indicating her exalted spiritual status.

> Gurudev and Mataji's relationship may have been that of a married couple, but in essence, it was and possibly continues to be an exceptionally powerful spiritual alliance.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.31

## Encounters with Buddhe Baba and Augarh

- **Buddhe Baba:** On one night she awoke to find Gurudev conversing with someone he addressed as Baba. Gurudev ended the conversation abruptly; the next day he revealed Buddhe Baba had told him she was overhearing them. See [[entities/buddhe-baba]].
- **Augarh at Har ki Pauri:** Augarh bent to touch her feet as a mark of respect at the Haridwar aarti, though she had no recollection — the cacophony of conch shells and chants had distracted her at the moment. See [[stories/augarh-har-ki-pauri]].
