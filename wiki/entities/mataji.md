# Mataji (Sudesh Sharma)

> *Gurudev's wife. Called Mataji — "mother" — by his disciples. The Shakti to his Shiv.*

> "The queen he wed became his pillar of strength.
> The Shakti to his Shiv.
> She took on the mantle of mother to his clan,
> serving selflessly as long as she lived."
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.26

## Who

**Sudesh Sharma** married Gurudev in **1960**. She was born in **Bilga, near Ludhiana in Punjab**, from a well-respected family. Her father, a coal trader, passed away during her childhood. She was the youngest of seven siblings. Her eldest brother earned recognition as a freedom fighter actively associated with the Indian National Congress; another brother, **Rudra ji**, chose the path of education, committing himself to teaching others.

Rudra ji was the first in her family to meet Gurudev, in **1960**. He wrote home afterwards: *"I conveyed my impression of him as a kind-hearted individual and an ideal match for my sister."* Years after her marriage she became known reverently as **Mataji** (mother) among Gurudev's disciples.

She worked as a **schoolteacher**, contributing her salary to the running of the household alongside the money Gurudev gave her.

## Marriage

Shortly after their wedding, she found Gurudev motionless on the bed:

> "One day, I went to talk to him and found him lying motionless on the bed. He was not breathing. I frantically tried to wake him, shaking his hands, feet, and body, but there was no reaction. In a panic, I called my sister-in-law to see what had happened to him. She calmly explained, 'There's no need to be anxious; he is in paath'. Back then, I did not understand what paath meant."
> — Mataji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.75

She later discovered that he had meditated near water bodies throughout his teens, spending entire days in meditation. He maintained a diet of stark simplicity — roti with jaggery when vegetables were unavailable — and was notably self-reliant, visiting many saints and expanding his knowledge through direct contact.

Shortly after marriage, Gurudev left to pursue spiritual attainment, temporarily setting aside his householder duties. A few years later, meditating **near the Santoksar Lake in Amritsar**, he heard an akashvani telling him that his spiritual quest could only reach completion if he returned to his wife and embraced the life of a householder — a message Hingori suggests was "possibly orchestrated by his then future guru, Buddhe Baba." He returned to Mataji and settled into the householder's life, a path he later formalised as [[concepts/grihasth-ashram]].

> [!warning] **Santoksar: lake or gurudwara?** *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.6 specifies the akashvani was heard "near the Santoksar Lake in Amritsar." Other sources — including the *Guru of Gurus* and earlier wiki pages — describe the same event at "Gurudwara Sri Santoksar Saheb in Amritsar." The gurudwara is built on the banks of the Santoksar Sarovar (holy tank), so the two descriptions likely refer to the same site. Flagged in [[sources/witnessing-greatness]].

Gurudev affectionately addressed his wife as **"Master."** When he told her, *"Master, wait and see what I become when I turn 35!"* she assumed he meant a raise or promotion at his job. She did not know he was referring to the age at which he would become a mahaguru.

She describes him: *"He was incredibly grounded and humble. He hardly ever shared stories of his encounters with divine beings. I never felt the urge to intervene in his prolonged sessions of paath; he seemed to be extending help to others through it, which I respected. But he also had a lively and playful side that balanced out his spiritual depth. He cracked a good joke, diligently met his work commitments, and enjoyed life's ordinary pleasures just like anyone else. We had our movie nights, explored new places together, and shared countless everyday joys, living a largely normal life."*

Their middle daughter Ila ji recalls a humorous domestic episode: when Gurudev challenged Mataji's cooking, she dared him to try himself. He accepted, prepared a vegetable dish — and used a week's supply of tomatoes in the process, repeatedly seeking everyone's approval that it was delicious. *"Mummy had to remind him that it had to be tasty when he used a week's supply of tomatoes in one dish!"*

## Mataji's Spiritual Journey

Mataji's spiritual path was entirely self-directed — Gurudev never tried to steer it. She immersed herself in mantra chanting, finding a deep affinity for the **Gayatri mantra**. When she shared her experiences with Gurudev, he acknowledged the mantra's significance. As her commitment deepened, he shared the powerful **Mahagayatri mantra** with her — a gradual unveiling that indicated his measured approach to imparting spiritual wisdom.

## Family Life

Gurudev frequently stated that his five children embodied the reincarnated spirits of his ancestors and spiritual connections, and he ensured they received proper upbringing and education. The household was a busy place: disciples visiting from across the country extended Gurudev's spiritual discussions into the early morning hours. He would occasionally wake Mataji at odd hours to prepare tea for visitors. *"Despite her occasional light-hearted protests, she showed remarkable grace and patience, ever ready to support both his endeavours and our endless quest for knowledge."* — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.77

## The Shakti to His Shiv

She was the **Shakti (feminine principle)** to his **Shiv (masculine principle)** — shouldering responsibility for the household while he focused on seva. Running a home on meagre finances while raising young children was no cakewalk, yet she never complained. She became a supportive partner and **facilitator** in his journey.

She was often perplexed by the depth of his spiritual knowledge. She could not fathom its source — she had only seen him read Urdu newspapers or detective fiction like *Colonel Vinod*, and nothing more.

Gurudev never imposed his belief system on her. He pragmatically encouraged her to adhere to her own spiritual and religious practices, once telling her she did not need to chant any mantras because half the benefit of his nisvarth seva and *paath* would accrue to her anyway. He later gave her the powerful **Mahagayatri mantra** — and ribbed her that she should stop chanting lest she become more powerful than him. Their good-natured banter was delightful to watch.

> "No husband ever born has been spared by femininity!"
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.30

## Mother to Disciples

Mataji was a fierce and loyal mother figure to Gurudev's disciples. When Gurudev — a hard taskmaster — put his disciples through trials by fire, she would transform into a **fierce defence lawyer**, fighting their cases valiantly in what disciples called "**Judge G's courtroom**" while they hid from his gaze.

> Mataji treated us as if we were her children. She defended us, cared about our well-being, and cheered us on as we navigated the snakes and ladders of Guruism.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.30

## Spiritual Force in Her Own Right

Mataji was not only a supporter — she was a **spiritual force in her own right.**

**The Annapurna miracle:** When a disciple hosting his daughter's engagement ceremony found he had far more guests than food prepared, he requested Mataji's help. She accompanied him to the kitchen, peered into the food containers, covered them with lids, and instructed those present not to look inside while serving. Food prepared for about 150 people turned out to be more than enough for around 250. When Gurudev heard, he remarked: *"Your mother is Annapurna"* (the giver of food and nourishment). See [[stories/mataji-food-miracle-engagement]].

**Skull fracture healing:** When a disciple's son suffered multiple skull fractures in an automobile accident, Mataji arrived at the hospital and placed her hands on the boy's head. She walked away assuring the parents everything would be fine. A week later, the MRI scan revealed only one microfracture.

## Uma Prabhu ji's Tribute

Uma Prabhu ji, a disciple, captures the dynamic of the Shiv-Shakti partnership from the outside:

> "More than Gurudev, who was very driven, Mataji must have had a difficult life. Her quiet simplicity and spirituality strongly anchored his spiritual drive. That is why the concept of Shiv and Shakti is appropriate in their situation. Mataji's resilience, grit, and simplicity resulted in the spirituality that oozed from her, which is why she did not need to give any blessing. Her presence and the aura she had was enough to bring comfort."
> — Uma Prabhu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.86

Every morning, without fail, Mataji undertook the laborious task of separating butter from curd using a **madhani** (traditional churning tool), to ensure the purity of the ghee used at the sthan temple — a task Gurudev was exacting about. After completing the morning chores, she went to school to teach, and when she returned many hours later, a massive pile of work awaited her. Hingori: *"She was the Queen of Queens."*

## Legacy of Seva

Mataji's unwavering dedication carried forward Gurudev's legacy of nisvarth seva after he passed into the beyond. Her commitment to his cause reflected her undying love for her husband and his selfless work. She was on her feet at work and seva for at least eighteen hours every day; her entire life was marked by self-sacrifice. Hingori's verdict: *"Outside of fiction, such a wife does not exist!"* (*The Guru of Gurus*, Who's Who, p.255)

She passed away in **May 2014.** On the hour of her death, the stars were auspiciously aligned. It was a queen's muhurat given to very few — indicating her exalted spiritual status.

> Gurudev and Mataji's relationship may have been that of a married couple, but in essence, it was and possibly continues to be an exceptionally powerful spiritual alliance.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.31

## Encounters with Buddhe Baba and Augarh

- **Buddhe Baba:** On one night she awoke to find Gurudev conversing with someone he addressed as Baba. Gurudev ended the conversation abruptly; the next day he revealed Buddhe Baba had told him she was overhearing them. See [[entities/buddhe-baba]].
- **Augarh at Har ki Pauri:** Augarh bent to touch her feet as a mark of respect at the Haridwar aarti, though she had no recollection — the cacophony of conch shells and chants had distracted her at the moment. See [[stories/augarh-har-ki-pauri]].
