# Quotes — Mantras

Mantra vidya as Gurudev's disciples witnessed it: the Chamunda, Mahagayatri, Mahamrityunjay and Mahaguru mantras. Primary source: *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11. See [[concepts/mantra-vidya]].

## Siddh Mantras

> His mantras were siddh, distinct from those found in traditional scriptures or online resources. He selflessly bestowed these powerful mantras upon his disciples, enabling them to achieve siddhi in remarkably short durations.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.149
[[concepts/mantra-vidya]] · [[concepts/mantra-transfer]]

> "Mantras, particularly those bestowed by a siddh guru, must be approached with utmost reverence."
> — Ashish Khanna ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.152
[[concepts/mantra-vidya]]

## Chamunda — Armour

> "These negative energies are sensed as intense pressure around the head. However, specific mantras, such as the Chamunda mantra, act as armour against these unsettling energies. As we start chanting the mantra internally, the heaviness begins to fade."
> — Ridhim ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.150
[[concepts/spiritual-attacks]]

> "At times, when facing hostile spirits, they can disguise themselves as revered entities like Shiv, attempting to provoke or challenge you. Sometimes, they may even use flattery as part of their cunning tactics."
> — Rajeev Hazarat ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.151
[[concepts/spiritual-attacks]] · [[concepts/spiritual-healing]]

> "When I started chanting the Chamunda mantra, I grew to around 20 feet in height, holding two weapons of blue light, and I chopped the spirit into pieces!"
> — Darius Moos ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.151
[[concepts/sukshma-sharir]]

## Intention

> The power of a mantra is greatly amplified when aligned with the healer's focused intention. This synergy explains how a single mantra can be effectively applied to achieve success across diverse spiritual endeavours.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.151
[[concepts/intent-as-healing]]

## Mahagayatri

> "Mahagayatri is more than just a mantra; it embodies the spirit of the four Vedas. Charged with energy, it grants tangible and spiritual rewards to those who chant it. One can break free from the endless loops of birth and death by invoking its power."
> — Gurudev, recalled by Ravi Trehan ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.154
[[stories/vashisht-fragments-mahagayatri]] · [[concepts/mukti]]

> "Guru Vashisht revealed that starting with the following generation, only a siddh guru could impart the full and original Mahagayatri mantra to the disciple, and this transmission would occur in utmost secrecy."
> — Ravi Trehan ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.154
[[entities/guru-vashisht]] · [[concepts/transmission-of-power]]

> Engulfed by the mantra's fiery force, I lost control of my temper… That moment was a grounding reflection of my behaviour and a testament to the influencing power of the mantra vibrating within me.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, pp.154–155
[[stories/hingori-mahagayatri-fire-and-water]]

> "The essence of Gayatri is the life in water."
> — Raji ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.155

## Mahamrityunjay and the Mahaguru Mantra

> "[It] controls all that is destructible in the world. Its essence goes beyond safeguarding life; it prevents disintegration and ensures continuity."
> — Raji ji on the Mahamrityunjay mantra, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.160
[[concepts/spiritual-healing]]

> "The bond between a guru and their disciple is sanctified through each mantra, yet all mantras ultimately merge into a single, unified form… This union represents the harmonic synergy of every naad or sound within the universe. Therefore, the Mahaguru mantra is the final, infinite mantra."
> — Raji ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.160

> — Raji ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, p.161
