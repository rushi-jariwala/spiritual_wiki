# Quotes — Peerless Mentorship

How Gurudev mentored: no sermons, customised insight, sahaj discipline, ordinary people asked to do extraordinary things, and a guru who asked not to be idolised. Primary source: *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10. See also [[quotes/guru]].

## Trust Yourself More Than the Herbs

> "Tu kaagaz bhi maathe pe lagaa ke usko de degaa, toh woh theek ho jaayega (Even if you bless a piece of paper and give it to a visitor, it will serve as a cure)".
> — Gurudev to Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.141
[[stories/gurudev-diary-guru-not-doctor]] · [[concepts/intent-as-healing]] · [[concepts/aura]]

> These 15 words became a lesson for life, leading me to realise the immense power of the human aura. When channelled through a focused mind, it can unleash capabilities beyond one's wildest imagination.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.141
[[concepts/aura]] · [[concepts/spiritual-healing]]

## Teaching Without Sermons

> "In other places, you were greeted with sermons and hymns. But at the sthan, those traditions were absent. Gurudev's teachings were purely experiential and personal."
> — Mrs Jolly, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.142
[[concepts/customised-guidance]]

> "The nature of Gurudev's spiritual guidance was customised, where certain insights were relevant to one person but not necessarily to another… The guiding philosophy was simple: every individual is an integral part of the param-aatma, and by offering solace to all life forms, one finds comfort in return."
> — Pradeep ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.142
[[concepts/customised-guidance]] · [[practices/seva]]

## Sahaj — Detachment Within Attachment

> "Bhakti with Gurudev was sahaj. It unfolded as a journey of incremental steps. It was less about renouncing the world or adopting asceticism and more about achieving equilibrium—engaging with familial and societal responsibilities while cultivating a spirit of detachment. 'Detachment within attachment' is what he preached."
> — Bindu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.142
[[concepts/vairagya]] · [[concepts/grihasth-ashram]]

> "His path to spiritual evolution was lined with discipline, yet it was remarkably straightforward. It was as natural as a river's flow."
> — Bindu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.143
[[analogies/analogies]]

> Our only competition was with ourselves; it was our spirits that he was endeavouring to refine.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.142
[[concepts/guru-disciple]]

## Transform Your Gunas

> "Merely reciting mantras is not enough. It is crucial not only to chant but to actively transform your gunas. There is immense value in introspecting and recognising your flaws, then diligently working to rectify them."
> — Gurudev, as recalled by Uma Prabhu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, pp.142–143

## Ordinary People, Extraordinary Tasks

> "Gurudev asked any random person, who had no idea how, to start curing people. How could they have possibly known how to heal? They obeyed what Gurudev told them, and people found relief. Only a very evolved being and exceptional mentor can help others reach the level to which he helped us evolve."
> — Surender ji (Kanpur), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.144
[[concepts/spiritual-healing]] · [[concepts/transmission-of-power]]

> We were like 'pre-cooked meals' awaiting microwaving. Gurudev pressed the right buttons to awaken our dormant capabilities linked to our past lives.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, p.144
