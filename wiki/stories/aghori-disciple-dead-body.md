# The Aghori's Disciple and the Levitating Dead Body

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.133–134

> One of Gurudev's disciples had been an understudy of an aghori before coming to Gurudev. He told us, the aghori made him recite mantras while sitting on a dead body, even as it started levitating. The aghori had warned him that if the dead body's head came to life and turned 180 degrees, he would chop off the body's head to protect him. After training him in the black arts for a short period, the aghori refused to take him on as his disciple, since he knew he was to get a much greater (and sattvic) guru than himself in time to come.

**Context:** Narrated by one of Gurudev's disciples as personal testimony of his prior training under an aghori master. The incident illustrates the extremity of tamasic mantra siddhi practice — graveyards, dead bodies, and spirit energy as the medium. The aghori's refusal to fully accept the man as a disciple is notable: he recognised that a greater, sattvic guru was destined for this student and chose not to obstruct that path.

This story is the most specific account in the Hingori corpus of aghori initiatory practices in the tradition of negative tantric vidya. The aghori appears to have integrity despite his practices — a reminder that Hingori's framework does not collapse all aghoris into villains. (See [[concepts/aghor]] — Aghor philosophy is described as "highly evolved beyond the seemingly messy optics.")
