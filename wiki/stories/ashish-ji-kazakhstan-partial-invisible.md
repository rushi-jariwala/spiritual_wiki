# Ashish Ji Becomes Partially Invisible in Kazakhstan

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, p.40–41

## Full Narrative

> A striking example occurred with Ashish ji during a vacation in a frozen lake region near Almaty, Kazakhstan. He recounts a sudden and inexplicable transformation upon stepping out of the car: "Within five minutes of getting out of the car, I felt three times my size. My demeanour and tone of voice changed, and I felt incredibly powerful. My friends were taking photographs as people do on vacation. I sternly warned them not to take mine, but they ignored my advice."
>
> However, they were awestruck when they reviewed the photos at their hotel. One of the pictures of Ashish ji was inexplicably incomplete: it showed only the lower half of his body, with everything above the waist absent, as though he had become partially invisible.
> — Ashish ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, p.40–41

## Context

Unlike Gurudev's deliberate invisible walkthrough ([[stories/rajpal-sekhri-gurudev-invisible]]), this episode was entirely involuntary — Ashish ji felt the transformation happen, warned his friends, but had no conscious control over it. Hingori notes that Gurudev's disciples and their downlines have experienced similar phenomena without deliberate intent. The unexplained expansion of size and the change in demeanour match the experience of a nirman kaya — or a more expanded form of the spirit body — momentarily manifesting through the physical frame.

The photographic evidence anchors the account in a format that leaves no interpretive room. The lower half visible, the upper half absent: a partial state between visible and invisible, consistent with the chapter's description of evolved yogis stopping light from reflecting off the molecular structure of their bodies.

Ashish ji is the same disciple who experienced his guru's bilocation in Goa ([[stories/ashish-khanna-goa-bilocation]]).
