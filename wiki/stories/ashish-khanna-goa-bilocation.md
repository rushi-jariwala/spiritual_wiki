# Ashish Khanna Ji's Guru Appears in Goa

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, p.39–40

## Full Narrative

> Ashish Khanna ji, who manages a sthan at the Moolchand Hospital campus in Delhi, says, "I was in Goa for business, and my cousin and I decided to have lunch at a restaurant. When we asked the waiter for menu suggestions, he recommended prawns. I had been advised against consuming seafood on my first visit to the sthan, but somehow, this slipped my mind. After ordering prawns, my cousin excused himself to use the washroom, leaving me to finalise the remaining selection. As I placed the menu on the table, I was stunned to see my guru, a disciple of Gurudev, sitting across the table. 'How are you here?' he asked. I told him I was with my brother. He remarked, 'I hear the food here is excellent'. I replied, 'Yes, we have ordered prawns'. He smiled; we chatted briefly, and then he left."
>
> Ashish ji immediately phoned his gurubhai in Mumbai, excitedly sharing that he had just met their guru. However, he was stunned when his gurubhai informed him that he had been in a long meeting with their guru in Mumbai and had left just two minutes earlier! Witnessing his guru's uncanny supernature triggered Ashish ji's complete surrender to him, and eating seafood became a footnote in his culinary history.
> — Ashish Khanna ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, p.39–40

## Context

The encounter shows bilocation operating not only through the mahaguru himself but through his **disciple** — an indication that the capacity for bilocation, acquired through proximity and transmission from Gurudev, propagates down the lineage. This is consistent with the book's broader thesis: the mahaguru is also a "saint-maker," and many of the supernature capacities documented in this chapter appear in his disciples' disciples as well.

The instruction about seafood had been given on the sthan's first visit — a simple custom of respect. Its near-violation in Goa, and the guru's appearance precisely at that moment, mirrors Gurudev's appearances to Nakul ji and other disciples when they are at crossroads. The result — *"complete surrender"* — is the text's own language for the spiritual fruit of such encounters. See [[concepts/surrender]].
