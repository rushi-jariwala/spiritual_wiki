# Ashish Khanna Ji — Bilocation in Goa and Partial Invisibility in Almaty

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, pp.38–41

## Full Narrative

### Bilocation in Goa

Ashish Khanna ji manages a sthan at the Moolchand Hospital campus in Delhi.

> "I was in Goa for business, and my cousin and I decided to have lunch at a restaurant. When we asked the waiter for menu suggestions, he recommended prawns. I had been advised against consuming seafood on my first visit to the sthan, but somehow, this slipped my mind. After ordering prawns, my cousin excused himself to use the washroom, leaving me to finalise the remaining selection. As I placed the menu on the table, I was stunned to see my guru, a disciple of Gurudev, sitting across the table. 'How are you here?' he asked. I told him I was with my brother. He remarked, 'I hear the food here is excellent'. I replied, 'Yes, we have ordered prawns'. He smiled; we chatted briefly, and then he left."

Ashish ji immediately phoned his gurubhai in Mumbai, excitedly sharing that he had just met their guru. However, he was stunned when his gurubhai informed him that his guru had been in a long meeting with them in Mumbai and had left just two minutes earlier. Witnessing his guru's uncanny supernature triggered Ashish ji's complete surrender, and eating seafood became a footnote in his culinary history.

### Partial Invisibility in Almaty

On a vacation near a frozen lake region in Almaty, Kazakhstan, Ashish ji had an involuntary and inexplicable experience:

> "Within five minutes of getting out of the car, I felt three times my size. My demeanour and tone of voice changed, and I felt incredibly powerful. My friends were taking photographs as people do on vacation. I sternly warned them not to take mine, but they ignored my advice."

When they reviewed the photographs at their hotel, one picture of Ashish ji was inexplicably incomplete: it showed only the lower half of his body, with everything above the waist absent, as though he had become partially invisible.

— *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, pp.38–41

## Context

Two distinct phenomena are recorded here for the same disciple. The Goa bilocation demonstrates that the mahaguru's lineage itself carries the capacity for nirman kaya projection — not only Gurudev, but his initiated disciples can appear in two places at once. Ashish ji's guru was physically in Mumbai, yet a complete, interactive version of him appeared at a restaurant table in Goa. See [[concepts/astral-travel]] (nirman kaya in action) and [[concepts/supernature]].

The Almaty experience is perhaps more striking for being involuntary and physical: Ashish ji did not consciously project himself, yet the partial invisibility was documented by camera. Hingori notes this as evidence that disciples empowered through the lineage can undergo supernormal transformations without intending to. The same principle is recorded for Gurudev himself — walking unrecognised through crowds when he chose not to be seen — but Ashish ji's case occurred without his conscious will. See [[stories/rajpal-sekhri-invisibility-gate]] for Gurudev's deliberate version.

The Goa account in *Witnessing Greatness* provides much greater detail and confirms the identity of the narrator (Ashish Khanna ji, Moolchand sthan) compared to the brief reference in [[concepts/astral-travel]].
