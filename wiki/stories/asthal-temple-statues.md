# The Asthal Temple — Statues as Living Presence

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, pp.45–48

## Full Narrative

### Origins: The Dream Commission

A site in the village of Asthal in Dehradun, Uttarakhand, had an unfinished Shivalay that had not been touched in sixteen years. Gurudev appeared in a disciple's dream asking for its completion; this led to the establishment of a temple and sthan there.

Karamjit ji, a devotee closely linked to this founding, shares a vision he had thirty-five years before the inauguration:

> "Gurudev showed me a place in Dehradun that would draw and help many people."

When Karamjit ji visited Asthal for the temple's inauguration, he was astonished: the newly established sthan and temple were strikingly similar to the place Gurudev had shown him in a vision three decades earlier.

### The Consecration

The creation of statues of Gurudev and Mataji at Asthal was a substantial undertaking. Over a dozen disciples of Gurudev oversaw their making. More than sixty-five disciples and their downlines performed the *pran pratishtha* — the Vedic ritual of infusing life (pran) into a deity's idol or statue, essential for consecrating new idols in temples. This collective effort amplified the temple's spiritual potency.

### The Smiling Statue

Ridhim Thapar ji, who does healing seva at Asthal:

> "Once, while praying at the temple, I looked up at the statue. Gurudev's face had turned, and he was smiling at me. It was as though the statue had come to life."

### Alpana Ji — Conversation with the Statue

Alpana ji, a devotee, describes a difficult period:

> "I was facing the worst financial crisis ever. During that time, I often visited Asthal, sitting in the temple and confiding in Gurudev's statue as though it were my father."

> "Once, as I spoke to the statue, I could have sworn it smiled at me!"

> "When my son lost his job in Canada, I implored Gurudev's statue to help him find employment. It felt as though Gurudev was truly listening to me. The very next morning, my son called to tell me, 'Mumma, I've been contacted by Hilton for an interview'. And, miraculously, he secured the job!"

In a separate moment, Alpana ji saw not Gurudev but Lord Shankar himself:

> "On another occasion, it wasn't Gurudev I saw, but Lord Shankar, sitting in meditation with folded hands, radiating light from where the statue stands. The statue took on a blue hue, glowing with an aura. I was sceptical at first, but each time I looked, I witnessed the same form."

### Shilpa Malhotra Ji — Ball of Light

Shilpa Malhotra ji, who has dedicated decades to seva:

> "In my mind's eye, I saw Gurudev appear in a towering form behind the statue. He looked down at me and extended his hand, offering me a ball of light."

When she shared this vision with her guru, he explained that the vision symbolised the transfer of the mahaguru's energy to Shilpa ji.

### Mohan — From Sceptic to Believer

Mohan, the temple's pandit, began his tenure at Asthal as a sceptic. A visiting devotee encouraged him: "Don't just see this as a statue; consider it as Gurudev's presence." He decided to test this.

> "Plagued by a toothache, I addressed the statue, requesting healing. I placed a glass of jal at its feet, which I drank shortly after. To my amazement, my toothache disappeared. I later sought relief from some ongoing urinary tract-related problems. Incredibly, that issue was also resolved, and I remained symptom-free for the next two to three years!"

### Gautam Bhushan Ji — Consciousness Inside the Statue

Gautam Bhushan ji, who conducts healing seva at the Delhi sthan:

> "While meditating at the temple at Asthal, I experienced a powerful surge of energy that seemed to transport my consciousness inside Gurudev's statue. Looking through the statue's eyes, I felt an overwhelming sensation of omnipotence, as if I understood everything there was to know. During this state, I saw a devotee from Delhi meditating in the temple. He had been suffering from severe pain and was part of a remote healing group where sevadaars offer healing via video conferencing. I directed my intention toward healing him. The entire experience lasted about 20 minutes."

> "About a week later, during a video healing session, the same devotee from Delhi informed us that the intense pain, which he had previously described as '20 on a scale of 10', had completely vanished!"

Hingori's own commentary on the Asthal statues closes the chapter: *"The mahaguru's statue has made my role almost redundant, and it is an honour to witness and embrace this irrelevance."*

— *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, pp.45–48

## Context

The Asthal phenomena represent a distinct phase in the post-death expression of the mahaguru's presence. Where earlier accounts document Gurudev appearing in person or in spirit form, the Asthal stories demonstrate that inanimate objects — statues consecrated through *pran pratishtha* by a critical mass of initiated disciples — can themselves become active channels of his consciousness.

The pattern holds across all accounts: sceptics (Mohan the pandit) are more reliably converted than believers, because their healing cannot be attributed to wishful thinking. The healings through *jal* placed at the statue's feet are structurally identical to the jal-based healings of Gurudev's physical life, suggesting that the mechanism — the mahaguru's intent operating through a charged medium — continues to function without his physical body.

Gautam Bhushan ji's account is among the most unusual in the entire book: the boundary dissolves not between devotee and Gurudev, but between devotee and statue, as his consciousness enters the idol and heals through its eyes. This inverts the usual direction of flow (grace descending from statue to devotee) and suggests the statue has become a two-way portal.

> [!tip] The *pran pratishtha* ceremony performed by sixty-five disciples and their downlines is the operative mechanism: collective shakti, concentrated in a ritual of consecration, creates a focal point capable of sustaining the mahaguru's consciousness even in his physical absence. This is not mere symbolism — it is described as a functional extension of his presence.
