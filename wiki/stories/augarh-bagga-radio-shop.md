# Augarh at Mr Bagga's Radio Shop in Hamidpur

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.134–135

> In the case of Mr Bagga of Hamidpur, Augarh visited his radio shop, decades ago. With no intentions of buying, he pestered Mrs and Mr Bagga and drove them up the wall. Finally, Mr Bagga lost it and using the choicest Punjabi cuss words asked them to leave his shop.
>
> When Mr Bagga arrived in Gurgaon, Gurudev greeted him with an onslaught of sarcasm, repeating each word from the conversation he had with the mystery shopper.

**Context:** The identity of the "mystery shopper" was Augarh — the head of the Aghor sect and Gurudev's mysterious disciple. The incident occurred decades before the time of writing. Augarh's deliberate pestering of Mr Bagga appears to have been a test of temperament. Gurudev's knowledge of every word from the exchange — though physically elsewhere — confirms both his omniscience of the disciple world and his connection to Augarh. Mr Bagga learned through mortification that the unseen world is watching.

This encounter adds a mischievous dimension to Augarh's character — he is described in the same chapter as resembling "Luca Brasi of Mario Puzo's *The Godfather*... an accomplice to Gurudev in twisting the tails of his disciples and testing them."
