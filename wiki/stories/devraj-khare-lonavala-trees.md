# Devraj Khare Ji — The Trimmed Trees at Lonavala

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev always emphasised that our actions should align with the nature's rhythm. Devraj Khare ji's story highlights how nature can sometimes push back against human endeavours. He begins by setting the scene: "Periodically, we go to Lonavala, where we have a home with a garden and many trees. I took responsibility for the garden's upkeep, including tree maintenance."
>
> He recalls a specific incident: "Some years ago, I noticed that one of the trees was attracting a lot of insects and required trimming. I instructed the local gardener to trim the tree, supervising the task myself. However, the following morning, I woke up with bed bug bites all over my body."
>
> "A couple of weeks later, I approached the gardener once more, requesting him to prune the excessively grown creepers," he recalls. However, the next day, he was afflicted with bug bites again. This was particularly perplexing since he had already spent several nights in Lonavala without any untoward incident. It led him to speculate a possible link between these bites and the trimming of the trees.
>
> On his third visit, Devraj ji decided to test his hypothesis: "I made it a point to ask the gardener to trim a particular tree. As the gardener did so, I felt a strange, tingling sensation on my body. The following day, the bites reappeared. I then understood the correlation between my actions and bodily reactions."
>
> In a moment of introspection, he approached the trimmed tree: "I folded my hands and asked for forgiveness, promising never to do it again". This realisation was further reinforced in a later incident involving palm trees. While walking in the evening, the gardener pointed out snakes camouflaging in them. Devraj ji's initial reaction was to suggest trimming the trees, but he immediately felt a sensation in his left hand. Realising the importance of not interfering with nature's course, he said, "Just let nature be."
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, pp.167–168
