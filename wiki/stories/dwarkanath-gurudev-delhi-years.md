# Dwarkanath ji's Memories of the Delhi Years

## Full Narrative

> "Nagpal and I went back to our days as youngsters in Agra. After relocating to Delhi, we secured jobs and became roommates. One day, Nagpal mentioned that a colleague from Hariana was searching for a place to stay. I was open to the idea, suggesting he could move in with us if we got along well. Upon meeting Gurudev, I was immediately struck by his humility and intellect. Consequently, the three of us agreed to share the apartment."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.16

> "Gurudev's job as a soil surveyor required him to travel extensively, so he was only home for about half the year. I yearned for his delightful company when he was away on tours. It is worth noting that my elder brother only came to visit when Gurudev was around. Gurudev had a way of telling jokes and sharing anecdotes that left everyone wanting more. Such was his charisma that every meeting transformed casual acquaintances into ardent admirers."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.16

> "One trait of his that profoundly impacted me was his complete detachment from money."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.17

From a young age, Gurudev spent his pocket money on feeding animals and birds, or treating his friends to sweets and soft drinks. He rarely spent money on himself. His reluctance to accept leftover change during transactions was a habit Dwarkanath ji himself tried — and found far more difficult than it appeared. Occasionally, Gurudev would reach into his pockets and, to everyone's surprise, produce a bundle of notes — which Hingori interprets as a power granted to him later in life as an acknowledgement of his steadfast altruism.

> "At Gurudev's insistence, I travelled to Gurgaon monthly for Bada Guruvar. The mahaguru insisted that I fly instead of taking the train. Miraculously, during those years of frequent financial shortfalls, the necessary funds for the flight tickets would somehow materialise—found under my mattress, hidden in a cupboard drawer, or secreted in a coat pocket. I am confident that the mahaguru's benevolence was at work."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.18

> "Living together for so long, we were oblivious of his spiritual powers. He was far more evolved than we realised. While we slept, he often rose, unfolded his cot, wrapped himself in a blanket, and meditated for hours. Nagpal would question him, and he would say he was doing his paath. He often made predictions that would invariably come true. And then there were the unexplained conveniences, like arriving at a movie theatre without tickets, only to find someone waiting with exactly three tickets for us. We experienced many such mysterious events."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.18

Despite living together for years, nothing could fully prepare Dwarkanath ji for their reunion after Gurudev had ascended to the status of a mahaguru:

> "During my visit to Gurgaon, it was challenging to reconcile my memories of my old friend with his new role as a guru. However, as time passed, I began to grasp the extent of his spiritual influence. During one of our meetings, Gurudev gestured for me to sit beside him. Out of deep respect, I chose to sit on the floor, struck by the powerful energy he radiated. His presence was so commanding that it seemed to dissipate any negativity around us."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.18–19

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, "Back to the Future," Ch.1, pp.16–19. All testimony attributed to Dwarkanath ji.
