# Dwarkanath ji — From Roommate to Devotee

## Full Narrative

Dwarkanath ji was KL Nagpal ji's landlord and lifelong friend, who became Gurudev's third roommate in their modest East Delhi apartment. His testimony spans years of shared life and culminates in a Gurgaon reunion that reconciled past and present in a single moment.

### The First Meeting

> "Nagpal and I went back to our days as youngsters in Agra. After relocating to Delhi, we secured jobs and became roommates. One day, Nagpal mentioned that a colleague from Hariana was searching for a place to stay. I was open to the idea, suggesting he could move in with us if we got along well. Upon meeting Gurudev, I was immediately struck by his humility and intellect. Consequently, the three of us agreed to share the apartment."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.01

### Charisma and Companionship

> "Gurudev's job as a soil surveyor required him to travel extensively, so he was only home for about half the year. I yearned for his delightful company when he was away on tours. It is worth noting that my elder brother only came to visit when Gurudev was around. Gurudev had a way of telling jokes and sharing anecdotes that left everyone wanting more. Such was his charisma that every meeting transformed casual acquaintances into ardent admirers."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.01

### Detachment from Money

> "One trait of his that profoundly impacted me was his complete detachment from money."

> From a young age, Gurudev spent his pocket money on feeding animals and birds or treating his friends to sweets and soft drinks. He rarely spent money on himself, preferring to use it to benefit others. One notable habit illustrating his detachment was his reluctance to accept leftover change during transactions. Having tried to adopt this habit myself, I can attest to its difficulty — it is far more challenging than it appears.

> Occasionally, Gurudev would reach into his pockets and, to everyone's surprise, pull out a bundle of notes. I wonder if he begrudgingly wielded some power to conjure up the currency, using it solely in circumstances that demanded nisvarth seva. It seems plausible that this extraordinary ability was granted to him later in life as an acknowledgement of his steadfast altruism and complete detachment from money.

— Dwarkanath ji and Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.01

Hingori adds a personal confirmation: at Gurudev's insistence, he travelled to Gurgaon monthly for Bada Guruvar. The mahaguru insisted on air travel. During years of frequent financial shortfalls, the necessary funds for flight tickets would materialise — found under a mattress, in a cupboard drawer, or secreted in a coat pocket.

### Night Paath and Unexplained Conveniences

> "Living together for so long, we were oblivious of his spiritual powers. He was far more evolved than we realised. While we slept, he often rose, unfolded his cot, wrapped himself in a blanket, and meditated for hours. Nagpal would question him, and he would say he was doing his paath. He often made predictions that would invariably come true. And then there were the unexplained conveniences, like arriving at a movie theatre without tickets, only to find someone waiting with exactly three tickets for us. We experienced many such mysterious events."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.01

### The Gurgaon Reunion

> "During my visit to Gurgaon, it was challenging to reconcile my memories of my old friend with his new role as a guru. However, as time passed, I began to grasp the extent of his spiritual influence. During one of our meetings, Gurudev gestured for me to sit beside him. Out of deep respect, I chose to sit on the floor, struck by the powerful energy he radiated. His presence was so commanding that it seemed to dissipate any negativity around us."

— Dwarkanath ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.01

Hingori observes: while the narrative of transitioning from friends to foes is standard, evolving from a friend to a devotee is unique. It speaks volumes about Dwarkanath ji's humility and wisdom in acknowledging and honouring his once-roommate as his spiritual guru.
