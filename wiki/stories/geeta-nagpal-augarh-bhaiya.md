# Geeta Nagpal Ji and "Augarh Bhaiya"

## Full Narrative

> Geeta Nagpal ji, a sevadaar at the Mumbai sthan, shares: "I once had a vision of what I believed was a thin-faced, bearded form of Lord Shankar holding a trishul in his left hand," she begins. "After I sketched it and presented it to Guruji (Gurudev's disciple), he identified the form as Augarh. He suggested I frame the drawing and hang it in my room for protection. He also advised that I could seek Augarh's assistance whenever needed."
>
> Geeta ji started conversing daily with this framed image, referring to it affectionately as 'Augarh Bhaiya'. "During terrible asthma attacks at night, I would look at his picture and plead, 'Augarh Bhaiya, please help me'. Miraculously, I would start feeling better within minutes of requesting him," she explains.
>
> "During this time, my husband, Deepak, and I regularly visited a Hanuman temple where we would pour oil over a statue of Shani every Saturday. One particular Saturday morning, I awoke with intense pain in my left leg, doubtful I could even stand in the queue at the temple. Before departing, I turned to Augarh Bhaiya and said, 'Please handle this problem. I am leaving for the temple.'
>
> "When we arrived at the temple, we encountered a thin man with long, unkempt hair sitting on the pavement, wildly gesturing and shouting, 'Come, come!' The only route into the temple required passing by him. As I walked past, he abruptly seized my left leg and pressed a spot on my calf muscle just below the knee. It all occurred in mere seconds; I was stunned into silence but managed to continue into the temple grounds.
>
> "After completing the ritual at the Shani statue, Deepak and I handed out vada pav and tea to the needy outside the temple. When Deepak offered a vada pav to the unkempt man, he unexpectedly threw it onto the street, startling us both."
>
> Later, when Geeta ji arrived home, she realised the pain in her leg had vanished entirely. She adds, "I recounted the entire episode to Guruji the next morning, who revealed, 'That was Augarh'. Since that mysterious encounter, the pain has never come back."
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.8, pp.111–112
