# Giri Ji's Mother — "Her Uterus Will Not Be Removed"

## Full Narrative

> In 1991, amidst the setting of the sthan in Punjabi Bagh, a notable event unfolded that would leave an indelible mark on the lives of Giri ji and his mother. It was here, in Gurudev's company, that Giri ji received distressing news through an unexpected phone call. His mother, back in Mumbai, had been diagnosed with uterine cancer, and her doctors recommended the surgical removal of her uterus.
>
> The news cast a shadow of grave concern over Giri ji, but Gurudev's response was both immediate and reassuring.
>
> "Do not be troubled, my son," the mahaguru said calmly and compassionately, "Your mother has borne my disciple and I am indebted to her. I assure you that her uterus will not be removed."
>
> Following the mahaguru's guidance, Giri ji left for Mumbai to care for his ailing mother. Gurudev's instructions were precise: a jar of tea was to be placed in their home temple every morning at 5.30, and after making the bhog,¹ Giri ji would give his mother a cup of this blessed tea to drink.
>
> "A few days later, my mother expressed a desire to speak to Gurudev," he continues. "She greeted him over the phone and, following a brief conversation, engaged in a ritual where she took nine sips of water while Gurudev was on the line. This process was repeated for three days."
>
> On the fourth day, following Gurudev's guidance, Giri ji and his mother went to the doctor for pre-surgery tests. The results were astonishing. The doctor announced, "It appears there was a mistake on our end. The current tests show no signs of cancer in your mother's uterus. It's likely there was an error in the initial test results, resulting in a misdiagnosis. We deeply apologise for the unnecessary stress this has caused."
>
> Giri ji's mother, who was once on the brink of undergoing major surgery, lived an additional 30 years completely free of cancer.
>
> ¹ Food offering made to a deity or spiritual power as part of a ritual or prayer. After the food is offered and the deity or power is believed to have partaken in it, the remaining food is consumed and/or distributed as prasad
> — Hingori and Giri ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.9, pp.124–125
