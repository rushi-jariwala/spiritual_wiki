# Guddan Ji — Fourteen Years of Arthritis

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev consistently highlighted that the essence of healing lay not in his interventions but in the deep faith of the individuals seeking his aid. This principle was illustrated through various examples of steadfast belief, notably in the extraordinary recovery of Guddan ji.
>
> Her brother, Surender ji, reflects sombrely on her extended ordeal: "Guddan endured 14 excruciating years of severe, deformative arthritis. Her hands, feet, and joints were severely swollen, and her right foot visibly contorted. The pain was so acute that even the lightest touch of a soft cloth caused her to scream in agony. Despite countless medical treatments, there was no relief in sight."
>
> The turning point came with their encounter with Gurudev: "We had heard of Gurudev's miraculous healing abilities and decided to meet him. Guddan even discontinued her medications, confident that the guru she was about to meet would heal her.
>
> "Our initial interaction with him at the sthan in his home in Shivpuri was brief. Guddan lay on the floor while he sat on a divan. Without uttering a word, he gazed deeply into her eyes. He then instructed her to stand, gently touching her misaligned joint and realigning her leg. 'How do you feel? Do you still feel pain?' he inquired gently. Her response was brief but miraculous, 'No'. And just like that, she began to walk on her own. We knew we had witnessed a miracle," he reflects, with gratitude.
>
> Eventually, Gurudev established his first sthan outside Gurgaon at the home of Guddan ji and Surender ji in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh. For many like them, the quest for healing—whether for themselves or their loved ones—became the catalyst that awakened their latent healing powers.
> — Hingori and Surender ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.9, p.123

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.9, "Sage of Solace," p.123

> [!question] One Guddan ji or two?
> [[stories/guddan-barfi-healing]] (*The Guru of Gurus*, p.225) tells of a Guddan ji who, as a young girl, asked her brother to take her to Gurgaon and vomited a piece of barfi. The Kanpur Guddan ji of *The Guru of Gurus*' Stages chapter gave up 14 years of drugs on faith — which matches this account. Both Guddan ji figures have a brother, but the settings differ (Gurgaon vs. the Shivpuri home) and so do the mechanisms (barfi expelled vs. joint realigned). The text does not say whether they are one person.
