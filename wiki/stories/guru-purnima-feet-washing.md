# Guru Purnima Feet-Washing — Drinking the Guru's Jal

## Full Narrative

> On special days such as Guru Purnima, a tradition involved disciples washing Gurudev's feet with water. The disciples would then drink this water infused with his spiritual energy. This was a way to receive the mahaguru's blessings, deepening their bond with him.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

## Context

This practice appears in the context of the chapter's broader teaching on water as a spiritual medium — that water absorbs and transmits the energy of whoever interacts with it. Gurudev's feet, charged with the accumulated tapasya of a lifetime, would transfer their shakti into the water. The disciples who drank that water were receiving his energy in a form that the body could directly absorb.

The washing of the guru's feet is one of the formal acts of [[concepts/guru-vandana]] — it expresses reverence, acknowledges the north/south pole energy exchange (feet as the negative pole, the crown as the positive), and generates positive samskars of humility and devotion. Here the practice is extended: the water itself becomes the blessing, and drinking it completes the circuit of transmission.

See also [[stories/gurudev-sipped-water-mantra-transfer]] for the everyday version of this principle: Gurudev sharing his sipped water with disciples as a mantra transmission.
