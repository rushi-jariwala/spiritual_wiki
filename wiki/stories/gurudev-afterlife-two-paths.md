# Two Paths in the Afterlife — Plain Road to Higher Realms

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev often spoke of choices that await us in the afterlife. He talked about two paths: one that looks enticing and another that is simple and plain. Unlike what most believe, he advised us to choose the plain path. He said the simpler route led to the higher spiritual dimensions, while the attractive path could mislead us and keep us stuck in lower realms.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.95

The narrator adds his personal confirmation:

> My journeys through three distinct realms — two higher and one lower — brought his words to life for me. The higher realms were bright yet sparsely inhabited, a stark contrast to the lower realm, which felt like a dark, seedy nightclub. Perhaps these experiences were designed to prepare me for my role as a reservation manager for the thinly populated higher realms!
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.95

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.95

## Context

This teaching appears in the chapter on sensory perception and mastery, following the section on sight and the danger of being drawn by visual beauty. The connection is direct: the senses — particularly sight — draw the jivaatma toward what is attractive, stimulating, and entertaining. In the afterlife, that same sensory pull operates at the level of choosing which realm to enter.

The enticing path looks pleasant and stimulating; the plain path looks unremarkable. A jivaatma still driven by vaasnas and unmanaged sensory preferences will instinctively choose the enticing path — and thereby enter a lower realm. The plain path, unattractive to the senses, leads upward.

Hingori's three direct experiences are offered as empirical verification: the higher realms were *bright* but *sparse*, while the lower realm was *dark* and *crowded* — a combination of sensory qualities that deliberately inverted the expected pattern. Brightness is usually associated with joy; here the crowded darkness of the lower realm would appear more welcoming to an entertainment-seeking spirit.

> [!tip] This teaching is a direct application of [[concepts/senses-management]] to the post-death state: the senses follow what they are trained to follow. An unmanaged sense of sight, drawn to the beautiful, will choose the beautiful path in the afterlife and arrive at the wrong destination.
