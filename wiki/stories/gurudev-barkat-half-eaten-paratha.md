# The Half-Eaten Paratha — Gurudev on Barkat

**Source:** Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.80–81 (via Renu ji)

---

Gurudev treated food with deep respect. His home held an unwritten rule: nothing left on plates unfinished. When the family had fussy-eating children — as most children are — Mataji would quietly eat their leftovers herself.

On one occasion, disciples from Himachal Pradesh, Kanpur, and their families were staying at the house. Someone left a plate in the kitchen sink with a half-eaten paratha. Indu didi, in charge of kitchen seva, had not seen who left it.

> When Gurudev walked into the kitchen, he saw the plate with wasted food. "Indu, who has left the food?" he inquired. She admitted she did not know, but offered to eat it herself. Gurudev picked up the plate and ate the leftovers before she could react.
>
> "Learn to respect food," he later told them all. "This is barkat — those who have it, waste it. Ask someone less fortunate what a square meal is worth. We must be grateful for what we have."

Renu ji's reflection:

> "His words made me realise that I must value the barkat of being able to consume a full meal regularly."

---

## What It Reveals

Gurudev did not lecture first. He *acted* — eating the food himself rather than asking Indu didi to do it, so no one else bore the weight of someone else's wastefulness. Only after the act did he name what had happened.

Barkat — the luck of abundance, the grace of making one's resources last — is not a passive condition. In Gurudev's teaching, those who waste it disrespect both the barkat and those who lack it. The act of consuming the leftover paratha was seva of a peculiar kind: closing a small karmic account that a guest had opened carelessly.

> [!tip] Compare with the earlier note in the same chapter: Gurudev adding heaps of white butter to parathas for visitors. The generosity toward guests and the insistence on not wasting food are the same teaching from opposite directions — give abundantly to others, waste nothing for yourself. See [[concepts/barkat]].
