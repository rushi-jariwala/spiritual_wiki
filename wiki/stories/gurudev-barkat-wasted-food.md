# Gurudev Eats the Wasted Paratha — On Barkat

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.83–84
**Narrator:** Renu ji (Gurudev's eldest daughter)
**Setting:** The family home during a gathering of disciples and their families

## Full Narrative

> He treated food with a lot of respect. Our house had an unwritten rule that no one would leave food on our plates unfinished. Like most kids, we were fussy eaters, so Mummy had to eat our leftovers. Once, disciples from Himachal Pradesh and Kanpur and their families stayed with us. Someone left a plate with a half-eaten paratha in the sink. Indu didi, in charge of kitchen seva, failed to notice who it was. As Daddy walked into the kitchen, he saw the plate with wasted food. 'Indu, who has left the food?' he inquired. She admitted she did not know but offered to eat it anyway. Daddy picked up the plate and ate the leftovers before she could react. 'Learn to respect food,' he later told us all. 'This is barkat—those who have it, waste it. Ask someone less fortunate what a square meal is worth. We must be grateful for what we have.'

Renu ji adds: *His words made me realise that I must value the barkat of being able to consume a full meal regularly.*

## Teaching

*Barkat* (abundance; the luck of making your buck last longer) is not a passive condition — it is actively sustained or dissipated by how one relates to what one already has. Gurudev's act of eating the leftovers himself, rather than discarding them or assigning the act to someone else, made the teaching indelible: respect for food is not an attitude but a practice.
