# Gurudev's Birth and the Cobra

## Full Narrative

> One spring morning, Gurudev's mother placed her infant son in the crib on the terrace of their house, only to return a few minutes later to find a hooded cobra seated on him. As she stood paralysed with fear, the cobra slid away and disappeared through a crevice in the wall. Unsure of what the cobra sighting meant, Gurudev's parents consulted a temple priest who told them that it was an excellent omen, indicative of the boy's exalted future.
>
> A similar prophecy was made by a sadhu (ascetic) who visited Gurudev's house a few years later. He informed Gurudev's mother that her son would become a powerful saint, 'someone like Shiv', when he turned thirty-five.

— Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, p.8

## Witnessing Greatness — Rashmi ji's Account (Ch.1)

*Witnessing Greatness* attributes the cobra account to Gurudev's sister **Rashmi ji**, who heard it from their mother:

> "As he rested peacefully in a crib on our terrace, a cobra sat atop him, its hood spread wide in a protective stance. The very sight petrified our mother. But before long, the snake retreated, disappearing into the crevice of a wall. Desperate for answers, she sought the wise counsel of a pandit. He interpreted the event as an omen, signalling that my brother was destined for unparalleled greatness."
> — Rashmi ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, p.10

The chapter draws explicit parallels to the births of **the Buddha, Jesus Christ, and Lord Krishna**, whose births were also accompanied by prophetic revelations. The "someone like Shiv at 35" prophecy is preserved.
