# Gurudev Scans Spiritual Children Before Eating

## Full Narrative

> Bittu ji, who observes, "Gurudev saw food as sacred and worthy of respect. He would sometimes bring food to his mouth but then decide against eating. One day, noticing Gurudev's reluctance to eat, I could not help but question him."
>
> Bittu ji: "You have always emphasised the importance of respecting food. Today, you are not adhering to that principle."
>
> Gurudev: "When I am on the verge of eating, are my eyes shut or open?"
>
> Bittu ji: "They are shut."
>
> Gurudev: "Before I take a bite, I shut my eyes and scan to ensure that all my spiritual children have been fed. If even one remains hungry, I find it hard to eat. Their hunger weighs on me."
>
> — Bittu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, "Deception of Perception," p.92.

## Context

Bittu ji had heard Gurudev teach that food deserves respect. When the mahaguru repeatedly brought food to his lips and set it down uneaten, Bittu ji challenged him on the apparent contradiction. The dialogue reveals the opposite of what it seemed: the restraint was not disrespect for food but a refusal to eat while any one of his spiritual children remained hungry. Gurudev's scan before every meal was an act of omni-presence — a silent roll-call conducted with closed eyes before every bite.

Hingori's commentary: *"For him, abstaining from food was not symbolic; it was a heartfelt act of offering his own share to those in greater need. This conviction is admirable, yet difficult to embody. Only someone with immense will can resist physical temptations in pursuit of a higher purpose."*
