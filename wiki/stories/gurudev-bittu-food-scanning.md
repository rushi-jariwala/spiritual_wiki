# Gurudev Scans Before Eating

## Full Narrative

> Bittu ji, who spent many years in Gurudev's company, observes: "Gurudev saw food as sacred and worthy of respect. He would sometimes bring food to his mouth but then decide against eating. One day, noticing Gurudev's reluctance to eat, I could not help but question him."
>
> Their conversation:
>
> Bittu ji: "You have always emphasised the importance of respecting food. Today, you are not adhering to that principle."
>
> Gurudev: "When I am on the verge of eating, are my eyes shut or open?"
>
> Bittu ji: "They are shut."
>
> Gurudev: "Before I take a bite, I shut my eyes and scan to ensure that all my spiritual children have been fed. If even one remains hungry, I find it hard to eat. Their hunger weighs on me."
>
> For him, abstaining from food was not symbolic; it was a heartfelt act of offering his own share to those in greater need. This conviction is admirable, yet difficult to embody. Only someone with immense will can resist physical temptations in pursuit of a higher purpose.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.92–93

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7 (Deception of Perception), pp.92–93

## Context

This episode illuminates the mechanism behind Gurudev's frequent abstinence from food. He would bring food to his mouth but then set it aside — a habit observers noted without fully understanding. His answer to Bittu ji's challenge reveals the practice as a form of real-time empathic scanning: a quiet inner audit of whether his wider spiritual family was fed before he would permit himself to eat.

This was not performative austerity but a sustained act of seva through the body. Renu ji adds a complementary account: Indu Sharma ji (a devoted disciple managing the Gurgaon kitchen) once noticed that Gurudev had not eaten dinner the previous night and urged him to have a hearty lunch. Gurudev's reply: *"Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda."* — When one experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison.
