# Gurudev Closes His Eyes Before Eating — The Scan

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev would go days with just tea and no food. Among those puzzled by this was Bittu ji, who observed: "Gurudev saw food as sacred and worthy of respect. He would sometimes bring food to his mouth but then decide against eating. One day, noticing Gurudev's reluctance to eat, I could not help but question him."
>
> Their conversation:
>
> **Bittu ji:** "You have always emphasised the importance of respecting food. Today, you are not adhering to that principle."
>
> **Gurudev:** "When I am on the verge of eating, are my eyes shut or open?"
>
> **Bittu ji:** "They are shut."
>
> **Gurudev:** "Before I take a bite, I shut my eyes and scan to ensure that all my spiritual children have been fed. If even one remains hungry, I find it hard to eat. Their hunger weighs on me."
>
> For him, abstaining from food was not symbolic; it was a heartfelt act of offering his own share to those in greater need. This conviction is admirable, yet difficult to embody. Only someone with immense will can resist physical temptations in pursuit of a higher purpose.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.91–92

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, "Deception of Perception," pp.91–92

## Context

This dialogue is one of two recorded instances of Gurudev explaining his food abstinence to Bittu ji. The other, from *The Guru of Gurus* pp.106–107, frames it more broadly: *"if any member of my spiritual family anywhere in the world goes to bed hungry, I do not eat that night"* — see [[stories/gurudev-not-eating-hungry-disciples]]. The WG version adds the physical detail of the closed-eye scan, giving the practice a concrete, almost meditative form: a moment of inward sensing before every meal.

Renu ji adds another facet via a memory of Indu Sharma ji: when Indu didi noticed Gurudev had not eaten dinner the previous night, he smiled and said *"Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda"* — one who experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison. See [[stories/gurudev-indu-subzi-mussoorie]].
