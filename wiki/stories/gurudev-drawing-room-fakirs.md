# The Drawing Room for Twenty-Five — "We Are Fakirs"

## Full Narrative

As the family grew and the flow of visitors swelled, Renu ji petitioned Gurudev for more space — a private drawing and dining room. His initial response was characteristic:

> "When we were growing up, Renu didi expressed to Daddy her desire to expand our home with a drawing and dining room, citing the need for more space. Initially, Daddy was hesitant. 'What difference does it make? You can make do with the space you have,' he remarked. Despite his reluctance, she was resolute in her request."
>
> — Alka ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.84

In time, Gurudev agreed — and one additional floor was built. But what the children imagined as a family room, Gurudev immediately recalibrated:

> "Once the room was completed, he looked around and commented, 'It is perfect, and about 20 to 25 people can sleep here comfortably'. This surprised Renu didi. She had imagined the drawing room as a private space for family and house guests, but Daddy saw it as a communal space."
>
> — Alka ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.84–85

> "We are fakirs," he gently reminded Renu ji, emphasising the irrelevance of luxury in their lives.
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.85

> "Renu didi then understood that Daddy always prioritised the needs of the public over personal comforts and quietly accepted his decision."
>
> — Alka ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.85

## Related Concepts / Entities

- [[entities/mahaguru]] — the home is the sthan; personal and public are not separate categories in his world
- [[concepts/vairagya]] — "we are fakirs": vairagya expressed not as renunciation of family life but as indifference to private comfort within it
- [[concepts/grihasth-ashram]] — the householder path practised with genuine non-attachment; space belongs to all
- [[practices/seva]] — communal sleeping arrangements are a structural expression of seva as the home's primary orientation

> [!tip] The episode is a micro-version of the grihasth-ashram teaching. Gurudev did not refuse to expand the house — he accepted the addition while redefining its purpose. The "fakir" declaration is not asceticism; it is a statement about whose needs take precedence.
