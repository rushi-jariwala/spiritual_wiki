# Gurudev Shuts His Eyes Before Eating — Bittu Ji's Question

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev would go days with just tea and no food. This self-restraint was an inherent trait for him, seamlessly integrated into his minimalistic lifestyle, yet it often left those around him puzzled. Among them was Bittu ji, who observes, "Gurudev saw food as sacred and worthy of respect. He would sometimes bring food to his mouth but then decide against eating. One day, noticing Gurudev's reluctance to eat, I could not help but question him."
>
> Their conversation follows:
>
> Bittu ji: "You have always emphasised the importance of respecting food. Today, you are not adhering to that principle."
>
> Gurudev: "When I am on the verge of eating, are my eyes shut or open?"
>
> Bittu ji: "They are shut."
>
> Gurudev: "Before I take a bite, I shut my eyes and scan to ensure that all my spiritual children have been fed. If even one remains hungry, I find it hard to eat. Their hunger weighs on me."
>
> For him, abstaining from food was not symbolic; it was a heartfelt act of offering his own share to those in greater need. This conviction is admirable, yet difficult to embody. Only someone with immense will can resist physical temptations in pursuit of a higher purpose.
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.92–93

### Companion Anecdote — Indu Didi's Concern

> Renu ji shares a touching memory about Indu Sharma ji, a central figure in the Gurgaon kitchen who was as devoted as a daughter to Gurudev: "One day, when Indu didi noticed that Daddy had not had his dinner the previous night, she expressed concern and emphasised that he should have a hearty lunch. With a kind smile, Daddy responded, 'Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda'". In English, this translates to when one experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison.
>
> This sentiment captures the essence of Gurudev's beliefs. Beyond mere thoughts on food, it unveils the heart of his philosophy. For the mahaguru, caring for others always took precedence over personal gratification.
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7 — "Deception of Perception", pp.92–93. Narrators: [[entities/bittu|Bittu ji]]; Renu ji (on Indu Sharma ji).

## Context

Bittu ji's challenge turns Gurudev's own teaching on food respect (see [[stories/gurudev-food-barkat-leftover-paratha]]) back on him. The answer shows the abstention was not a lapse in that respect but its extension: the closed eyes were an inner scan of his "spiritual children". The dialogue is the practical face of the *Guru of Gurus* line, "He who learns to feed others before feeding himself transcends the desire for food" (p.171).
