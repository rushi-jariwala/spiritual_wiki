# Gurudev and the Farm Animals at Khandsa

## Full Narrative

Alka ji recounts an episode that began as a mild family grievance — Gurudev appeared to forget his children's names — and resolved into something that astonished them all:

> "Renu didi was always our spokesperson, petitioning Daddy on our behalf. She would nudge him, saying, 'You really should take your kids on a trip'. On one occasion, she half-jokingly, half-seriously confronted him, 'You only recall our nicknames, not our given names!' Daddy assured her, 'Of course, I know your given names'. Sceptical, Renu didi challenged him, 'Alright then, what are they?' To our surprise, Daddy effortlessly recited our given names and classes. That managed to appease Renu didi, at least for a while."
>
> — Alka ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.81–82

Ila ji then adds the episode that gave this a punchline the family never forgot:

> "Daddy never forgot the names of the farm animals, though. He would take us to Khandsa for a picnic, lead us to the shed and introduce us to the resident cows and buffaloes. He would point to each animal and call them by name, 'Kali. Ganga. Jamuna'. Renu didi would say, 'Daddy, you are just making up those names!' But Daddy assured her that he was not making anything up. To our amazement, he directed his attention to a jet-black buffalo standing in the corner and addressed her by name. In response, the buffalo rumbled as if acknowledging him. He continued to call out to other farm animals nearby, and each responded with distinctive sounds. Despite forgetting our given names sometimes, he knew the names of every farm animal in the shed!"
>
> — Ila ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.82–83

When Gurudev and Mataji travelled to America, the children's distance from their father deepened — and was now literally geographic. Phone calls required a journey:

> "When our parents travelled to America, communication wasn't as easy as it is now. Without a phone at home, we would journey a fair distance at a fixed time every weekend just to talk to them. Chacha ji, our paternal uncle, always accompanied us. After Daddy had inquired about our well-being, Chacha ji would update him on the status of each farm animal, especially the cows and buffaloes. Their conversations sometimes lasted an hour, making us quite impatient. In those days, making a long-distance call was expensive, and their long discussions about animal welfare meant we had to wait even longer for our turn to speak with Mummy. It got frustrating at times. So, we told Chacha ji that he would not get the receiver until we spoke to Daddy and Mummy! But on a serious note, Daddy genuinely loved animals. He cared for their well-being even when he was miles away."
>
> — Ila ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.83
