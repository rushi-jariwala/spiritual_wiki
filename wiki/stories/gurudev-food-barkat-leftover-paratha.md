# Gurudev Eats the Leftover Paratha — On Barkat

## Full Narrative

Renu ji describes the unwritten rule around food in the family home — and the moment that made it permanent:

> "He treated food with a lot of respect. Our house had an unwritten rule that no one would leave food on our plates unfinished. Like most kids, we were fussy eaters, so Mummy had to eat our leftovers. Once, disciples from Himachal Pradesh and Kanpur and their families stayed with us. Someone left a plate with a half-eaten paratha in the sink. Indu didi, in charge of kitchen seva, failed to notice who it was. As Daddy walked into the kitchen, he saw the plate with wasted food. 'Indu, who has left the food?' he inquired. She admitted she did not know but offered to eat it anyway. Daddy picked up the plate and ate the leftovers before she could react."
>
> — Renu ji (Gurudev's eldest daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.53–54

> "'Learn to respect food,' he later told us all. 'This is barkat — those who have it, waste it. Ask someone less fortunate what a square meal is worth. We must be grateful for what we have.' His words made me realise that I must value the barkat of being able to consume a full meal regularly."
>
> — Renu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.54

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6 — "Roots to Wings"

## Related Concepts / Entities

- [[entities/mahaguru]] — acts before speaking; eats the abandoned food himself rather than reprimand a disciple
- [[concepts/barkat]] — the teaching's key term: abundance is not entitlement; wasting it diminishes the blessing
- [[practices/seva]] — Gurudev's liking for feeding visitors ("he made them feel welcome and at ease") stands in direct contrast with wasted food; both are expressions of seva's care for others

> [!tip] Gurudev's gesture — eating the leftovers himself — is more direct than any sermon. He did not ask Indu didi to eat them; he stepped ahead of her. The lesson arrived through action first, words second.
