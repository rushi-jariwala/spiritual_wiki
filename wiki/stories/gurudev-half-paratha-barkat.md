# The Half-Eaten Paratha — Learn to Respect Food

## Full Narrative

> Reflecting further on his values around food, Renu ji explains, "He treated food with a lot of respect. Our house had an unwritten rule that no one would leave food on our plates unfinished. Like most kids, we were fussy eaters, so Mummy had to eat our leftovers. Once, disciples from Himachal Pradesh and Kanpur and their families stayed with us. Someone left a plate with a half-eaten paratha in the sink. Indu didi, in charge of kitchen seva, failed to notice who it was. As Daddy walked into the kitchen, he saw the plate with wasted food. 'Indu, who has left the food?' he inquired. She admitted she did not know but offered to eat it anyway. Daddy picked up the plate and ate the leftovers before she could react. 'Learn to respect food,' he later told us all. 'This is barkat—those who have it, waste it. Ask someone less fortunate what a square meal is worth. We must be grateful for what we have'. His words made me realise that I must value the barkat of being able to consume a full meal regularly."

— Renu ji (Gurudev's eldest daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.83

## Related Concepts & Entities

- [[entities/mahaguru]] — demonstrates respect for food through action before instruction; his teaching is embodied, not merely spoken
- [[entities/mataji]] — held the kitchen and household; the unwritten rule about food was a shared family norm
- [[practices/seva]] — *barkat* (abundance) is not hoarded but shared; wasting it is a severing of gratitude
- [[concepts/non-doership]] — the teaching is framed not as personal deprivation but as awareness of the grace behind every meal

> [!tip] *Barkat* (abundance; the luck of making your buck last longer) is Gurudev's term for the grace that sustains a household. Wasting food is read not as an aesthetic failing but as an indifference to that grace.
