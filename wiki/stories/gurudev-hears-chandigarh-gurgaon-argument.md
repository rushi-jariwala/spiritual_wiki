# Gurudev Hears the Chandigarh-Gurgaon Argument

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev used his sense of hearing as a spiritual tool. He would hear our thoughts and perceive spoken words from great distances. This remarkable ability revealed itself during a road trip I took with my father from Chandigarh to Gurgaon. The journey was fraught with tension, as we argued most of the way. Upon arriving in Gurgaon, Gurudev gently yet promptly admonished me for my behaviour, fully aware of the entire exchange. His vigilance was such that nothing went unnoticed.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.95

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7 (Deception of Perception), p.95

## Context

This incident is one of several accounts of Gurudev's supernormal hearing — his capacity to track conversations happening at a physical distance. Hingori situates it in the broader arc of Gurudev's sensory evolution: as a young man, Gurudev enjoyed music (whistling popular tunes, playing a mouth organ, favouring the songs of Binaca Geet Mala on radio). Over time, his relationship with sound shifted entirely — from listening for pleasure to using hearing as a precision spiritual instrument, capable of reaching across geography to monitor his disciples' conduct.

The admonishment was delivered "gently yet promptly" — the mahaguru's characteristic fusion of correction and care. Nothing about the road trip had been reported to him; the knowledge arrived through the hearing he had refined over decades of practice.
