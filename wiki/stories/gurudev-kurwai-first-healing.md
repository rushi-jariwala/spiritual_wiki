# Gurudev's First Public Healing at Kurwai

## Full Narrative

> In 1973, Gurudev and his team set up camp in the small town of Kurwai in Madhya Pradesh. Nagpal ji, who accompanied the mahaguru on this trip, remembered that a man named Dhanna on whose land the team had set up camp was running a high fever. When he finally showed up after four days, he mentioned his illness in passing. Gurudev sprinkled jal on him, placed a hand on his forehead, and within a minute, Dhanna felt better.
>
> Another man approached Gurudev the next day at Dhanna's behest. He asked for relief from severe abdominal cramps. When Gurudev rubbed the man's stomach, the cramping stopped. From then on, an increasing number of people began to visit the camp, searching for solutions to their problems.
>
> The team moved to Ashok Nagar, 70 kilometres from Kurwai, a few days later. People arrived at the camp in bullock carts to meet Gurudev as word spread about his miraculous touch. Those who came with physical or mental ailments were partially or wholly cured before leaving the campsite.
>
> Many years later, during a conversation with a few disciples, Gurudev mentioned that the Kurwai public healing began with Buddhe Baba's command to perform nisvarth seva. Gurudev was initially hesitant to help and heal a few people he knew had done many wrongs in the past. Buddhe Baba, on the other hand, instructed him to serve without prejudice. And so he did.
>
> Gurudev was thirty-five years old when he performed the first public healing in Kurwai. The prophecy of his emergence as 'someone like Shiv' had come true.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, pp.58–60

## *Witnessing Greatness* Version — Guna (Ch.13, p.179)

> In 1973, a routine soil survey camp in Guna, a small town in Madhya Pradesh, transformed into a remarkable scene of hope, faith, and healing. Initially just an observer, Nagpal ji found himself in the middle of an unexpected event as his colleague and friend revealed an extraordinary ability to heal large groups of people. He recalls, "The camp was flooded with people arriving in bullock carts, each carrying 15–20 individuals, all desperate to experience Gurudev's healing touch. The crowd was as diverse as their issues—some wrestling with personal turmoil while seemingly incurable physical conditions afflicted others. Gurudev tended to them all. Some of those who arrived struggling to walk found themselves able to return home with newfound strength in their steps!"
>
> Nagpal ji was struck by the stark contrast between Gurudev's ordinary life and his extraordinary abilities as a spiritual healer. The events he witnessed compelled him to seek an explanation from the mahaguru regarding the nature of these healings. However, every attempt to unravel the enigma was met with Gurudev's hearty laughter, leaving Nagpal ji's curiosity piqued and the mystery unsolved.
>
> Gurudev's healings in Guna at the age of 35 fulfilled the prophecy that he would become 'someone like Shiv' at that age.
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.13, p.179

> [!warning] Kurwai or Guna?
> *The Guru of Gurus* (pp.58–60) places the 1973 first public healing at **Kurwai**, the bullock-cart crowds at **Ashok Nagar** 70 km away, and the fulfilment of the 'someone like Shiv' prophecy at Kurwai. *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.13 (p.179) names **Guna** for the bullock carts ("15–20 individuals" each) and the prophecy's fulfilment. Same year, same witness (Nagpal ji), same age; the texts do not reconcile the place names.
