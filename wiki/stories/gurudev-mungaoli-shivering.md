# The Mungaoli Camp — Gurudev Stops the Shivering

## Full Narrative

> The mahaguru exemplified remarkable control over his physical sensations. While we layered ourselves to stay warm in the cold winters of the hills, Gurudev's comfort in merely a sweater illustrated his mastery. He further demonstrated this control at his camp at Mungaoli, Madhya Pradesh. There, despite the cold, I initially wore only a T-shirt. However, upon Gurudev's return, apprehensive of his reaction, I quickly put on a sweater, only to find myself shivering uncontrollably. His simple question, "Why did you do that?" miraculously stopped my shivering, showcasing not just his control over his own physical sensations but also his influence on the physical sensations of those around him.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.94–95

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.94–95

## Context

The chapter frames this incident as an illustration of the final sense: touch and temperature. Gurudev's mastery was demonstrated in two ways simultaneously — his own physical comfort in cold that required only a sweater while others layered heavily, and his direct influence over Hingori's body.

The key mechanism is Hingori's *pretence*: he was comfortable in a T-shirt until self-consciousness about Gurudev's judgement made him put on a sweater. The shivering began not from the cold but from the act of concealment. Gurudev's question — "Why did you do that?" — was not a reprimand but a precise diagnosis: the shivering was caused by the pretence, not the temperature. Once named, it stopped.

The narrator reflects: "This experience taught me that our senses, though strong, can be influenced in ways we might not realise. My time with him also made me understand the importance of being genuine and the harsh pitfalls of pretence."
