# The Naming Saga — Santprakash, the Bhrigu Samhita, and the Rain

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev's sister Rashmi ji shares the fascinating story of her grandfather's pilgrimage to Gurudwara Santoksar Sahib in Amritsar, Punjab. The pilgrimage had one goal—to obtain blessings for his daughters-in-law so that they could give birth to boys. A sage at the Gurudwara gifted him two fruits and jal, hinting at the name of a future child: Santprakash. However, the pages of the Bhrighu Samhita whispered a different prophecy. The ancient Rishi Bhrigu foretold the rise of a divya aatma with a different name who would be born in the small town of Hariana in Hoshiarpur, Punjab.
>
> An unusual downpour—a sign seen as auspicious—coincided with Gurudev's birth. Initially intended to be named Santprakash, his grandmother interpreted the rain as a significant omen and chose a name that symbolised mastery over rain—a name also prophesied with remarkable accuracy by Rishi Bhrigu.

— Hingori (citing Rashmi ji), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1, pp.9–10

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.1 "Back To The Future," pp.9–10. ## The Two Competing Prophecies

| Source | Name suggested | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Sage at Gurudwara Santoksar Sahib | **Santprakash** | "Enlightened saint" |
| Bhrigu Samhita | A name beginning with **Raj-** | "King of Kings" (Rajinder; Raj-Indra = master of the rain god) |

The grandmother resolved the tension at the moment of birth — when the unseasonal downpour read as an Indra omen — by choosing **Rajinder**. The Bhrigu Samhita's millennia-old prophecy was fulfilled through a human decision made in real time, not through a divine override.

> [!tip] This is precisely the [[concepts/destiny|destiny mechanism]] in action: the future is not imposed on the present, it is *expressed through* present choices. The grandmother did not know about the Bhrigu Samhita prophecy; she responded to the rain. The two converged because they had to.
