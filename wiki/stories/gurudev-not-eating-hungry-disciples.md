# "If Any Member of My Spiritual Family Goes Hungry, I Do Not Eat"

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev was so focused on seva that he barely slept. He began his day early to meet with patients before heading to the office. He also spent evenings after work attending to those who required his assistance.
>
> He ate whatever was cooked because he had mastered his senses. He avoided wasting food, and even though fresh food was prepared for him, he often ate leftovers from the day before. Four to five times every month, he refused to consume even a morsel of food. Once when he declined dinner for the second consecutive night, Bittu ji accused him of wilfully neglecting his health. Gurudev sat Bittu ji down and said, "Putt, you question me every day about my reason for not eating the food you serve. I may be physically present here in Gurgaon, but if any member of my spiritual family anywhere in the world goes to bed hungry, I do not eat that night!"
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, pp.106–107

## Source

*The Guru of Gurus*, "The Spiritual Compass," pp.106–107

## Cross-Source Variant — *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.7

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.7 (p.92) provides a different framing of the same teaching — with a verbatim dialogue that adds the detail of Gurudev *scanning* before each meal:

> Bittu ji: "You have always emphasised the importance of respecting food. Today, you are not adhering to that principle."
>
> Gurudev: "When I am on the verge of eating, are my eyes shut or open?"
>
> Bittu ji: "They are shut."
>
> Gurudev: "Before I take a bite, I shut my eyes and scan to ensure that all my spiritual children have been fed. If even one remains hungry, I find it hard to eat. Their hunger weighs on me."
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

The GG version emphasises the global reach of Gurudev's empathy; the WG version adds the *eyes-closed scanning* practice — an act of clairvoyant empathy performed before each meal. This is not a ritual abstention but a live real-time check. The two accounts are complementary.

#### The Joy of Feeding Others — Renu ji's Memory of Indu Sharma ji

A related anecdote from the same chapter (Renu ji recalling Indu Sharma ji, a central figure in the Gurgaon kitchen and devoted as a daughter to Gurudev):

> One day, when Indu didi noticed that Daddy had not had his dinner the previous night, she expressed concern and emphasised that he should have a hearty lunch. With a kind smile, Daddy responded: "Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda."
> — Renu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.93

*When one experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison.* The same Punjabi maxim appears in a different context in [[stories/gurudev-indu-subzi-mussoorie]] — there Gurudev divides the last portion of subzi with Indu ji. Both incidents arrive at the same truth; this WG Ch.7 version makes the causal chain explicit: absence of personal hunger as a direct consequence of experiencing the joy of feeding others.
