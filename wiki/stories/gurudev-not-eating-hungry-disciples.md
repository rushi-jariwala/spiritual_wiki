# "If Any Member of My Spiritual Family Goes Hungry, I Do Not Eat"

## Full Narrative

### The Guru of Gurus Version

> Gurudev was so focused on seva that he barely slept. He began his day early to meet with patients before heading to the office. He also spent evenings after work attending to those who required his assistance.
>
> He ate whatever was cooked because he had mastered his senses. He avoided wasting food, and even though fresh food was prepared for him, he often ate leftovers from the day before. Four to five times every month, he refused to consume even a morsel of food. Once when he declined dinner for the second consecutive night, Bittu ji accused him of wilfully neglecting his health. Gurudev sat Bittu ji down and said, "Putt, you question me every day about my reason for not eating the food you serve. I may be physically present here in Gurgaon, but if any member of my spiritual family anywhere in the world goes to bed hungry, I do not eat that night!"
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, pp.106–107

### Witnessing Greatness Version — The Eyes-Shut Dialogue

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.7 gives a richer version of the exchange, including the specific mechanism Gurudev described — a sensory scan conducted with closed eyes before each meal:

> Bittu ji observed: "Gurudev saw food as sacred and worthy of respect. He would sometimes bring food to his mouth but then decide against eating."
>
> One day, noticing Gurudev's reluctance to eat, Bittu ji questioned him:
>
> Bittu ji: "You have always emphasised the importance of respecting food. Today, you are not adhering to that principle."
>
> Gurudev: "When I am on the verge of eating, are my eyes shut or open?"
>
> Bittu ji: "They are shut."
>
> Gurudev: "Before I take a bite, I shut my eyes and scan to ensure that all my spiritual children have been fed. If even one remains hungry, I find it hard to eat. Their hunger weighs on me."
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.91–92

The narrator's commentary: "For him, abstaining from food was not symbolic; it was a heartfelt act of offering his own share to those in greater need. This conviction is admirable, yet difficult to embody. Only someone with immense will can resist physical temptations in pursuit of a higher purpose."

The chapter also records a related memory from Renu ji: Indu Sharma ji — a devoted kitchen figure at the Gurgaon sthan — once noticed that Gurudev had not had dinner the previous night and urged him to eat a hearty lunch. His response:

> *"Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda."*
> (When one experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison.)
> — Gurudev, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92
