# Gurudev Scanning Disciples Before Eating

## Full Narrative

> Bittu ji, who spent years in Gurudev's company, frequently noticed that the mahaguru would sometimes bring food to his mouth but then decide against eating. One day, noticing Gurudev's reluctance to eat, Bittu ji could not help but question him.
>
> **Bittu ji:** "You have always emphasised the importance of respecting food. Today, you are not adhering to that principle."
>
> **Gurudev:** "When I am on the verge of eating, are my eyes shut or open?"
>
> **Bittu ji:** "They are shut."
>
> **Gurudev:** "Before I take a bite, I shut my eyes and scan to ensure that all my spiritual children have been fed. If even one remains hungry, I find it hard to eat. Their hunger weighs on me."
>
> For him, abstaining from food was not symbolic; it was a heartfelt act of offering his own share to those in greater need. This conviction is admirable, yet difficult to embody. Only someone with immense will can resist physical temptations in pursuit of a higher purpose.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

A complementary memory is shared by Renu ji, about Indu Sharma ji — a central figure in the Gurgaon kitchen who was as devoted as a daughter to Gurudev:

> "One day, when Indu didi noticed that Daddy had not had his dinner the previous night, she expressed concern and emphasised that he should have a hearty lunch. With a kind smile, Daddy responded: *'Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda.'*"
> — Renu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

In English: *When one experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison.*
