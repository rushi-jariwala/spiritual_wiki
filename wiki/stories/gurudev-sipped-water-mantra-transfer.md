# "I Have Transferred 10,000 Recitations to You" — Water as Mantra Medium

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev used water in unique ways. He would sometimes sip from a glass of water and then pass the rest to someone else. This act was a way of sharing his spiritual energy and qualities with the other person, sometimes to cure and sometimes to help them on their spiritual journey. For example, he often gave his disciples mantras by sharing his sipped water with them, saying, "I've transferred 10,000 recitations of this mantra to you." Such statements signified that he was imparting some of his extensive spiritual practices, such as mantra chanting, to bolster their spiritual development.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.92–93

## Kamlesh's Poisoned Sherbet

Told in direct contrast to this healing use, the same chapter records:

> A disturbing instance is the case of a young girl named Kamlesh from Gurgaon, who unknowingly drank sherbet mixed with a harmful taweez. The consequences could have been dire if the mahaguru had not intervened in a timely manner.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

The *taweez* (amulet) was dissolved or placed into the sherbet. Gurudev's timely intervention prevented the full damage. The two incidents are placed together deliberately — the same transmissive property of water that Gurudev used for healing could, in the wrong hands, be turned to harm.

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.92–93

## Context

The act of sharing sipped water is intimately personal in Indian spiritual culture — it represents the transfer of the teacher's own body-contact. By using this medium, Gurudev was doing more than sharing mantras in the conventional sense (see [[concepts/mantra-transfer]] for the documented laung-based and paath-hours transfer methods). He was using the water itself as a charged vehicle: his mouth had been in contact with it, encoding it with his energy, intention, and accumulated mantra siddhis.

The figure of "10,000 recitations" was not a metaphor but a literal account: Gurudev was transferring a portion of his own sadhana to the disciple, accelerating their development by decades. This mechanism is consistent with the broader principle of [[concepts/guru-kripa]] — grace that operates through the guru's intention, not just through formal rituals.
