# "We Are Fakirs" — The Drawing Room That Became a Dormitory

## Full Narrative

> "When we were growing up, Renu didi expressed to Daddy her desire to expand our home with a drawing and dining room, citing the need for more space," Alka ji shares. "Initially, Daddy was hesitant. 'What difference does it make? You can make do with the space you have,' he remarked. Despite his reluctance, she was resolute in her request."
>
> In time, Gurudev agreed to the idea, allowing for the addition of one more floor to their modest house. Alka ji continues, "Once the room was completed, he looked around and commented, 'It is perfect, and about 20 to 25 people can sleep here comfortably'. This surprised Renu didi. She had imagined the drawing room as a private space for family and house guests, but Daddy saw it as a communal space."
>
> "We are fakirs," he gently reminded Renu ji, emphasising the irrelevance of luxury in their lives. Alka ji adds, "Renu didi then understood that Daddy always prioritised the needs of the public over personal comforts and quietly accepted his decision."

— Alka ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.85
