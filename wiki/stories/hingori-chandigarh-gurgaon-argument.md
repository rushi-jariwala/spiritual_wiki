# Gurudev Hears the Chandigarh–Gurgaon Argument

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev used his sense of hearing as a spiritual tool. He would hear our thoughts and perceive spoken words from great distances. This remarkable ability revealed itself during a road trip I took with my father from Chandigarh to Gurgaon. The journey was fraught with tension, as we argued most of the way. Upon arriving in Gurgaon, Gurudev gently yet promptly admonished me for my behaviour, fully aware of the entire exchange. His vigilance was such that nothing went unnoticed.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.94

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, "Deception of Perception," p.94

## Context

The chapter places this anecdote within a broader description of Gurudev's relationship with sound. In his youth, he enjoyed music — whistling popular tunes, playing the mouth organ, loving the classic songs from the black-and-white era featured on the radio show *Binaca Geet Mala*. As he progressed spiritually, his relationship with auditory stimuli transformed: from simply enjoying sound, he used his sense of hearing as a spiritual tool. The capacity to hear over long distances — and to hear thoughts — emerged from that transformation.

The Chandigarh–Gurgaon incident is the clearest documented instance of clairaudience across a physical distance: hearing not just words but an entire argument spanning a journey of several hundred kilometres.

This is consistent with [[stories/blank-cassette-disciple]] — Gurudev's cassette-based demonstration of hearing what was never said in his presence — though that account concerns a past conversation already completed rather than a live transmission.
