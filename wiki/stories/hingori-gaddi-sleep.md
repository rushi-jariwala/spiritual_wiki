# Hingori Sleeps on Gurudev's Gaddi

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.137–139

> During one of my long stays in his sthan, Gurudev insisted I sleep on his gaddi every night. I was honoured to do that not realising the implications. I woke up one morning unable to move from the posture I had adopted in my sleep. Although I could see around the room, my eyes were shut. At another time, I could float a few inches within the framework of my body. I soon realised this was no ordinary bed. Another noteworthy experience I had while sleeping there was when I saw Mataji, Gurudev's wife, sitting on the floor breast feeding a child. To my surprise, the child was me as an infant.

**Context:** During a long stay at the sthan, Gurudev specifically insisted Hingori sleep on his gaddi every night — the seat from which he conducted healing seva and which accumulated enormous spiritual energy over years of use. Hingori did not fully understand why until the effects became undeniable:

1. **Immobilisation with open awareness** — woke unable to move, yet could see the room with eyes apparently shut; a state resembling yogic *samadhi* or deep trance without deliberate practice.
2. **Floating** — could float a few inches within the body's physical framework; consistent with accounts of the spirit body (sukshma sharir) slightly separating from the physical.
3. **Vision of infant self** — saw Mataji breastfeeding an infant who turned out to be himself as a baby; a vision across time, possibly indicating the guru's maternal relationship to the disciple's spiritual birth.

The three experiences together demonstrate that the gaddi is not a passive piece of furniture but an active field of accumulated spiritual energy that acts upon whoever rests within it.
