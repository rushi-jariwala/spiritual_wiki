# Hingori Breaks a Tantric Magician's Performance with a Keel

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.145–146

> Another time, while walking on a street in Gurgaon, I came across a magician performing tricks by the kerbside. As I stopped to watch, I heard him tell his audience that the cloth doll by his side would smoke a cigarette without any human intervention. I realised this was a tantric trick and suspected the magician was taking the help of a spirit to perform the act. A thought struck me: what if I encircled the doll mentally with a keel, preventing the spirit from entering the circle and failing to do the magician's bidding, thereby not allowing the doll to perform. I encircled the doll mentally and sure enough, it did not smoke and the magician looked flustered. He tried repeatedly, but to no avail. Not wanting to ruin his show, I broke the keel and left. From the corner of my eye, I saw the doll smoking a cigarette.

**Context:** A street magician in Gurgaon was using a spirit to animate a cloth doll — a tamasic siddhi. Hingori perceived this, drew a mental *keel* (circle of spiritual protection, used here offensively: as a barrier preventing the spirit from entering), confirmed the effect by watching the magician's repeated failures, then consciously reversed it to let the show continue before leaving.

This is the most playful misuse incident in the chapter — benign in effect (the magician was not harmed, the keel was lifted) and motivated by curiosity rather than anger or personal gain. Yet Hingori places it in the same section as the train delays and the bull incident, indicating that even exploratory use of power against someone's act — without their knowledge or harm to anyone — still qualifies as misuse in the tradition's reckoning.

The **keel** appears here as an offensive/containment tool rather than defensive circle. This is its first appearance in the Guru Sutra text in this specific use-case.
