# Hingori Defeats a Mantra Attacker at the Gaddi

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.140–142

> During my early years of seva, while sitting on the gaddi I recall a man sitting in front of me, looking me in the eye and muttering a mantra. While I could not understand what he was trying to do or say, I felt energy being thrown at me. Immediately, I too started reciting a mantra. Soon, I heard an inner voice asking me to switch to the Mahagayatri Mantra while staring deeply into his eyes. As I did, the man's eyes instantly shut and tears gushed out. He then fell at my feet begging for mercy. I realised, I had unwittingly defeated him in a spiritual contest. Since it was based on the throw of spiritual energy from both sides (transmitted through the mantras used), it was definitely not just a friendly match! He swore to never play mischief again and begged me not to punish him. I wondered how someone could even imagine getting into the negative side of spiritualism. Gurudev always taught us the positive use of energy and warned us against wishing somebody ill, for if we did, we would pay the price of a negative deed.

**Context:** During Hingori's early years of conducting seva at the gaddi, a man — apparently a tamasic practitioner — sat before him and initiated a direct mantra-based energy attack under the guise of receiving healing. Hingori perceived the energy being thrown but did not understand its nature. An inner voice (his intuition or inner teacher) guided him to switch to the Mahagayatri Mantra.

The decisive move was the combination of the mantra and unwavering eye contact — channelling the counter-energy directly through the gaze back to the source. The man collapsed in tears and begged mercy.

This is a first-person account of the Mahagayatri used defensively — not as retaliation but as a shield that redirects the attacking force. The Udhav encounter (see [[stories/udhav-sadhus-mantra-contest]]) follows the same structure.
