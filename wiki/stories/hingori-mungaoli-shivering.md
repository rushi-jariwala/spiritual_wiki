# "Why Did You Do That?" — The Mungaoli Shivering

## Full Narrative

> The mahaguru exemplified remarkable control over his physical sensations. While we layered ourselves to stay warm in the cold winters of the hills, Gurudev's comfort in merely a sweater illustrated his mastery. He further demonstrated this control at his camp at Mungaoli, Madhya Pradesh. There, despite the cold, I initially wore only a T-shirt. However, upon Gurudev's return, apprehensive of his reaction, I quickly put on a sweater, only to find myself shivering uncontrollably. His simple question, "Why did you do that?" miraculously stopped my shivering, showcasing not just his control over his own physical sensations but also his influence on the physical sensations of those around him.
>
> This experience taught me that our senses, though strong, can be influenced in ways we might not realise. My time with him also made me understand the importance of being genuine and the harsh pitfalls of pretence.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.95–96

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, "Deception of Perception," pp.95–96

## The Teaching

Hingori's interpretation makes the lesson explicit: the shivering was not caused by the cold — it was caused by the pretence. The moment he put on the sweater out of apprehension rather than genuine need, the pretence triggered the very discomfort he was hoping to avoid. Gurudev's question, "Why did you do that?" cut through the pretence, and the shivering stopped.

The story operates on two levels simultaneously:
1. **Sensory mastery** — Gurudev was comfortable in a sweater while others layered up; his control of physical sensation was not performance but attainment.
2. **Authenticity over pretence** — Hingori's body responded not to the temperature but to the inauthenticity. The guru's influence extended to the physical sensations of those around him.
