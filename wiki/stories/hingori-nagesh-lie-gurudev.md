# Hingori Lies to Gurudev About Nagesh

## Full Narrative

> During one of my visits to the soil survey camps where Gurudev spent 30-40% of his time working, Gurudev's cook, Nagesh asked me for monetary help for his sister's wedding. Since he was short of funds, I gave him INR 11,000 and felt good about it as the money was going to someone who was in service of my guru. In giving him money, I had fulfilled a duty towards my guru. Or, so I thought.
>
> A few weeks later Gurudev asked me if I had given money to Nagesh. By then, I had been made aware that Nagesh was collecting money despite being forbidden by Gurudev to do so. Gurudev intended to fund a part of the wedding himself and had therefore forbidden him to collect money from others. When Gurudev was informed about my monetary contribution, he questioned Nagesh who blatantly denied it. If I confessed, Nagesh would have been in big trouble. To safeguard him, I decided to lie. Gurudev quizzed me numerous times and in diverse ways but I lied every time.
>
> Some days later, while on a morning run, I stepped into a small ditch and twisted my foot. Even though I was in agony, every time I went to meet Gurudev, I tried to mask my pain as I did not want to appear vulnerable. About three days later, still in agony, I sat in a corner of the sthan. On his way to work, Gurudev asked me to join him on his drive to office. Once again, he asked me if I had given any money to Nagesh. This time I confessed, since I knew he was already aware of it. His words to me were, "You must never lie to your guru. Never!".
>
> I could sense forgiveness in his eyes. He asked one of his older disciples to cure my sprain. Soon after, the pain vanished.
> — Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.12, pp.121–122

## Source

*Guru Sutra*, Ch.12, "Guru Awelna," pp.121–122.

## Notes

The story illustrates both guru awelna and its resolution. Hingori's original act — giving money to Nagesh — was done with good intent: to help someone in service of his guru. But Nagesh had been explicitly forbidden to collect money, and Hingori then compounded the situation by lying to Gurudev repeatedly.

The twisted foot arrived without announcement, days after the lie. Hingori's instinct to mask the pain from Gurudev — "I did not want to appear vulnerable" — is a further layer of the same concealment, stopping only when Gurudev persisted in the car ride.

The cure was as immediate as the forgiveness: Gurudev asked a senior disciple to address the sprain and the pain vanished on the spot. The sequence — awelna, consequence, genuine confession, forgiveness, healing — is precise and unambiguous.

> [!tip] The punishment was not the guru's doing; Hingori's inner consciousness had squared off the samskar of guilt through three days of agony. Gurudev's role on the other side was the same: forgiveness without drama, followed by healing. This is the model Hingori presents for how guru awelna typically resolves when the disciple genuinely confesses.
