# Hingori's Raft Vision — Introduced to Buddhe Baba

## Full Narrative

> My confusion about Buddhe Baba's identity only deepened when I had a powerful vision in which Gurudev guided me to a riverbank where a raft was moored. Upon it stood a divine being wearing a green toga with a distinct square-shaped face that hinted at an ethereal nature. The way Gurudev acted around this figure indicated that this being was of a higher order, even superior in some spiritual aspect. As we boarded the raft, Gurudev reverently offered the only seat to the divine figure accompanying us. He and I stood still as the raft glided seamlessly across the water. The journey unfolded in silence, and I felt I was on that raft because Gurudev wanted to introduce me to this divine being.
>
> Was this figure the elusive Buddhe Baba? Was he a powerful saint of yore? Was he Sitaram ji of Benaras? Amidst various possibilities, certainty eludes me.
>
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, p.27

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2 — "Invisibly Yours, Buddhe Baba"

- [[concepts/astral-travel]] — the vision may be an out-of-body or dream-state encounter with astral-plane reality

> [!question] Hingori does not confirm this was Buddhe Baba. The visual description — green toga, square-shaped face, ethereal quality — matches neither the "elderly white-clad figure with flowing white beard" seen by Renu ji, Puran ji, and Rashmi ji, nor any of the other descriptions. The figure's *higher spiritual order than Gurudev* is, however, consistent with the Buddhe Baba relationship. See [[entities/buddhe-baba]] — Identity section.

> [!tip] The detail that Gurudev *offered the only seat* to this being is architecturally significant. Gurudev, who himself refused more garlands than his guru wore (see [[entities/buddhe-baba]] — Testimonies section), enacts the same hierarchy of deference in Hingori's vision. Gurudev's reverence is the data: whoever this figure is, Gurudev placed him above himself.
