# Hingori at Bayri — The OM on the Shivling and the Face He Could Not See

## Full Narrative

> Let us turn back the clock to the events surrounding my first visit to Renuka. The graphic of an OM appeared whenever Vashisht ji poured water over the Shivling at the sthan. I, too, saw it while visiting Bayri with a few others. But what my fellow travellers saw that I could not was an image of Gurudev on it. Feeling disappointed yet unwilling to accept something simply because others did, I left the scene in quiet disagreement.
>
> We stopped for lunch a little later at the home of Karamchand ji, another disciple. We sat on the mud floor and were served lunch on leaf plates. I cleaned the floor before setting the plate down. When I picked up the plate after lunch, I saw a perfect graphic of the OM inlaid on the ground below it. Though I decided not to tell my host, I took it as a sign and ran back to the sthan to take another look at the Shivling. Vashisht ji had already left, so I jumped over the compound wall and asked the Shivling to show me what I had not seen before. What I saw next was nothing short of astonishing! Despite my complete disbelief, an outline of a face appeared instead of the OM and remained there. The Shivling did not need my faith to showcase its power, but this incident demonstrated how spiritually charged the place is.
>
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.13, pp.189–190

## Three Decades Later

> After more than three decades, I recently guided groups of disciples and devotees to Renuka, inviting them to experience and honour the sacred energy of the place. Many witnessed the OM symbol appearing on the Shivling when incense was lit around it. It is also believed that Gurudev, along with some of his disciples, had meditated in the hills of Renuka in a previous life, and their past-life samadhis may be still present there.
>
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.13, p.190

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.13 — "Training Trails," pp.189–190. The Shivling is the neglected one Bittu ji restored on Chandramani Vashisht ji's land at the start of the Renuka camp (p.188; see [[stories/gurudev-renuka-parshuram-alliance]]).
