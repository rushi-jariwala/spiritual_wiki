# The Strawberry Seller Who Knew Hingori's Name

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.127–128

> I vividly recall meeting a stranger, an evolved being, who randomly told me that I was fortunate to get pre-cooked bread and did not have to bake my own. Though, I had met him once prior to this incident, he seemed to know a lot about me and seemingly had come to test me (sometimes celestial beings appear to assess people on their spiritual progress, while making them aware of their shortcomings). I am not sure how I scored on the test, but he certainly left me spooked.
>
> A few weeks prior to the incident, I had seen the same stranger on the street as he navigated through the traffic to reach my car to sell strawberries. While trying to sell me his produce, he mentioned he had been waiting to meet me since he saw me in his dream. It sounded bizarre. Why would a strawberry seller whom I had never encountered, see me in his dream? Anyway, as I bought the fruit and asked him to keep the change, he blessed me with immense wealth.

**Context:** The stranger appeared first as a strawberry seller who sought out Hingori's car through traffic, having seen him in a dream. Weeks later the same man reappeared and told Hingori he was "fortunate to get pre-cooked bread and did not have to bake my own" — a metaphor for receiving a guru's pre-digested spiritual knowledge rather than having to acquire it independently through a lifetime of practice.

Hingori frames this person as an "evolved being" or possibly a celestial being sent to assess his progress and make him aware of his shortcomings. The encounter introduces the chapter's theme: spiritual power does not have to be earned from scratch when a genuine guru transmits it directly.

The "pre-cooked bread" metaphor is one of the most vivid in the corpus for the privilege of a siddh guru's grace: centuries of spiritual distillation served ready to receive.
