# Humsa Ji's Two Encounters with Augarh

## Full Narrative

> Augarh continues to test the third and fourth generation of the mahaguru's disciples in his unique, shape-shifting ways.
>
> Humsa ji shares a compelling experience: "I was at a shop outside Gurudev's samadhi in Najagarh, arranging for flowers and organising food distribution for visitors and passersby. While directing the shopkeeper, a beggar approached from behind. He had a wheatish complexion, curly hair, and piercing brown eyes. Despite his modest height, his presence was striking. He demanded assertively, 'Khaana de (Give me food).' His voice resonated with authority. When I offered him a packet of wafers, he rejected it decisively, saying, 'Ye nahin chahiye (I don't want this)'. He insisted again, 'Khaana de.'
>
> "My instincts signalled there was something extraordinary about him. The shopkeeper suggested fetching chole kulche from the food stalls outside the nearby Shirdi Sai temple. I consented and requested him to fetch two servings of chole kulche for the man. The beggar seemed satisfied upon hearing this, but as I turned towards the samadhi with the flowers, he spoke again, 'Ab mujhe aage le chal (Now take me ahead)'. I looked at him and replied, 'Please take the food and let me proceed to the samadhi'. After paying my respects and leaving the samadhi, I asked the shopkeeper if the beggar was a regular sight around there. He responded, 'I have never seen him before.'"
>
> Later, Humsa ji discussed this encounter with her guru, who laughed and revealed, "That was Augarh testing you". Anxious, she asked, "Did I fail or pass?" Her guru responded reassuringly, "You passed the test."
>
> Humsa ji recounts another encounter she believes was her second meeting with Gurudev's enigmatic disciple, Augarh. She shares, "As I stepped out of the Mumbai sthan after seva on a Saturday, accompanied by two other sevadaars, Roshini ji and Srimohi ji, I noticed a beggar approaching us. He was dragging a pristine white sack, and his unbuttoned shirt was remarkably clean for someone scavenging the streets. As he smiled at me, he extended his hand, offering a fresh banana. There was something electric about him. Though I cannot be entirely certain, my instinct told me that the figure standing before me was none other than Augarh ji."
>
> The man exuded an energy that was impossible to ignore. Roshini ji recalls, "Though he appeared as a beggar, there was something about him—a peculiar energy that I felt almost immediately. It was so intense that I instinctively began chanting the Mahagayatri mantra, unable to comprehend what I was experiencing fully. Only later, when we discussed the encounter, did I realise that both Humsa ji and Srimohi ji had also felt the same palpable energy."
>
> Humsa ji continues, "I felt compelled to give him something to eat, so I turned towards the sthan to fetch some biscuits. But after a few steps, I realised that no biscuit packets were left in the kitchen. Then I remembered an unopened box of biscuits in my bag and decided to offer it to him. As he stood there, still holding the banana out to me, I held out the box of biscuits, creating a momentary stalemate between us."
>
> Roshini ji adds, "Faced with the impasse, I took the biscuits from Humsa ji's hands and requested him to accept it and go his way. He took the box with a smirk, juggled it for a moment, and then slowly walked away."
>
> When Humsa ji later shared the encounter with her guru, he remarked that according to the Brighu Samhita, bananas would be served to visitors at Gurudev's sthans. Humsa ji reflects, "Since that incident, bananas are offered as prasad to those who visit the Mumbai sthan."
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.8, pp.113–115
