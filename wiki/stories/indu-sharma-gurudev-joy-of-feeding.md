# Indu didi's Concern and the Joy of Feeding Others

## Full Narrative

> Renu ji shares a touching memory about Indu Sharma ji, a central figure in the Gurgaon kitchen who was as devoted as a daughter to Gurudev: "One day, when Indu didi noticed that Daddy had not had his dinner the previous night, she expressed concern and emphasised that he should have a hearty lunch. With a kind smile, Daddy responded, 'Jennu khellaan che mazaa aa gaya na, khaane da mazaa nahin rehnda'."
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92

In English: *When one experiences the joy of feeding others, the pleasure of feeding oneself pales in comparison.*

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, pp.91–92

## Context

This is a second occasion in the *Witnessing Greatness* record where Gurudev uses the same Punjabi phrase. The earlier occurrence (narrated in *The Guru of Gurus*, pp.34–35, and told from Renu ji's point of view on a trip to Mussoorie) captures the teaching through a meal shared with Indu ji at the kitchen in Mussoorie. This WG Ch.7 version is Renu ji's memory of a different moment — at the Gurgaon sthan, after Gurudev had skipped dinner the night before. The context here is not abundance shared but *self-denial in service*: Gurudev did not simply feed others, he withheld food from himself in order to give.

The narrator observes: "This sentiment captures the essence of Gurudev's beliefs. Beyond mere thoughts on food, it unveils the heart of his philosophy. For the mahaguru, caring for others always took precedence over personal gratification."
