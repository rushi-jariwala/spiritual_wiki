# Kapill Ji's Dream — Gurudev Opens a Gurubhai's Fourth Eye

## Full Narrative

> Kapill Malhotra ji, who oversees a sthan in the serene hill station of Lonavala near Mumbai, recounts a remarkable experience that affirms the subtle and mysterious nature of spiritual guidance: "I once dreamt of accompanying Gurudev and Guruji (Gurudev's disciple) on a walk, along with one of my gurubhais. As we were walking, I observed Gurudev placing his right hand on the soft part of my gurubhai's head, a spot referred to as the talvi."¹
>
> When their eyes met, Gurudev smiled at Kapill ji and revealed, "Maine iska kaam kar diya hai (I have done his work)", which Kapill ji instinctively understood as Gurudev opening his gurubhai's fourth eye.
>
> Unlike the third eye located on the forehead, the fourth eye is said to be at the back of the head. Its activation is likened to the gentle tearing of thin tissue paper, bestowing the unique ability to perceive from behind. Gurudev, interestingly, often opted to close the third eye of individuals, a measure intended to protect them from the overwhelming distractions associated with glimpsing into the past, present or future. As a guide of unparalleled wisdom, he took measures to shield his disciples from the potential pitfalls of these abilities, ensuring their spiritual journey progressed smoothly and without impediments.
>
> ¹ Soft part of the skull at the back of the head; the location of the crown or Sahasrara chakra
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10, pp.145–146

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.10 (Peerless Mentorship), pp.145–146. The chapter carries a photograph captioned: "Kapill ji watches as Gurudev activates his gurubhai's fourth eye."

## Notes

- The **fourth eye** here matches Hingori's own account in *The Guru of Gurus* (pp.151–153): "something like a thin paper tore at the back of my head… I could suddenly see what was behind me." Both locate it at the back of the head and both use the image of thin paper tearing. See [[concepts/stages-of-spiritual-transformation]] (Divya Gyan).
- The **third eye** half of the passage — Gurudev "often opted to close the third eye" — is the pattern of [[stories/hingori-third-eye-opened-closed]], where Hingori says "Mine was not a unique case, as he did this to most of his disciples."
- "Guruji (Gurudev's disciple)" is the same unnamed figure who appears in the Ch.9 healing testimonies ([[stories/kanika-bhalla-clot-vanished]], [[stories/rishabh-gopiganj-vision-gratitude]]).
