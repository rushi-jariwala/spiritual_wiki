# Kapill Ji's Keels — The Delhi Hotel and the Dehradun Room

## Full Narrative

> Kapill ji recounts a similar experience: "On one of our trips to Delhi, we stayed in a hotel that seemed low on occupancy despite its prime location. One night, as I slept, a menacing, towering figure loomed in my dream, threatening my gurubhai, sleeping nearby. The next morning, I confided in Guruji (Gurudev's disciple), who advised me to visualise the entity and trap it within a keel using the Mahagayatri mantra. This did the trick," he says with a smile. "I could never book a room in that hotel after that because its occupancy had touched the roof!"
>
> He continues, "I recall another incident. Guruji (Gurudev's disciple) had left his energised handprint in a room in Dehradun. But one night, a gurubhai was attacked by an aggressive force while resting in that very room. Angered by this, Guruji summoned me to secure the room with a keel, trapping the harmful spirit. Subsequently, when Guruji entered the room, the negativity was dispelled in mere minutes!"
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, pp.159–160

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11 (Sacred Syllables), pp.159–160. Photograph caption: "Kapill ji uses the Mahagaytri mantra to create a keel."

## Notes

- **Keel** is defined in the chapter's footnote (p.158): "Energy boundaries to contain or neutralise negative energies within a given space. Additionally, they function as protective barriers, efficiently shielding areas, individuals, and objects from negative influences within a specified radius." Here the keel **traps** the spirit — containment first, then clearing.
- The story follows Hingori's keel around Nandlal's shop ([[stories/nandlal-shakti-ball]]), introduced by "Kapill ji recounts a similar experience."
