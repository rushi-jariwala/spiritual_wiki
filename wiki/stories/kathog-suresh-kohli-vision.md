# Suresh Kohli's Seven Nights of Vision at Kathog

## Full Narrative

> Suresh Kohli, a teacher at the school across the dorm, found out about Gurudev from Thapa. Suresh ji dismissed Thapa's words, assuming they were spoken under the influence of alcohol Thapa consumed regularly. Nonetheless, Suresh ji's curiosity got the better of him, and he set out to meet the man whose miracles had captivated the small town.
>
> Gurudev greeted him warmly and poured him a cup of tea. During the ensuing conversation, Suresh ji asked Gurudev if he could help him meet the supreme power. Gurudev instructed him to meditate at night with a glass of water kept by his side.
>
> Suresh ji recalled seeing a vision of Gurudev in his home temple while sitting in meditation that night. The image appeared for seven nights in a row. However, nothing happened on the eighth night.
>
> On the ninth day, he returned to meet the mahaguru. Gurudev greeted him with the words, "Hun mainu ess tarah vekhiya Masterji, te samajhna rab nu paa lita!" (From now on, whenever you see a vision of me, know that you have met the supreme power). Suresh ji recognised Gurudev as more than a saint and bowed at his feet.
>
> As the crowds at Kathog swelled, Gurudev began seva at the school premises to accommodate the large number of people who came. When he needed more hands-on-deck, he initiated several teachers from the same school, like Suresh ji, Shambhu ji, and Santosh ji, into seva by making them drink the jal he had sipped. Over the next few days, men with no practical knowledge of spiritualism (in this lifetime) became spiritual healers.
> — Hingori, *The Guru of Gurus*, pp.74–76

## *Witnessing Greatness* Version (Ch.13, pp.182–183)

> Suresh Kohli ji, a school teacher who transferred to Kathog in March of the same year, recounts his first encounter with Gurudev. As he shares his story, he speaks of a local tantric named Thapa, who lived near the school. Thapa was the bearer of news about a man who had established camp across the school premises, bearing the sacred symbol of the OM on his hand and chest, along with a trishul. Suresh ji doubted Thapa's words, suspecting the claims were merely drunken ramblings. However, curiosity got the better of him, and he decided to visit the camp. There, at the gate, stood Gurudev—a man with a "commanding presence". Gurudev not only confirmed Thapa's assertions but also revealed the OM symbol embossed on his right hand.
>
> Suresh ji recalls their conversation: "I asked Gurudev for a cure for tonsilitis and an opportunity to meet God. He advised me to meditate at night with my eyes open and a glass of water nearby. That night, I saw Gurudev's moving image in front of my almirah, which was filled with photographs of deities. I was stunned. I had just met him and had no prior devotion to him. Yet, there he was, appearing in my sacred space. I had so many questions!"
>
> This extraordinary vision continued for seven nights. On the eighth night, however, the image did not appear. Suresh ji approached Gurudev the next day, asking, "What happened last night? I did not see you."
>
> Gurudev replied, "If you see me that way again, you will have met God". Suresh ji remembers feeling excited, realising, "This is the God I was looking for. I have finally found him!"
>
> He elaborates on the impact of the mahaguru's presence on Kathog, noting, "Despite Kathog's small size, thousands and thousands flocked to meet Gurudev. The influx of people was so huge that we ran out of kadas, cloves, cardamoms, and peppercorns. Miracles were a common sight. However, our activities drew attention, and someone complained to the administration that the school was neglected because the teachers were busy doing seva.
>
> "This led to an inspection by the deputy director of education, who was surprised to find no classes in session. When he confronted me, I introduced him to Gurudev. Gurudev helped him with some personal issues he faced. Afterwards, the deputy director reported that the school was functioning normally, and even he became a follower of Gurudev."
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.13, pp.182–183

New in this telling: the request was for a cure for **tonsilitis** as well as to meet God; meditation "with my eyes open"; the vision "in front of my almirah, which was filled with photographs of deities"; the camp running out of kadas, cloves, cardamoms and peppercorns; the **deputy director of education** who came to inspect and became a follower. The illustration is captioned "Suresh Kohli ji sees a vision of Gurudev in front of his almirah" (p.183). Gurudev's words differ in form — *GoG*: "From now on, whenever you see a vision of me, know that you have met the supreme power"; *WG*: "If you see me that way again, you will have met God" — but agree in sense.
