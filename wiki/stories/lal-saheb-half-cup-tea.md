# Lal Saheb's Initiation — The Half-Cup of Tea

## Full Narrative

**Source:** Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, pp.53–55

Lal Saheb's entry into the sthan was marked by nonchalant curiosity rather than any pressing quest — setting his story apart from most disciples who came seeking healing or guidance.

A self-described average Indian-Canadian, his life was filled with social festivities until a chance meeting at a temple piqued his interest in a guru visiting Toronto.

> "I had no pressing issues and no ailments. Merely a whim led me to Gurudev's doorstep."
> — Lal Saheb, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, p.53

### Days of Waiting

Following an impromptu decision, Lal Saheb found himself in a seemingly endless waiting game that tested his patience over several days. His bemusement with the concept of a guru was about to take a turn.

On the **third day** of his persistent visits, he received **half a cup of tea** — a gesture he initially thought of as a mistake. Nonetheless he drank it. Later, when called upon by Gurudev, he found himself agreeing to undertake seva, a concept alien to him at the time.

Though Lal Saheb was unaware at the time, the half-consumed tea was the medium through which Gurudev imparted a part of his spiritual essence.

### The Jyot at Dawn

Months later, a visit to Gurgaon subjected Lal Saheb to a similar pattern of wait and brief encounters, culminating in Gurudev's cryptic instruction to be present when Mataji lit a *jyot* (sacred flame) at the sthan at dawn on the first day of the new year.

These interactions — though fleeting — marked the beginning of Lal Saheb's transformation into a devout disciple.

### Preordained from Toronto

Hingori adds a telling detail: Gurudev had set his sights on Lal Saheb even before setting foot in Toronto. Hingori was privy to Gurudev's expressed intent to travel there to give someone *seva*. The ensuing events were a testament to faith and patience — virtues that Lal Saheb unknowingly exhibited, which led to his spiritual awakening.

> Each disciple's path to the mahaguru was uniquely tailored yet perfectly woven into a preordained spiritual assembly.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, p.55
