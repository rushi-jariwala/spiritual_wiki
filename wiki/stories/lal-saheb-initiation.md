# Lal Saheb's Initiation — The Half Cup of Tea

## Full Narrative

> Lal Saheb's initiation into the sthan was marked by a nonchalant curiosity rather than a quest for solutions, setting his story apart from the rest. As a self-described average Indian-Canadian, his life was filled with social festivities until a chance meeting at a temple piqued his interest in a guru visiting Toronto. Following an impromptu decision, Lal Saheb was embroiled in a seemingly endless waiting game that tested his patience over several days.
>
> "I had no pressing issues and no ailments. Merely a whim led me to Gurudev's doorstep," he recalls. His bemusement with the concept of a guru would soon take a turn. On the third day of his persistent visits, he received half a cup of tea, a gesture he initially thought of as a mistake. Nonetheless, he drank it, and later, when called upon by Gurudev, he found himself agreeing to undertake seva, a concept alien to him at the time. Though Lal Saheb was unaware, the half-consumed tea was the medium through which Gurudev imparted a part of his spiritual essence.
>
> Months later, a visit to Gurgaon subjected Lal Saheb to a similar pattern of wait and brief encounters, culminating in Gurudev's cryptic instruction to be present when Mataji lit a jyot at the sthan at dawn on the first day of the new year. These interactions, though fleeting, marked the beginning of Lal Saheb's transformation into a devout disciple.
>
> Gurudev had set his sights on Lal Saheb even before setting foot in Toronto, a fact I was privy to when he expressed his intent to travel there to give someone seva. The ensuing events were a testament to faith and patience, virtues that Lal Saheb unknowingly exhibited, which led to his spiritual awakening.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, pp.53–55
