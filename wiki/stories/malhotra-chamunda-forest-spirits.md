# Malhotra Ji and the Chamunda Mantra in the Forest

## Full Narrative

> Gurudev had a knack for showcasing the effectiveness of the mantras he imparted to his disciples using highly creative methods to demonstrate their power. This was particularly evident in an incident with his first disciple, RC Malhotra ji, where he showcased the efficacy of the Chamunda mantra.
>
> In the early 1970s, in a remote camp setting, Gurudev led Malhotra ji on a journey through a dense forest. As they ventured deeper, Malhotra ji began to perceive ethereal beings emerging from the shadows, stirring a sense of fear within him. Eventually, he found himself alone in the forest as Gurudev had seemingly vanished. The mahaguru had hidden behind a tree to watch his disciple's reaction. Faced with the advancing ethereal figures, Malhotra ji recalled Gurudev's teachings and began to mentally chant the Chamunda mantra with intense fervour. As the mantra's vibrations filled the air, the ghostly entities began to retreat before they disappeared from view. This experience, masterfully orchestrated by Gurudev, was an exhibition of the Chamunda mantra's extraordinary capability to ward off seen and unseen spiritual threats, solidifying its role as a powerful tool for spiritual protection.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, pp.149–150

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11 (Sacred Syllables), pp.149–150.

## Notes

- The chapter follows the story with its gloss on the Chamunda mantra: "a Shakti mantra dedicated to Goddess Chamunda, a wrathful form of Goddess Durga… slaying the demons Chanda and Munda, from whom she derives her name. She symbolises protection from and the destruction of negative energies." (p.150)
- The mantra is chanted **mentally**, as Gurudev taught (see [[concepts/mantra-transfer]] — Ajapa Japa).
- Gurudev hiding behind a tree to watch the disciple's reaction is the same pattern as the unannounced examinations of [[synthesis/tests-in-disguise]].
