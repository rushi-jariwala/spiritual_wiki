# Malhotraji Misuses Power — Trains and a Bull

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.144–145

## The Train Delays

> Gurudev's first disciple, Malhotraji experimented with power by misusing it, just as I did. He would often miss his train returning home late from work. He discovered, by using the power he was granted, he could will the train to be delayed, and, in fact, did so on several occasions. When Gurudev found out about this misuse of power, he was upset and reprimanded him.

## The Bull and the Fruit Cart

> On another occasion, Malhotraji, inquired about the price of fruit from a vendor near his home. The vendor was rude, and asked why he wanted to know the prices since he usually never bought any fruit. Angered by his rudeness, Malhotraji willed a bull standing close by, to attack the fruit cart and overturn it. He succeeded, but this was a failure in the eyes of Gurudev who paid the vendor for his damaged goods and threatened to withdraw Malhotraji's power if he misused them again.

**Context:** Malhotraji — Gurudev's first and most senior disciple — fell into the same trap as Hingori and others: using power for personal convenience and as a response to petty anger. Delaying a train is a use of power that disrupts an entire system of people for personal benefit. Willing a bull to attack a fruit cart punishes a rude vendor through an animal and a bystander's property.

Gurudev's response in the fruit cart case is particularly instructive: he does not withdraw power immediately — he pays the vendor's loss (making the material consequence right) and then issues the warning. The guru makes the harm good before addressing the disciple. The threat to withdraw power is the ultimate deterrent — and was apparently sufficient.

These episodes are narrated alongside Hingori's own misuse stories as proof that the temptation to misuse is universal among early disciples — not a character flaw specific to one person, but a predictable consequence of the intoxication power produces.
