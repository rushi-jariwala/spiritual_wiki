# Nakul Malhotra Ji's Guru Bilocates Between Sthan and Hospital

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, p.39–40

## Full Narrative

> Nakul Malhotra ji, a Mumbai-based sevadaar, shares a mystifying experience that unfolded during his visit to the sthan in Bandra on a Bada Guruvar. At that time, his mother was hospitalised at Bombay Hospital in Marine Lines. After offering his respects to his guru (Gurudev's disciple), Nakul ji set off for the hospital. A few minutes later, he received an unexpected call from his father. "Guruji just visited your mother at the hospital," his father shared. "He looked in through the door, smiled, and left." Confused, Nakul ji responded, "It can't be. I was just with him at the sthan moments ago." His father was adamant, asserting, "I am certain it was him." Given that the travel time between Bandra and Marine Lines typically takes about 40 minutes, even without traffic, this bewildering occurrence left Nakul ji utterly amazed.
> — Nakul Malhotra ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, p.39–40

## Context

The precision of the account is its strength: Nakul ji had just been at the sthan himself, so the time-gap between the two sightings can be measured against Bombay's traffic realities. Even under optimum conditions, 40 minutes separate Bandra from Marine Lines — making the simultaneous presence physically impossible. The casual nature of the hospital visit — looking in, smiling, leaving — suggests not a dramatic intervention but an act of quiet, compassionate oversight.

The story parallels the KL Bakshi ji / Sunil Ghai bilocation (Gurudev in London while seated in Gurgaon) documented in the same chapter, and the earlier accounts in *The Guru of Gurus* where Gurudev appeared at a hilltop temple while remaining in his office. See [[concepts/supernature]] and [[concepts/astral-travel]].

Both the Goa story ([[stories/ashish-khanna-goa-bilocation]]) and this account involve a disciple of Gurudev bilocating — confirming that the capacity propagates through the lineage.
