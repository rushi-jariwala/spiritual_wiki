# Nitu Ji and the Bichoo Booti — Nature Has a Cure

## Full Narrative

During summer camp visits to Himachal Pradesh, Gurudev's children accompanied him on tour for a few weeks. On one such visit, Nitu ji — Gurudev's youngest son — encountered the Bichoo Booti (Poison Ivy):

> "One incident during our visit to Daddy's camp in Himachal Pradesh stands out in my memory. While playing near the road, I accidentally brushed against something with my hand as I made way for a herd of passing donkeys. Little did I know then that it was a plant called Bichoo Booti. The brush against this shrub brought intense redness, pain, and swelling to my hand. Unaware of the plant's effects, I rubbed my hand in distress, only worsening the pain. Seeking comfort, I ran to Daddy's room."
>
> — Nitu ji (Gurudev's youngest son), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.82

> "Concerned, Daddy inquired, 'What happened to you, Pandit ji?' I explained the incident through tears, not understanding why he kept asking questions instead of offering immediate healing. But Daddy remained composed, asking for more details and investigating the incident thoroughly. Finally, he suggested, 'Let us go to where this happened.'"
>
> — Nitu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.82–83

> "I led Daddy to the spot, hoping for relief from the unbearable pain. He identified the Bichoo Booti as the culprit and pointed to its roots as the cure. He gently applied the plant's roots to my hand, and I felt immediate relief. 'Son, nature has a cure for everything,' he said, 'all you have to know is where to look for it.'"
>
> — Nitu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.83

Alka ji adds a parallel episode that shows the same teaching taking root in adulthood:

> "I used to get sick quite often as a kid. When I went to Daddy, he would bless cardamoms and cloves and give them to me. I also took medicines prescribed by the local doctor. 'Learn to heal yourself,' he remarked one day. I had no idea what he meant. Years later, after Daddy had moved on, I was experiencing severe pain in my arm, and his words began to ring in my ears. I focused on my arm for a minute or two while chanting my mantras. Though the pain subsided, I assumed it was temporary relief and that the pain would return. However, when the pain did not return for another 15 days, I was confident that I was completely healed. I realised what Daddy meant — if we have self-belief, we can heal ourselves."
>
> — Alka ji (Gurudev's daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.83
