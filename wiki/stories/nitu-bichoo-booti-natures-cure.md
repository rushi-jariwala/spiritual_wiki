# Nitu Ji and the Bichoo Booti

**Source:** Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.82–83
**Narrator:** Nitu ji (Gurudev's youngest son)

> During the summer holidays, the family would accompany Gurudev on tour for a few weeks. During one such visit to his camp in Himachal Pradesh, Nitu ji was playing near the road when a herd of donkeys passed by. In making way for them, his hand brushed against a plant — Bichoo Booti (Poison Ivy). The contact brought intense redness, pain, and swelling. Unaware of the plant's effects, he rubbed his hand in distress, worsening it.
>
> "Seeking comfort, I ran to Daddy's room.
>
> "'Concerned, Daddy inquired, 'What happened to you, Pandit ji?' I explained the incident through tears, not understanding why he kept asking questions instead of offering immediate healing. But Daddy remained composed, asking for more details and investigating the incident thoroughly. Finally, he suggested, 'Let us go to where this happened.'
>
> "I led Daddy to the spot, hoping for relief from the unbearable pain. He identified the Bichoo Booti as the culprit and pointed to its roots as the cure. He gently applied the plant's roots to my hand, and I felt immediate relief."
>
> "Son, nature has a cure for everything," he said, "all you have to know is where to look for it."

## What It Illustrates

Gurudev addressed Nitu ji by his usual affectionate name, **"Pandit ji"** — the same name he gave his son as a marker of their past-life connection (Gurudev had confided that his own father would reincarnate as their son). His response to the crisis was not an immediate laying-on of hands. He **investigated**: asked questions, understood the cause, then led Nitu ji back to the scene. The cure was the plant's own root — nature's counter to its own sting.

The method is incidental. The lesson is embedded in the approach: slow down, observe, trace the cause, and the answer will be in the same territory as the problem. The healing teacher does not reach for an external solution; he looks at what is already present.

> [!tip] Contrast with Alka ji's later realisation: "Learn to heal yourself." Gurudev planted a seed of self-sufficiency in both children through *different* incidents — teaching Nitu ji to trust nature, and teaching Alka ji to trust herself. Both teachings point to the same direction: the cure is never far from the cause, and requires neither panic nor dependence. See [[concepts/intent-as-healing]].
