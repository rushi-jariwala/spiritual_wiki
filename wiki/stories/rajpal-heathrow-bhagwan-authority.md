# Rajpal Ji at Heathrow — The Question of Authority

## Full Narrative

> Several years ago, in the departure lounge of London's Heathrow airport, Rajpal ji asked Gurudev whether Bhagwan was upset by the great guru's ability to change, erase, and rewrite people's destinies. The mahaguru explained to his disciple that Bhagwan was pleased with him for curing his fever and removing his illness. Seeing the perplexed look on Rajpal ji's face, he added, "Bhagwan is within each being. Bhagwan has given me the stature of a guru and the authority that comes with it. I exercise this authority so that the sick can be healed."
> — *The Guru of Gurus*, p.225

## Source

*The Guru of Gurus*, Healing — Approach to Healing, p.225.

## Notes

The question Rajpal ji raises is a genuinely difficult one: does a guru who changes destinies overstep a divine boundary? Gurudev's answer resolves it on two levels. First, he frames the power as delegated rather than self-arrogated — Bhagwan gave him the stature and the authority. Second, he locates Bhagwan *within each being*, dissolving any external hierarchy between the healer's act and divine will. The guru is not bypassing Bhagwan; he is acting as Bhagwan's instrument within the authority Bhagwan has conferred.

This connects directly to [[concepts/non-doership]] — the authority belongs to Bhagwan; Gurudev is merely the conduit.

## *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.9 — The Full Dialogue (p.139)

> In an exchange with Rajpal ji, the mahaguru delved into the philosophical depths of spiritual healing and the role of a guru in altering trends of destiny.
>
> Gurudev said, "Many approach me, frustrated with ailments that persist despite numerous treatments. What do I do? I reconfigure their fate; I relieve their suffering, for I am their guru."
>
> Curious, Rajpal ji inquired, "By altering the course set by the divine, don't you fear displeasing God?"
>
> Gurudev answered confidently, "Quite the opposite—it delights God immensely."
>
> Perplexed, Rajpal ji questioned further, "But if you are altering God's plan, wouldn't that upset him?"
>
> With a gentle laugh, Gurudev explained, "Not at all, my son. By healing someone, I am soothing the divine presence within them. In this way, I am also alleviating God's own distress. This same divine force has given me the title of Guru and endowed me with the power to heal. God does not meddle in my work; I am in control here. Who will provide solace to those suffering if I do not step in?"
>
> Gurudev's words often went beyond the grasp of conventional understanding. His insights into spiritual healing highlighted deep synergies between the healer's energies and those of the person being healed. His approach involved focusing on healing the divine essence in beings and altering the trends of fate to bring them relief. In this holistic view, healing went beyond physical, mental, or emotional repair to become a restorative act that brought balance to the collective consciousness.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.9, pp.139–140

Ch.9 does not name Heathrow. It adds "I reconfigure their fate," "it delights God immensely," healing as "alleviating God's own distress," and "God does not meddle in my work; I am in control here."

> [!warning] "I am in control here" vs. "I am not the doer"
> *The Guru of Gurus* (p.227): "I don't cure anyone who comes for help. They cure themselves. I am not the doer." Ch.9: "God does not meddle in my work; I am in control here." Both passages ground the authority in the divine ("This same divine force has given me the title of Guru"), but the self-description differs sharply. See [[concepts/non-doership]] and [[synthesis/conditions-of-healing]].
