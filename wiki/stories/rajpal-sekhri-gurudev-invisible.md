# Gurudev Walks Through a Crowd Unseen

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, p.40–41

## Full Narrative

> Bindu Lalwani ji recalls two experiences shared by her father, Rajpal Sekhri ji: "Gurudev and Papa travelled from Gurudev's office in Connaught Place to his home in Gurgaon. A large crowd had gathered outside the gate of Gurudev's home. 'Gurudev, there are so many people to meet you. It will take us hours to get to your room,' Papa said. 'They will stop us only if they see us,' Gurudev remarked casually. Papa was perplexed hearing this, but what happened next was unbelievable!" she exclaims. "Gurudev exited the car and began walking briskly towards the gate. No one stopped him or Papa, and they entered the house, proving that he could choose to make himself and anyone accompanying him fully invisible!"
> — Bindu Lalwani ji (narrating Rajpal Sekhri ji's account), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, p.40–41

## Context

Highly advanced yogis, Hingori explains in this chapter, can transform the molecular structure of their bodies to prevent light from reflecting off them — and thereby vanish from sight. The crowd outside Gurudev's Gurgaon home was large and presumably contained many who knew his face well. The casual remark — *"They will stop us only if they see us"* — makes the invisibility sound almost incidental, an unremarkable capacity to be deployed as practical necessity.

The scope extends to any companion: Rajpal ji walked through the crowd equally unseen. This detail corroborates what is documented when Hingori accompanied Gurudev into the Badrinath temple — also walking in and out unnoticed through well-managed priestly queues. See [[concepts/supernature]].

The same capacity was later found in disciples and their downlines — as when Ashish ji became partially invisible at the Kazakhstan lakeside (see [[stories/ashish-ji-kazakhstan-partial-invisible]]).
