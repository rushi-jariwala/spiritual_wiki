# Gurudev Invisible at the Gate — and at Badrinath

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, pp.40–41

## Full Narrative

### The Gate — Rajpal Sekhri Ji

Bindu Lalwani ji recalls two experiences shared by her father, Rajpal Sekhri ji.

A large crowd had gathered outside the gate of Gurudev's home in Gurgaon. When they arrived together, Rajpal ji remarked that it would take hours to get through to his room. Gurudev responded casually:

> "They will stop us only if they see us."

Gurudev exited the car and began walking briskly toward the gate. No one stopped him or Rajpal ji. They entered the house unobstructed, "proving that he could choose to make himself and anyone accompanying him fully invisible."

### Badrinath Temple

Hingori records his own experience:

> "In a spontaneous moment, while visiting the renowned Badrinath temple, I convinced Gurudev to come along. Despite the long queues meticulously managed by the priests, we glided in and out of the temple's precincts completely unnoticed."

— *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, pp.40–41

## Context

Hingori frames these accounts within a broader explanation of the mechanism behind invisibility:

> "Highly advanced yogis can transform the molecular structure of their bodies, effectively stopping light from reflecting off them and, thus, vanishing from sight."

The transformation is deliberate and precise — Gurudev chooses who is invisible with him (both Rajpal ji and Hingori pass through unnoticed alongside him). Compare [[stories/gurudev-invisible-srinagar-shop]] — where Gurudev was invisible to a shop owner who worshipped his photograph — for a context in which the invisibility serves a different purpose (allowing the disciple to purchase goods at market rate).

The Badrinath account is Hingori writing in the first person as a participant — one of the few places in *Witnessing Greatness* where he inserts himself as a witness rather than a narrator of others' experiences.
