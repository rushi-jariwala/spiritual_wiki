# Rashmi Ji's Grief and Buddhe Baba's Apparition

## Full Narrative

> During a session in which Gurudev imparted wisdom to his disciples, he mentioned that the true depth of his teachings would only be understood after he was 'gone'. Rashmi ji, who overheard this, misinterpreted his words as a forewarning of his imminent departure, which plunged her into deep despair.
>
> Recounting the experience, she says, "The thought of losing my brother left me grief-stricken, and I found myself weeping on the floor of his empty bedroom. Then, a luminous white light materialised, revealing an older man dressed in white with long flowing white hair. Seeing my distress, he gently encouraged me to eat. When I refused, he inquired about my reluctance."
>
> Overcome with emotion, Rashmi ji replied, "How can I eat when my brother will no longer be with me?" The compassionate figure reassured her, "Your brother is not leaving you; he is embarking on a soil survey tour and will return in a few months." With that, the being faded away.
>
> Finding solace in the older man's words, Rashmi ji regained her composure and ate her food. Upon Gurudev's return, she shared her experience with him, convinced that the serene figure wrapped in white light was none other than her brother's enigmatic mentor, Buddhe Baba.
>
> — Rashmi ji (Gurudev's sister), narrated in *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.2, pp.29–30

> [!tip] Buddhe Baba's role here is pastoral rather than disciplinary — he appears spontaneously to comfort a distressed family member without any invocation. The access to Gurudev's private life (knowing he is going on a soil survey tour, not departing permanently) suggests the guide operates with full knowledge of the mahaguru's plans.

> [!tip] Rashmi ji's misunderstanding is itself significant: Gurudev said his *teachings* would only be understood after he was gone. The ambiguity between physical departure and metaphysical departure runs through the chapter as a leitmotif — his presence outlasts his body.
