# Ravi Ji's Past-Life Visions — The Shahjahanpur Mansion and the Himalayan Cave

## Full Narrative

**Source:** Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, pp.51–52

Ravi ji, reflecting on the depth of the disciple-guru bond:

> "The guru shoulders the duty of guiding his disciples, binding his evolution to theirs. He cannot progress until they do; they are in his charge, and he cannot forsake them mid-journey. Gurudev made it clear that he returned to this life to advance his spiritual family beyond the point they had reached in their past lives."

When Ravi ji asked Gurudev to recount their past-life connections, Gurudev deflected with characteristic mystique:

> "My son if I were to simply recount your past, where would be the wonder in that? Recite the Mahaguru mantra as you drift into sleep and find out for yourself."

### The Shahjahanpur Mansion

Ravi ji did as instructed. During the mantra, he was transported back approximately 150–170 years to a grand mansion with twenty or more rooms, where Gurudev would visit and reside for months:

> "It was a place visited by those who are my gurubhais today."
> — Ravi ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, p.51

He drew the mansion and presented the sketch to Gurudev, who confirmed it stood in **Shahjahanpur, Uttar Pradesh**. Curiosity fuelled a verification journey: Malhotra ji went to Shahjahanpur with the sketch. The sketch matched the physical location, except that the courtyard — once paved with mud — had since been replaced with cement.

### The Himalayan Cave

When Ravi ji probed deeper into the nature of their spiritual relationship, Gurudev again urged him to seek answers from within. Persevering in meditation, Ravi ji was transported further back — to the mountains of North India. He saw his past self:

> ...an ascetic of old age, meditating in caves with Gurudev and a few gurubhais. The clarity of the vision was striking, from the pattern of his blanket to the cave that was Gurudev's sanctuary.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, p.52

Through these visions, Ravi ji understood that his connection with the mahaguru and his gurubhais was not woven in a single lifetime but across *centuries* of shared devotion and spiritual pursuit.
