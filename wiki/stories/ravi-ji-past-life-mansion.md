# Ravi Ji's Past-Life Mansion and Cave Visions

## Full Narrative

> After pondering the depth of the disciple-guru bond, Ravi ji approached Gurudev with a request: "I am eager to understand the nature of our past-life connections." Gurudev responded with a touch of mystique: "My son if I were to simply recount your past, where would be the wonder in that? Recite the Mahaguru mantra as you drift into sleep and find out for yourself."
>
> "While doing the mantra, I was transported back 150–170 years to a grand mansion, a dwelling with 20 or more rooms where Gurudev would visit and reside for months," Ravi ji recounts. "It was a place visited by those who are my gurubhais today."
>
> He drew the mansion and presented the sketch to Gurudev, who confirmed it stood in Shahjahanpur, Uttar Pradesh. Fuelled by curiosity, Malhotra ji went to Shahjahanpur with Ravi ji's sketch. There, the congruence between Ravi ji's dreamt memories and the present reality was affirmed, except that the courtyard, once paved with mud, had since been replaced with cement.
>
> When Ravi ji probed further into the nature of their spiritual relationship, Gurudev urged him to seek the answers within. Persevering in meditation, Ravi ji was transported even further back in time to the mountains of North India in a vision. There, he saw his past self, an ascetic of old age, meditating in caves with Gurudev and a few gurubhais. The clarity of the vision was striking, from the pattern of his blanket to the cave that was Gurudev's sanctuary.
>
> Through these visions, Ravi ji understood that the threads of his and his gurubhais' association with the mahaguru were not woven in a single lifetime but were part of a continuous spool, stretching back through centuries of shared devotion and spiritual pursuit.
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, pp.51–52
