# Ravi Trehan Ji Sees Gurudev in Shiv's Form

**Source:** *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3 — Impossible Is Possible, p.35–36

## Full Narrative

> Ravi Trehan ji remembers, "One day, I asked Gurudev, 'You are not who you appear to be. I would like to see you in your spiritual form.' In a dream a few days later, I saw him seated on tiger skin on the floor of a cave. A snake was wrapped around his neck and arms, ash was rubbed all over his body, and a trishul was placed nearby. The form resembled Lord Shankar, but his face belonged to Gurudev. I did not tell Gurudev about my experience when I met him the next day, but when he saw me, he put his finger to his lips and implied that I should keep quiet about it."
> — Ravi Trehan ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.3, p.35–36

## Context

The iconography of the dream is precisely that of Shiv in his Mahayogi aspect: tiger skin, serpent ornament, ash-covered body (bhasma), trishul. Gurudev's face on Lord Shankar's form confirms the chapter's recurring assertion that Gurudev is an *ansh* of Shiv — a manifestation rather than a mere devotee of Shiv.

The wordless instruction at their next meeting — finger to lips — demonstrates Gurudev's omniscience. He did not need to be told about the dream; he already knew. And his gesture of silencing Ravi Trehan ji illustrates the mahaguru's habitual concealment of his own nature: see [[concepts/supernature]] (Kept Under Wrap section) and the related story [[stories/blank-cassette-disciple]].

A strikingly similar dream was reported by Raj Laxmi ji (Guddan ji) of Kanpur, who saw Gurudev "seamlessly shifting his form between himself and Lord Shankar, back and forth, in a continuous transformation" — suggesting the Shiv-Gurudev identity is not occasional but the baseline nature beneath the ordinary exterior.
