# Remanika Tandon — The Kada in the Dream

## Full Narrative

> Remanika Tandon, a young devotee from the sthan in Baroda, Gujarat, recounts an episode of spiritual healing through a dream. "After spending four days in Haridwar with my mother's best friend's family, I returned home only to fall ill," she explains. "I was suffering from high-grade fever, body aches, cold, throat pain—symptoms that suggested a viral infection. Medication had no effect, which led me to seek help at the sthan. To my amazement, the fever and body ache vanished almost immediately, though the headache, cold, and cough lingered."
>
> Her mother, Bhavna ji, a sevadaar at the Baroda sthan, noted additional concerns about her daughter's condition. "The most troubling aspect was Remanika's behaviour; it was not normal," she shares. "She felt something crawling up her legs, harboured aggressive thoughts, and withdrew from communication entirely; she even stopped responding to her close friends. Devraj Khare ji, a sevadaar who does healing seva, advised that she spend a night at the sthan. She was surprisingly hesitant to stay overnight, a stark contrast to her usual comfort with the place," she adds.
>
> After some initial resistance, Remanika was persuaded to enter the sthan. "It took some effort to get her through the gate. Once inside, she slowly began to find her peace, though she was not entirely herself," Bhavna ji recounts. "About an hour later, she fell into a deep sleep despite heavy breathing and a lingering headache."
>
> That night, a significant dream offered Remanika relief. "At around 3 am, I dreamt of Gurudev entering the room and walking twice around my bed, visible only by his feet," she shares. "He then seated himself on the gaddi at the sthan. Moments later, my mother entered the room, and Gurudev disappeared, leaving behind a gift on the gaddi. My mother picked up a copper kada with a golden sheen, decorated with a peacock feather and a black pearl, and presented it to me, telling me it was from Gurudev. The moment I wore the kada, I awoke, realising it was all a dream."
>
> Bhavna ji continues, her voice filled with gratitude, "All her troubling physical symptoms had disappeared—her aches and pains were gone, her breathing stabilised, and her mind became clear. Completely healed, she returned to sleep, feeling well."
> — Hingori, Remanika Tandon and Bhavna ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.9, pp.130–131
