# Renu Ji's Confrontation and the Teaching on True Love

**Source:** Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.79–80
**Narrator:** Renu ji (Gurudev's eldest daughter)

---

As the crowd seeking Gurudev's help and blessings swelled outside their home, Renu ji experienced a growing distance from her father. Their moments together grew increasingly scarce — sometimes stretching ten to fifteen days, or an entire month, with barely a word exchanged. Hearing her classmates speak of time spent with their fathers made the absence heavier.

> "I mustered the courage one day to tell him that he did not have time for us and that Mummy was everything to us. I told him we could not survive without her. He locked his stare on me for two minutes. I expected him to be annoyed with me. Instead, he said, 'I do not know how to convey affection like your mother. One day you will realise, and then you will understand what true love is.'"

---

Hingori reflects on what Gurudev meant:

> Explaining the meaning of true love to a young child can be like describing a colour they have never seen. Love is more than an emotion. It is like radiation permeating everything, unrestricted by boundaries. It should envelop everyone like a fragrance, not just a select few.

---

Renu ji's reflection, shared after Gurudev's passing:

> "It was only in the aftermath of his passing that I began to grasp the depth of his concept of love. Perhaps, had I realised it while he was in his physical form, I would not have felt like I do now. I now understand what guru kripa is, how the guru imparts his wisdom, and how his influence encompasses all."

---

## What It Reveals

Gurudev did not defend himself. He did not offer Renu ji a promise or an explanation of his constraints. He acknowledged the feeling directly — *"I do not know how to convey affection like your mother"* — and then shifted the frame entirely: not "I love you despite the distance" but "you will one day recognise what this love looks like."

His love was of the kind that does not differentiate: it encompassed everyone who came to him with the same completeness that it encompassed his children. From a child's perspective, this looks like absence. From the other side of the mahaguru's passing, it looks like encompassing grace.

> [!tip] The contrast between Mataji's **intimate, directed love** and Gurudev's **non-differentiating love-as-radiation** is the Shiv-Shakti dynamic enacted within family life. Neither form is lesser — they are complementary. See [[concepts/grihasth-ashram]] and [[wiki/analogies/analogies]] (Shiv and Shakti analogy).
