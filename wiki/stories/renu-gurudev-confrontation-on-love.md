# Renu Ji Confronts Gurudev About Love

## Full Narrative

As the eldest of Gurudev's five children, Renu ji felt his absence most acutely. With an ever-swelling crowd outside the home and stretches of 10 to 15 days — sometimes an entire month — with barely a word exchanged between father and daughter, the distance weighed on her:

> "I mustered the courage one day to tell him that he did not have time for us and that Mummy was everything to us. I told him we could not survive without her. He locked his stare on me for two minutes. I expected him to be annoyed with me. Instead, he said, 'I do not know how to convey affection like your mother. One day you will realise, and then you will understand what true love is.'"
>
> — Renu ji (Gurudev's eldest daughter), *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, pp.79–80

The narrator — likely Hingori, himself a father of disciples — reflects on what Gurudev meant:

> Explaining the meaning of true love to a young child can be like describing a colour they have never seen. Love is more than an emotion. It is like radiation permeating everything, unrestricted by boundaries. It should envelop everyone like a fragrance, not just a select few.
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.80

Renu ji did not understand until after Gurudev's passing:

> "It was only in the aftermath of his passing that I began to grasp the depth of his concept of love. Perhaps, had I realised it while he was in his physical form, I would not have felt like I do now. I now understand what guru kripa is, how the guru imparts his wisdom, and how his influence encompasses all."
>
> — Renu ji, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.6, p.80

## Related Concepts / Entities

- [[concepts/guru-kripa]] — Renu ji's retrospective understanding connects the delayed love to guru kripa: the guru's grace extends beyond what the disciple can perceive in the moment

> [!tip] The exchange captures a structural pattern in Gurudev's teaching: his ek vakyas were often experienced as cold comfort or non-answers at the time of delivery. Their meaning arrived later — sometimes after his death — triggered by life itself. Renu ji's own words confirm the pattern: understanding came "in the aftermath of his passing."
