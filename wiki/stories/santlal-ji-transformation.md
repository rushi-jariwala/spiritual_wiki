# Santlal Ji — From Bureaucratic Arrogance to Disciplehood

## Full Narrative

> Santlal ji's transformation from a life of bureaucratic arrogance to spiritual enlightenment is a tale of stark metamorphosis. Initially, he was far from what one might consider a potential disciple, yet he evolved into an exemplar of disciplehood.
>
> He recounts his pursuit of a spiritual guru: "In the heart of Punjab lies Gangowal, where I heard of a wise sadhu. My first encounter with him was anticlimactic; he asked me to return the next day. Disappointed but persistent, I requested an early meeting, only to be asked to return every day for five days. I waited for hours in long queues to meet him. On the fifth day, the sadhu confessed that he could not take me under his wing, for my spiritual allegiance lay elsewhere—with someone present at the time in the same town, no less!"
>
> Years passed before fate finally guided Santlal ji to the guru he sought. A business journey brought him to Gurgaon, where chance encounters ushered him to meet the mahaguru. They met at midnight, and Gurudev's simple lungi-clad appearance took Santlal ji aback, challenging his preconceived notions of a guru's grandeur. He admits, "I was sceptical because I had trouble believing that a man so simple could be a guru. I asked him if he had visited Gangowal in 1960, and he said he had. I wondered whom he had met, and he perfectly described the sadhu I had met."
>
> During their first meeting, Gurudev shared an important insight: the journey to finding a guru is not driven by the disciples' search but by the guru's intuitive selection of his disciples. He also revealed the remarkable breadth of his bond with Santlal ji, which extended across nine previous incarnations. Additionally, he mentioned that the blessings Santlal ji's mother bestowed upon her son had brought forward their destined encounter by two years!
> — Hingori, *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.4, pp.52–53
