# Surinder Kaushal Ji Sees Gurudev Performing Namaaz

## Full Narrative

> To Gurudev, the various spiritual paths were like the fingers of a single hand—each unique, yet all integral to the whole. He recognised the harmony among these different traditions and shared alliances with many of the saints who established them.
>
> In a testament of oneness, Surinder Kaushal ji, a disciple from Chicago, shares, "Our sthan in Chicago is visited by a diverse congregation—Gujaratis, Punjabis, Americans, and even Muslims of Pakistani descent. Once, a young Muslim girl plagued by troubles approached the sthan, wondering if she could perform namaaz there. Without hesitation, I assured her she could. A remarkable experience followed some days later. On the eve of Mahashivratri, I had a vision of Gurudev doing namaaz in my kitchen. Startled, I asked him about this practice, to which he replied that many souls connect with him in this very manner. Noticing my curiosity about him facing north during the prayer—contrary to the common practice of facing the Kaaba—he gently explained that the essence of prayer transcends the direction in which one bows."
>
> Before meeting Gurudev, Surinder ji had a dream featuring Guru Gobind Singh. In his vision, Guru Gobind Singh was seated on a tiger skin, his long hair spread out, with a kamandal and a rudraksh mala, facing west. Gurudev interpreted this vision: "Guru Gobind Singh embodies the essence of Shiv."
>
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.8, pp.106–108
