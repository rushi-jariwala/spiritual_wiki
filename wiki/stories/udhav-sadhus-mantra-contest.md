# Udhav's Mantra Contest with the Sadhus

**Source:** Hingori, *Guru Sutra*, Ch.13, pp.142–143

> Udhav, one of Gurudev's younger disciples, experienced a spiritual attack. He was at the sthan at Gurudev's house when a jeep rolled in with some sadhus (ascetics). They asked for Gurudev and demanded several kilograms of rice and tins of cooking oil for a feast they were planning for their clan. On being informed that Gurudev was unavailable, they decided to wait until their demands were met. In defiance, their leader came and sat opposite Udhav and made piercing eye contact. Udhav stared back into his eyes while mentally reciting the Mahagayatri Mantra. It turned out to be a clash of egos and the gruff sadhu not being able to withstand the energy onslaught from Udhav, fainted, and his team beat a hasty retreat.

**Context:** The sadhus arrived at Gurudev's sthan demanding provisions — using implied spiritual intimidation as leverage. When Gurudev was unavailable, their leader attempted direct energy projection via piercing eye contact against Udhav, the junior disciple holding the fort. Udhav held the gaze and used the Mahagayatri Mantra mentally — the same technique that defeated the attacker in [[stories/hingori-mantra-contest]]. The sadhu fainted; the group fled.

The episode illustrates that even "sadhus" can weaponise spiritual energy for material coercion. Udhav's calm response — he neither argued nor capitulated — is the application of exactly the training Gurudev's disciples receive. A junior disciple defeats a gang of sadhus through a single mantra and steady gaze.

> [!tip] Udhav may be the same person as "Uddhav ji" who appears in [[stories/uddhav-ji-black-triangles]]. The spelling differs across chapters but the pattern — a younger disciple of Gurudev tested in extreme circumstances — is consistent.
