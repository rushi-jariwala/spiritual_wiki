# Guru Vashisht Fragments the Mahagayatri Mantra

## Full Narrative

> The mantra's discovery is attributed to Brahmarishi Vishwamitra, who, through intense and prolonged meditation, was bestowed with its divine revelation, making him both its originator and guardian—however, an intriguing turn of events led to Guru Vashisht fragmenting this mantra. Ravi Trehan ji explains the backstory: "The Mahagayatri mantra, found in the Rig Veda, was once chanted in its entirety by both deities and demons, granting immense spiritual power to both factions.
>
> "While the deities utilised this mantra for noble purposes, the demons exploited it for their evil goals. This misuse by the demons, especially their acts of desecrating the sacred fire rituals of the deities with bones and flesh, prompted the divine beings to seek a resolution to curb their growing menace. Distressed by the threat to their spiritual progression, the deities sought Lord Brahma's intervention. Directed from him to Lord Vishnu, and subsequently to Lord Shankar, they were eventually advised to seek help from Guru Vashisht.
>
> "Upon their arrival at Guru Vashisht's humble dwelling, a significant revelation awaited them. Guru Vashisht, engrossed in reading the Vedas, instantly recognised the divine assembly's purpose, as foretold by the page he was reading: the arrival of the deities alongside the accompanying trio of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shankar, signified the time to fragment the Mahagayatri mantra.
>
> "Guru Vashisht revealed that starting with the following generation, only a siddh guru could impart the full and original Mahagayatri mantra to the disciple, and this transmission would occur in utmost secrecy," Ravi ji shares.
>
> He concludes the tale by highlighting the outcome: "Thus, the mantra was divided, with the familiar Gayatri Mantra emerging as one part and the remaining eight words discreetly dispersed throughout the four Vedas, concealed from the world at large."
>
> Regarding the mantra's deeper essence, Ravi ji recalls Gurudev's words: "Mahagayatri is more than just a mantra; it embodies the spirit of the four Vedas. Charged with energy, it grants tangible and spiritual rewards to those who chant it. One can break free from the endless loops of birth and death by invoking its power."
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11, pp.153–154

## Source

*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.11 (Sacred Syllables), pp.153–154. Narrated by Ravi Trehan ji.

## Notes

- The chapter introduces the mantra thus: "a sacred verse from the Rig Veda… Its composition follows the Gayatri metre… 24 syllables divided into three lines, each with eight syllables. This mantra is a potent call to the solar deity Savitur, seeking illumination of the intellect as one progresses towards self-realisation." (p.153)
- **Eight words** — the same count as the "eight missing words" Buddhe Baba gave Gurudev at their first meeting in the forest (*The Guru of Gurus*; see [[entities/buddhe-baba]], [[concepts/mantra-transfer]]). Read together: Vashisht decreed that only a siddh guru could impart the full mantra, in secrecy — and that is how it reached Gurudev.

> [!question] Rig Veda verse vs. words dispersed across four Vedas
> The mantra is called "a sacred verse from the Rig Veda" and "found in the Rig Veda," while its eight concealed words are "dispersed throughout the four Vedas." The chapter does not reconcile the two.
