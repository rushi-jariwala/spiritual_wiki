# Feeding Others Before Self

*Synthesis date: 2026-09-28. Sources available: Karma Sutra, Aatma Sutra, Guru Sutra, The Guru of Gurus, Witnessing Greatness (Ch.1–7).*

## The Pattern

Across two biographical volumes, Gurudev's relationship to food follows one consistent rule: **the other is fed first; the self eats last, or not at all.** What looks like an austerity of taste turns out, in every account, to be an act of seva. Mastery of the tongue is the by-product, not the aim.

## The Mechanism

1. **Taste is not suppressed; it is outgrown.** Ch.7 insists the 1970s–80s shift was "not as a shift from pleasure to aversion, but to achieve mastery over his senses." Raji Sharma ji notes he still had "a soft spot for certain snacks such as gol-gappas" but "was not driven by cravings." This matches the observer-not-actor stance of [[concepts/senses-management]].
2. **A greater pleasure replaces a lesser one.** The Indu didi line names the mechanism directly: the joy of feeding others makes the pleasure of eating "pale in comparison." Desire is not fought; it is out-competed.
3. **The body's hunger is re-routed to the disciples' hunger.** "Their hunger weighs on me." The same empathy that took on disciples' karma in healing (see [[concepts/empathy]]) here takes on their hunger.

> [!tip] The inversion
> Ordinarily, fasting is a private discipline for one's own spiritual gain. In Gurudev's practice the fast is turned outward: it is "a heartfelt act of offering his own share to those in greater need" (*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.7, p.92). The same outward turn is what [[concepts/ulta-guru]] describes in the guru–disciple relationship.

> [!question] *The Guru of Gurus* describes a daily routine of lassi for lunch and nimbu pani for breakfast; *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.7 describes days of "just tea and no food". These may describe different periods of his life; the sources do not reconcile them.

> [!tip] Food as the examiner's medium
> In *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.8, Augarh's tests of disciples repeatedly turn on food — "Khaana de," a vada pav thrown on the street, a banana held out against a box of biscuits. Gurudev fed the other first; Augarh checks whether his disciples do. See [[synthesis/tests-in-disguise]].
