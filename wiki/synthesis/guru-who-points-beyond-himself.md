# The Guru Who Points Beyond Himself

*Date: 2026-09-28. Sources available: Karma Sutra, Aatma Sutra, Guru Sutra, The Guru of Gurus, Witnessing Greatness Ch.1–10.*

## The Pattern

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.10 gathers, in a few pages, a set of sayings in which Gurudev turns the disciple's attention away from himself:

| Saying | Witness | What it redirects |
|---|---|---|
| "Tu kaagaz bhi maathe pe lagaa ke usko de degaa, toh woh theek ho jaayega" | Hingori, p.141 | From the herbs to the healer's own aura — "trust in myself more than the herbs and plants" |
| "Our true accomplishment would be us surpassing his achievements" | Santlal ji, p.146 | From the guru's attainment to the disciple's |
| "Refrain from idolising me. Focus instead on transcending the act of worship." | Renu ji, p.147 | From worship of the guru to going beyond worship |
| "You can become Shiv yourself" | Dr Shankarnarayan ji, p.147 | From the guru's divinity to the disciple's |
| "Gurudev asked any random person, who had no idea how, to start curing people" | Surender ji, p.144 | From the guru as healer to the disciple as healer |

Hingori's gloss: "a person in complete alignment with their divine nature, as Gurudev was, has no necessity for external validation" (p.147); and on Santlal ji's account, "For someone as influential as Gurudev to encourage his followers to surpass him was not just ambitious but a testament to his humility" (p.146).

## The Same Pattern in Earlier Sources

- **Disciples can evolve beyond their guru** — *Guru Sutra* Ch.5: "The evolution of a disciple to a higher status is definitely a compliment to the guru who went beyond himself" (p.38); Gurudev to Hingori: "I will carry you on my shoulders so you can see from much higher" (p.38). See [[concepts/guru-disciple]], [[analogies/analogies]] (Carrying on Shoulders).
- **Launch pad, not crutch** — *Guru Sutra* Ch.9 (pp.93–94). See [[concepts/surrender]].
- **Switch sides** — *Guru Sutra* Ch.17 asks the disciple to imagine being the guru who is worshipped, and lists what the worshipped would *not* want: hymns, adulation, offerings. See [[concepts/guru-vandana]].
- **The guru as mirror** — *Guru Sutra* Ch.18: "A guru is nothing but a mirror image of the self at a realised level of the consciousness" (p.185). See [[synthesis/guru-sutra-arc]] (Phase 3).
- **Shiv in himself** — *Aatma Sutra* Ch.XIII: Kapill gazes at Shankar and sees himself; his guru tells him "there was no difference between Shiv and him." Ch.10's "You can become Shiv yourself" is the same teaching as an instruction rather than a vision. See [[stories/kapill-shiv-in-himself]].
- **"I have made you a guru, not a doctor"** — *The Guru of Gurus* (pp.97–98) frames the kaagaz saying as awakening the guru element in the disciple. See [[stories/gurudev-diary-guru-not-doctor]], [[concepts/guru-tattva]].
- **The compass** — "a Spiritual Compass guiding us to the true north" (*The Guru of Gurus*, p.109; repeated in Ch.10, p.148): a compass points away from itself.

## The Tension It Sits In

> [!warning] Total surrender and "refrain from idolising me" — in the same chapter
> Puran ji's one-line answer to what Gurudev expected from his disciples is "Total surrender" (p.146), and Hingori describes the disciple's mind as ceasing "to exist at an individual level" and existing "only in relation to the guru" (p.147). One page later Gurudev tells Renu ji not to idolise him. The chapter itself holds both. Hingori's framing reconciles them through identity rather than distance: "The disciple understands that they and the guru are manifestations of the same supreme consciousness but at different stages of their spiritual journey" (p.147). The same knot appears in *Guru Sutra* Ch.9 — surrender of tann, mann and dhann alongside "not to be dominated by anyone again" (see [[sources/guru-sutra]] Contradictions; [[stories/hingori-chira-self-esteem]]).

> [!warning] "Refrain from idolising me" vs. image-based devotion
> *The Guru of Gurus* (p.153): "Whenever you see an image of me, remind yourself that you have discovered god" (to Suresh Kohli ji). *Guru Sutra* Ch.14 teaches meditation on the guru and touching the guru's feet ([[concepts/guru-vandana]]). Ch.10's instruction to Renu ji is a stage-marker — "As you advance in your spiritual journey" — which suggests worship of the guru is a stage to be transcended rather than a practice to be refused. Hingori does not say this explicitly.

## Why It Matters

Read together, these sayings describe a guru whose success is measured by his becoming unnecessary: the disciple heals without herbs, surpasses the guru's attainments, goes beyond worship, and recognises Shiv in himself. It is the lived form of the Guru Sutra arc's final phase — the external guru serving until the guru within is met — witnessed in Gurudev's own words by his family and disciples.

> [!tip] "The mission and not the man" — *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.13
> "Gurudev embodied the essence of selfless service, ensuring that the focus remained on the mission and not the man" (p.187): his family did not know he was the 'Om Baba' of Kathog ([[stories/om-baba-sister-discovery]]); at Renuka "he instructed his disciples not to acknowledge him as their guru" (p.189; [[stories/renu-renuka-parshuram-test]]).
