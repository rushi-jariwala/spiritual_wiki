# The Tested Guru — Recognition Before Alliance

*Written 2026-09-28. Sources available: *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.1–13; *The Guru of Gurus* (complete); *Guru Sutra* (complete); *Aatma Sutra*; *Karma Sutra*.*

## The Pattern

[[synthesis/tests-in-disguise]] follows the tests Gurudev (and Augarh) set for disciples. *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.13 turns the direction round: in the camp years of Kathog and Renuka, **Gurudev is the one examined** — by a tantrik, by temple priests, and by the immortal who ruled Renuka. The chapter states the principle itself:

> Many spiritual luminaries often test each other's spirituality before recognising each other formally.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.13, p.189

| Examiner | Test | Gurudev's response | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thapa, tantrik at Kathog | A powerful spirit sent overnight to frighten "the illusionist" | Laughter — "You need to stop taking these silly tests!" | "replacing doubt with devotion" | [[stories/kathog-thapa-tantrik]] (*GoG* pp.73–75) |
| The priests of Jwalaji | The khadaus of a *siddh purush* — "only someone with immense spiritual strength could take even a single step in them" | "not only took one step but three" | "unequivocally proving his spiritual stature" | [[stories/jwalaji-khadaus-test]] (*WG* Ch.13, p.186) |
| Parshuram ji, at Renuka | Gurudev's "emotional ties to his family" — his seriously ill daughter brought to him | "If it is her destiny to die, we must accept it"; they leave before he wakes | "earning Parshuram ji's approval … the beginning of a lasting partnership" | [[stories/renu-renuka-parshuram-test]] (*WG* Ch.13, p.189) |

A fourth recognition is given without a test on Gurudev's part: Parshuram ji appearing at 27, then 18, then nine feet "as he recognised Gurudev's spiritual depth" (*WG* Ch.8, p.100; [[stories/gurudev-renuka-parshuram-alliance]]).

## Observations

1. **Each test matches the examiner's own measure.** The tantrik tests with a spirit; the priests with a relic of their temple; Parshuram ji with detachment from family. The sources do not generalise this; it is visible in the three accounts.
2. **Recognition opens the door to what follows.** After Thapa, devotion; after the khadaus, "spiritual recognition" (p.186); after Renu ji's visit, the alliance and the Bayri sthan, with Parshuram ji "recognised … as its chief patron" (p.188).
3. **Concealment until recognition.** "When Gurudev first arrived in Renuka, he instructed his disciples not to acknowledge him as their guru. He aimed to keep his identity hidden until he had established a significant spiritual alliance with Parshuram ji" (p.189). At Kathog his family did not know he was the 'Om Baba' ([[stories/om-baba-sister-discovery]]).
4. **The examined becomes the examiner.** The same Gurudev later set tests — and allowed Augarh to set them — for his disciples ([[synthesis/tests-in-disguise]]).

> [!tip] Vairagya under test
> The Renuka test is the one sourced instance of Gurudev's householder detachment being *examined* rather than described. It sits beside *The Guru of Gurus*' "Grihasth mein bhi vairagya hai" and Renu ji's own account of a father who "did not have time for us" ([[stories/renu-gurudev-confrontation-on-love]]). See [[concepts/vairagya]], [[concepts/grihasth-ashram]].

> [!question] Who set the Renuka test?
> Bittu ji reports that "Later, Gurudev revealed that Parshuram ji was testing him." Gurudev's displeasure on their arrival precedes the explanation; whether he knew in the moment that it was a test is not stated.
