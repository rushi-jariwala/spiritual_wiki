# The Hand That Lets Go — Missed Astral Invitations

*Date: 2026-09-28. Sources available: Karma Sutra, Aatma Sutra, Guru Sutra, The Guru of Gurus, Witnessing Greatness Ch.1–12.*

## The Pattern

*Witnessing Greatness* Ch.12 says it plainly: "Gurudev sometimes offered his disciples chances to accompany him on his astral voyages, though some unknowingly missed these opportunities" (p.171). Set beside the earlier books, the misses share one shape: **the guru offers, the point of contact is the body — his legs or feet — and the disciple's hold fails.**

| Who | Where | The offer | What failed | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hingori | Gurgaon (sthan room) | "Let me take you on a journey of the Brahmaand"; Puranji calls him in the dream state | "Limited by my physical orientation … I forced myself to physically wake up" | *Aatma Sutra* Ch.VI, p.67; *The Guru of Gurus*, p.246 — [[stories/hingori-puranji-missed-astral-tour]] |
| Sitaram Taki ji | Haridwar | "hold his legs and join him on an astral journey" | "Overwhelmed with fear … inadvertently released his grip on Gurudev's legs" | *The Guru of Gurus*, pp.98–99; *WG* Ch.12, p.173 — [[stories/sitaram-taki-haridwar-astral]] |
| Hingori | Gurgaon (paath) | "hold his feet, promising a celestial tour of the planets" | "as he started to convulse … my nerves betrayed me. I, too, let go" | *WG* Ch.12, p.173 |
| Rajpal ji, Sitaram ji, Suresh Sharma ji, RP Sharma ji | Hariana | "I have shown you all how to follow me" | took him for dead; massaged him with ghee — "My legs were in your hands. If only you had pressed that point, you would have come with me." | *WG* Ch.12, p.171 — [[stories/gurudev-sarson-saag-astral]] |

Three causes are named in the texts: **physical orientation** (Hingori waking up), **fear** (Sitaram ji, Hingori at the convulsion), and **not recognising what was happening** (the Hariana disciples reading an astral departure as death).

## Those Who Went

- **Surender Kaushal ji** (Chicago): "Gurudev made me touch the rings of the majestic Saturn!" (*WG* Ch.12, p.173); "made to touch all the planets, one by one" (*The Guru of Gurus*, p.246).
- **Santlal ji and Bakshi ji**: home and back through the grills — "Without his divine intervention, how would we have known the way to exit and re-enter our bodies?" (*WG* Ch.12, p.174). [[stories/santlal-astral-journey-home]]
- **Amichand ji**: went, but "found himself adrift and did not return. Gurudev had to travel astrally to bring him back" (p.174).
- **Guddan ji**: went without knowing it — "Who told you that you did not meet your mother? You did visit her. I sent you" (pp.175–176). [[stories/guddan-astral-visit-dying-mother]]
- **Ravi Trehan ji**: "the impression I received strongly suggested that I astrally travelled to Guru Lok with Gurudev" (p.174). [[stories/ravi-trehan-guru-lok-assembly]]
- **Hingori**: a bridge in Paris; a raft on an open sea (*The Guru of Gurus*, p.246).

In every case where the disciple went, the guru both **took** and **returned** them. "Guru bin gati nahi" (*Aatma Sutra* Ch.VI, p.68).

## The Legs as the Door — Both Ways

The same point of contact appears on the return journey. Only Mataji could bring Gurudev back in an emergency: she "would place both his toes together and pull his legs" (*The Guru of Gurus*, p.247). Ch.12 adds that "Sometimes, even a firm nudge would not stir him … re-entering the physical realm from his travels could take time … so patience was required" (Mataji, pp.170–171).

> [!tip] Holding the legs to go; pulling the legs to bring him back; "If only you had pressed that point." Across three sources, the guru's legs or feet are where the disciple joins or recalls the astral journey. The texts do not explain why; the pattern is noted here, not interpreted.

## The Caution Beside the Invitation

> He highlighted the risks associated with astral travel, cautioning against leaving the physical body unprotected. He explained that it could become vulnerable to takeover by powerful and malevolent entities.
> — *Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.12, p.175

The Santlal ji journey was done with "locked doors" and "a dhoop" (p.173); Amichand ji needed fetching. The invitations were real, but so was the risk — which may be why every successful journey in the record was made *with* the guru, not alone.

> [!question] Is the Gurgaon "celestial tour of the planets" (*WG* Ch.12) the same night as the Brahmaand offer (*The Guru of Gurus*, p.246; *Aatma Sutra*, p.67)? In the earlier telling Hingori was asleep and Puranji held the feet; in Ch.12 Hingori himself holds the feet and lets go at the convulsion. They read as two separate occasions.
