# Tests in Disguise — Augarh and the Guru's Unannounced Examinations

*Written 2026-09-28. Sources available: *Witnessing Greatness* Ch.1–8; *Guru Sutra* (complete); *The Guru of Gurus* (complete); *Aatma Sutra*; *Karma Sutra*.*

## The Pattern

Across three books, Gurudev's tests share one structure: **the examiner arrives unannounced, in an ordinary or unattractive form, and the disciple learns only afterwards that it was a test.** The result is then reported back to Gurudev — "Augarh tells me everything" (*Witnessing Greatness*, Ch.8, p.112).

## Observations

1. **Food is the recurring medium.** Three of the Augarh encounters turn on food offered or refused (wafers refused, chole kulche accepted; biscuits taken with a smirk; vada pav thrown away). This sits beside [[synthesis/feeding-others-before-self]]: Gurudev's own discipline was to feed the other first; Augarh examines whether the disciple does so — and on what terms.
2. **The examiner is loved, not feared alone.** Hingori: "most of the mahaguru's disciples didn't even come close to passing! But how can one not love him? His divine naughtiness is off the charts!" (*WG* Ch.8, p.113). FC Sharma ji describes "a mix of reverence and apprehension" (p.112).
3. **Protector and examiner are one.** The same Augarh "protected the sthan when Daddy was away" (Renu ji), guided Hingori's tea ([[stories/hingori-augarh-tea]]), and eased Geeta ji's asthma. The test and the grace come from the same collaborator — see [[concepts/spiritual-alliances]].
4. **The test outlives the guru.** "Augarh continues to test the third and fourth generation of the mahaguru's disciples" (p.113) — Humsa ji's encounters were interpreted by her guru, not by Gurudev.

> [!question] What exactly is tested?
> The sources name the pass/fail only for Bagga ji (fail) and Humsa ji's first encounter (pass). What criterion Augarh applies is not stated. Do not infer beyond this.
